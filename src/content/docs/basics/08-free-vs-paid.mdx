<Summary>
- **Free is enough at first.** All three services have a free plan. Try it for about a month, and it's not too late to think about paying once you feel like "I keep hitting the limit."
- Personal paid plans are generally **around $20 a month**: Claude Pro is $20/month, ChatGPT Plus is $20/month, and Google AI Pro is $19.99/month. (US prices, as of 2026-10-10)
- There are cheaper tiers too: ChatGPT Go is $8/month and Google AI Plus is $4.99/month.
- The difference with paid plans is mainly **how much you can use (limits) and advanced features**. Be sure to check the official pricing page before paying.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Tell apart what you can and can't do on a free plan.
- Decide for yourself, "Should I switch to paid now?"
- Compare the three services' personal plans at a glance.
- Know what to check before paying.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Free plan** | Basic use that doesn't cost anything. There's a limit on how much you can use. |
| **Usage limit** | The cap on how much you can use within a certain time. When you use it all, you wait until it resets with time. |
| **Subscription** | A way of paying that's charged automatically every month (or year). It keeps charging until you cancel. |
| **Personal plan / team or enterprise plan** | A plan for using alone vs. a plan for several people in a company or organization. This tutorial focuses on personal plans. |

### What you can do on the free plans (as of 2026-10-10)

- **Claude Free**: Chat on web, desktop, and mobile; web search; file creation and code execution; memory; artifacts; up to 5 projects. Claude Code is not included. ([C2](/claude/c02-plans-limits/))
- **Gemini free**: $0/month with a Google account. Includes Deep Research, Canvas, and Gems (as described for the US).
- **ChatGPT Free**: $0/month. However, on Free and Go the models are described as different from those on Plus and above. ([chat tutorial 5](/chat/05-compare-assistants/))

The **specific usage numbers** for free plans (such as how many messages) aren't published in the official documentation, including for Claude. The most accurate way is to use it yourself and see whether you hit the limit.

<Steps>

1. **Try free first**

   For about a week, have it do the things you usually want to do (asking questions instead of searching, polishing writing, summarizing). Making a note of what gets blocked will make the decision easier.

2. **Count how often you hit the limit**

   How many times a week did you see a message like "Please try again later"? If it's occasional, you can just wait. If it's almost every day, it's time to consider paying.

3. **Check whether it's a moment that calls for paid**

   If any of the following applies, paid can help.

   - You hit the limit often, and your work gets interrupted.
   - You need a better model or analysis of long material.
   - You want to use a coding tool (for example, Claude Code). Claude Code requires a paid plan.
   - You use it a lot every day for work, and the time it saves is worth the cost.

   On the other hand, if you only ask now and then, free is enough.

4. **Compare personal plans**

   Look at the quick reference table below. It's fairer to compare plans in the same price range.

5. **Try paying for one service for a month**

   Don't subscribe to several services at the same time. Use just one for a month, and if you're satisfied, keep going. Annual billing is cheaper than monthly, but a large sum goes out at once, so choose it after you've used the service enough.

6. **Do a final check before paying**

   - How much the price is in **your local currency**, and whether tax is added
   - Whether it **auto-renews** and how to cancel
   - If you pay through an app store, the price may differ from the web (the App Store price and the web price sometimes differ)

</Steps>

### What changes when you pay

| Service | Main differences on paid plans (per official info) |
| --- | --- |
| Claude Pro | More usage than Free (on 5-hour and weekly limits), Claude Code, projects (Free is up to 5; Pro lists no limit on the number), more models |
| Claude Max / Ultra | Max is 5x Pro's usage; Ultra is 10x Pro's weekly usage (no 5-hour limit for verified accounts). Both include Fable, monthly API credits ($100/$200), and early access to new features (as of 2026-10-31) |

### When you use up your limit (Claude)

- Paid plans have a **5-hour session limit** and a **weekly limit**. You can see the next reset time under Settings > Usage.
- If you want to keep using it after the limit, you can turn on **extra usage (usage credits)**. It's an optional feature billed separately for what you use, so be sure to set a monthly spending cap.
- You can read more in [C2](/claude/c02-plans-limits/).

## Common sticking points

### The prices on blogs and in the app don't match

- **Cause**: They differ because of region, annual vs. monthly billing, app store fees, and when plans were revamped.
- **Fix**: Trust the current figures on the official pricing page. The amount on the screen right before you pay is final.

### I paid and still hit the limit

- **Cause**: Paid plans have limits too. Long conversations and hard tasks use it up faster.
- **Fix**: Start new topics in new conversations, and use lighter models for simple tasks. If that's still not enough, consider a higher plan or extra usage.

### I want to cancel my subscription

- **Cause**: Subscriptions auto-renew.
- **Fix**: You have to cancel where you signed up (the web or an app store). If you paid through an app store, cancel in that store's subscription management. Check each service's official help for the exact menu.

### I was using the free plan and suddenly it says I can't use it

- **Cause**: You may have hit the limit, or the feature may be paid only.
- **Fix**: Read the message, wait for it to reset with time, or try another service's free plan.

## Practical tips

- **Keep free accounts at two or three services.** If one gets blocked, you can use another.
- **Look at the prices again every quarter.** Plan names and prices change often.
- **Don't use a personal account for company work.** Team and enterprise plans may have different training policies. See [tutorial 5](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).
- **If you're curious about everyday use,** gauge what you'd need with [tutorial 6](/basics/06-everyday-uses/).
