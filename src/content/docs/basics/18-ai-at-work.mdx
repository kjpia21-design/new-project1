<Summary>
- **Ask first.** Your company may allow only certain AI tools, or none. Its policy comes before anything on this page.
- **Personal and work plans handle data differently.** On personal accounts, Claude and Gemini describe settings that can let your chats be used to improve their services. The official pages I read for work plans say business data is not used for training by default. Details depend on the plan and your admin's settings.
- **Don't paste what you shouldn't share**: customer data, passwords, financial or health records, unreleased plans, confidential documents.
- **You are responsible for the result.** Check facts, numbers, and tone before anything goes out under your name.
- This is general information, **not legal or compliance advice**.

## Concepts to know first

This builds on [tutorial 5](/basics/05-privacy-safety/) (what not to paste), [tutorial 12](/basics/12-privacy-settings/) (training settings), and [tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/) (checking answers).

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Personal plan** | An account you signed up for yourself (for example Claude Free, Pro, or Max). |
| **Work plan** | An account managed by your organization (for example Claude Team or Enterprise, or a Google Workspace account). An admin controls settings. |
| **Model training / improvement** | Using chats to make the AI better. Whether this applies depends on the plan and settings. |
| **Shadow AI** | Using an AI tool for work that your company did not approve. A common source of policy trouble. |

What the official pages say (as of 2026-11-02; plans, regions and admin settings can change this):

| | Personal account | Work account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Claude** | Anthropic says chats may be used to help improve Claude when you allow it. You can switch this off in Settings > Privacy. Incognito chats are not used, and flagged chats may still be used for safety work. | Pricing page: "No model training on your content by default" for Team and Enterprise. The Primary Owner manages the account and data, and can export user data, which may include conversations. |
| **Gemini** | With Keep Activity on, some chats are reviewed by humans, and Google asks you not to enter confidential information. Reviewed chats can be kept up to three years, even if you delete your activity. | The Workspace privacy page says chats and uploaded files are not human reviewed or used to train models without permission. Prompt and response retention is set by admins. |
| **ChatGPT** | Not confirmed | OpenAI docs I could open say Business, Enterprise and Edu workspace data is not used to train models by default. |

## Step-by-step guide

<Steps>

1. **Check your company's rules**

   Look for an AI or acceptable-use policy, ask your manager or IT, and check whether your company provides an approved account. If nothing exists, ask rather than guess. Silence is not permission.

   <PromptBox>
   Draft a short message to my IT team asking: which AI tools are approved for work, which types of data I may enter, and whether AI-assisted work needs to be disclosed to customers.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Use the approved account, not a personal one**

   If your company gives you a work plan, use it for work. A personal account is governed by different settings than the work plan your admin manages. On Team and Enterprise plans, the official page says the Primary Owner manages the account and data. Do not assume that what you type in a work account is private from your organization.

3. **Sort information before you paste**

   | Group | Examples | What to do |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Usually fine | Public information, your own draft text with no names, general questions | Paste if policy allows |
   | Remove details first | Meeting notes with names, internal numbers, contract wording | Replace names and figures with placeholders like [CLIENT] |
   | Never paste | Passwords and login details, card or bank numbers, health records, customer personal data, confidential documents | Do not put in any AI tool unless your company explicitly approves that tool for it |

   Anthropic's consumer page itself recommends being thoughtful about financial information, health records, passwords, and confidential business or personal documents.

4. **Anonymize, then ask**

   <PromptBox>
   Below is meeting text where I replaced real names with [PERSON A], [PERSON B] and the client with [CLIENT]. Write a summary with decisions and action items. Do not guess missing names.
   </PromptBox>

   Remember that placeholders only help if the rest of the text does not identify the person or company on its own.

5. **Review before you send**

   You are accountable for what goes out. Check names, dates, numbers, quotes, and anything legal or financial against the original source (see [tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/)). Read the tone as your reader would.

   <PromptBox>
   List every number, date, name and factual claim in the text above as a table, so I can check each one against the source. Mark any you cannot support from the text I provided.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Keep a trace and be open about it**

   Save the source documents and your final version so you can explain how you got there. If your company or client requires disclosure of AI use, follow it. For copyright and ownership questions, see [tutorial 9](/basics/09-ai-copyright-ethics/).

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
The three groups in step 3 are this site's practical rule of thumb, not an official classification. Your company's data classification, if it has one, overrides it.
</Aside>

## Quick reference

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| No policy exists | Ask IT or your manager before using AI for work |
| Company provides an AI account | Use it, not a personal account |
| Document has customer names | Replace with placeholders, or don't use AI |
| Only a personal account is available | Ask first; if allowed, check its privacy settings (tutorial 12) |
| Output will be sent externally | Verify every fact and number yourself |

## Common sticking points

### My company has no AI policy

- **Cause**: Policies often lag behind tools.
- **Fix**: Ask in writing and keep the answer. Until you hear back, avoid confidential or personal data.

### I already pasted something I shouldn't have

- **Cause**: It happens, often under deadline pressure.
- **Fix**: Tell your IT or security team promptly. Deleting the chat may not be enough, because I could not confirm in the pages I read that deletion removes every copy. For Gemini, the privacy page says reviewed chats can be kept up to three years.

### The tool says business data isn't used for training. Is anything I paste safe?

- **Cause**: "Not used for training" is one statement. It does not say who inside your company can see the content, or how long it is kept.
- **Fix**: Read your admin's settings and agreement. On Claude Team and Enterprise, the Primary Owner can request user data exports.

### The AI sounded confident but was wrong

- **Cause**: AI can state errors fluently.
- **Fix**: Verify against the source. See [tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

## Practical tips

- Anonymize by default. It is a habit that works under any policy.
- When in doubt about a data type, ask. A short question costs less than an incident.
- Keep a short list of prompts that worked, without any sensitive content in them.
- Pages and settings change. This page is accurate as of 2026-11-02, and it is not legal advice.
