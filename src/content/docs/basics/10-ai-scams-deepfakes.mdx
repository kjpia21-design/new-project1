- Even as scam methods change, **the backbone of how to stop them stays the same.** If a contact asks for money, verification codes, a link click, or an app install, hang up first and check directly.
- Korea's Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) advises that if you get a suspicious call or text, you should **hang up first and call the official consultation line, 1394** (for users in Korea; September 2026 press release).
- Even if it's a family member's or friend's voice or message, **check directly using a contact you already knew** whenever money comes up. The FSS's fraud types include posing as a kidnapped child and impersonating an acquaintance on a messenger.
- If you've sent money or given out information, **ask your financial institution's call center to freeze payments right away.** You can also be connected from 112 and 1332 (Korean emergency and FSS lines).
- The reporting numbers in the "Quick reference" below list **only what was confirmed on official pages** (as of 2026-10-15). They're for users in Korea.

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Build the habit of **stopping to check** when money comes up, even if the voice or face seems real.
- **Ask** KISA's check service whether a link in a suspicious text is safe to tap.
- Know **what to do, and in what order,** if you've been victimized.
- Know **where to get help** if you see a fake image or video using your face.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Voice phishing** | Fraud that tricks people by phone or text into handing over money or personal information. The official term is telecommunications financial fraud. |
| **Smishing** | A method of getting you to tap a link in a text message so a malicious app is installed or your information is taken. |
| **AI voice scam** | Voice phishing that uses an AI-made voice. The FSS issued a press release on this topic in January 2026. However, we couldn't open the body of the release, so we **confirmed only the title**. We don't state specific methods or figures. |
| **Deepfake** | Images, video, or audio in which AI makes a person's face, voice, or actions look real. |
| **Payment freeze** | A measure in which you ask a financial institution to block further money from leaving an account used in a scam. |
| **Malicious app** | An app that gets installed secretly and lets a scammer control your phone as they like. |

<Aside type="note" title="What this tutorial couldn't confirm">

<Steps>

1. **If it asks for money, verification codes, links, or app installs, hang up first**

   Phishing Safe SOS's reporting page says, "Public agencies and financial institutions never ask for personal information or money transfers under any circumstances." The FSS press release advises that if you get a suspicious call or text, you should hang up first and call the official consultation line **1394**. The Phishing Safe SOS reporting page introduces 1394 as the joint response team's phone number, and explains it's the number to use when your phone number or business number is being misused in phishing (as of 2026-10-26).

   It also says that when you need to verify something, check the facts directly through the agency's **official website, app, or main phone number**, not a number the other person gave you. Don't panic even if the other person knows your name or phone number. The FSS guidance is to suspect fraud first if you're asked to open a link, install an app, or transfer money.

   <Aside type="caution" title="If a malicious app is installed on your phone">
   According to a footnote in the FSS press release, if your phone is infected by a malicious app, the scammer can access it freely, so even if you call the police (112) or the FSS (1332), **you may be connected to the scammer.** If you suspect this, contact them from someone else's phone or a landline. This last sentence is this tutorial's general recommendation based on the guidance above.
   </Aside>

2. **Verify urgent requests from family or friends one more time, even if the voice matches**

   The FSS's "main types of fraud" include the following. The first two are old methods that existed long before AI.

   - **Posing as a kidnapped or injured child**: The caller spoofs the child's number and tells the parent that the child has been in an accident or kidnapped, and demands money.
   - **Impersonating an acquaintance on a messenger**: They hack someone's messenger account and ask the family and friends registered on it for emergency funds.

   AI voices can be seen as one more tool added to these methods. So don't judge by the feeling that "the voice sounds real." **Hang up, then call back directly using the number you originally knew** to verify. This is this tutorial's general recommendation, applying the official guidance above (verify directly, hang up first) to family and friends.

3. **Don't tap links, and don't install apps**

   The FSS advises to **never click** an internet address (URL) of unclear origin in a text about things like a parcel address error, undeliverable delivery, or an unpaid fine. Tapping the link can install a malicious app that steals personal and financial information.

   - On Android, you can **block installing apps from unknown sources** in settings. The example path in FSS materials is Settings > Security and privacy > Automatic security risk blocking (Settings > Security and privacy). Menu names can differ by Android version.
   - If you've already tapped it or installed an app, it advises **turning off your phone or switching to airplane mode**, then getting measures such as malicious app removal and a phone factory reset at a nearby mobile carrier store.

4. **Ask KISA's smishing check service about suspicious texts**

   KISA Boho Nara's smishing check service lets you ask directly whether a text you received is smishing. Any citizen can use it. The answer comes back as one of three: **normal, caution, or malicious**. A message reported for the first time is marked "caution" at first, and if you check again after 10 minutes, it's judged normal or malicious and you're told which.

   You use it by adding the Boho Nara channel on KakaoTalk (confirmed 2026-10-19). The steps are described as: copy the smishing text, open the smishing check service, paste, and tap "Check smishing report result." Don't tap the link in the text; **copy only the text**.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="The Boho Nara smishing check service guide screen" officialUrl="https://boho.or.kr/kr/subPage.do?menuNo=205116" officialLabel="See the service guide at KISA Boho Nara" />

   Boho Nara states that KISA takes consultations on spam, hacking, and personal information infringement at **118**.

5. **A defense you can set up ahead of time: the Financial Transaction Safety Block service**

   The FSS press release introduces the **Financial Transaction Safety Block service** as something you can use when personal information leaks keep occurring. If you sign up, you can prevent loans, new accounts, or open banking from being used without your knowledge.

   - Loan safety block: credit loans, card loans, credit card issuance, and so on (in effect since 2024-08-23)
   - Non-face-to-face account opening safety block (in effect since 2025-03-12)
   - Open banking safety block (in effect since 2025-11-14)
   - How to apply: visit a branch of the financial institution you use, or use the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute's Account Info app or your bank's mobile banking

   Menu names on the application screens change often, so follow the app's instructions right when you apply.

6. **If you've sent money or given out information, act in this order**

   This summarizes the order on the FSS "What to do if you're a voice phishing victim" page. The more urgent it is, do step 1 first.

   After the immediate measures, it says to take further steps as the financial institution and police direct, such as revoking and reissuing your certificates and reporting a lost ID. At Phishing Safe SOS, under the National Police Agency, you can file a **report** if you prepare screenshots of call logs and recordings, and screenshots and link information of texts. It says you can report even without materials, but verification may take longer.

7. **How to look at suspicious photos, videos, and audio**

   Deepfakes can be hard to spot with your eyes and ears alone, so it's best to use several methods together. The methods introduced in Gemini app help are:

   - **Ask for verification in the Gemini app**: You can upload an image, video, or audio and ask something like "Was this image generated or edited with Google AI?" It checks for the SynthID watermark and content credentials. Limits are one file at a time, 100 MB or less, video under 90 seconds, and audio under 1 hour.
   - **Be sure to remember the limits**: This check recognizes **only content made with Google AI**. Even if no watermark shows up, it may have been made with another AI. It is not "proof that it's not fake."
   - **Look with your eyes**: See whether text on screen is garbled, whether hands, teeth, or the background look off, and whether shadows and lighting match.
   - **Search by image**: Use a tool such as Google Images to find the original or similar images.
   - **Metadata**: If you have the original file rather than a screenshot, you can see embedded information such as the capturing device and date.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="The Gemini app's guide to checking AI-generated content" officialUrl="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16722517?hl=ko" />

   YouTube says creators who upload AI content that's hard to tell from reality must disclose this, and when they do, viewers see a label. The absence of a label doesn't mean it's real, because the label relies on the uploader's disclosure. This last sentence is this tutorial's interpretation after reading the help page.

8. **If you see a fake using your face, don't struggle alone**

   There are places to get help for harm such as sexual images made with deepfakes.

   - **Central Digital Sexual Crime Victim Support Center** (for users in Korea): It describes helping with counseling, deletion support, distribution monitoring, and referrals to investigation, legal, and medical help for harms such as illegal filming, non-consensual distribution, threats to distribute, and deepfakes. The center's phone is 02-735-8994 (weekdays 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., weekends and holidays 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.), and the Women's Emergency Hotline 1366 is shown as 24 hours a day, 365 days a year (confirmed 2026-10-15). The center's website also has a route for third-party reports.
   - **Requesting removal from Google Search results**: You can request removal of fake sexual content that is identifiable as you, is fake, and was spread without your consent. However, it's only removed from search results; you need to make a separate request to the site where the content was posted.
   - **If your face was made with Google AI**: Images and videos where Google's generative AI created or altered your likeness without consent can be reported through a report form. There are conditions such as a personal Google account, age 18 or older, and a region where Google's selfie verification service is available, and only images and videos are supported, not audio (confirmed 2026-10-19).

   Before deleting the harmful content, screenshot it along with its address (URL), which helps when you report it. This last sentence is this tutorial's general recommendation based on those agencies' reporting guidance (which asks for URLs).

9. **How to ask AI about a suspicious text**

   Asking AI "Does this text look like a scam?" can be a useful reference. But don't take the AI's answer as the final judgment. Do the final check through the official channels above (1394, the smishing check service, the agency's main phone number). Mask personal information before pasting, as in [tutorial 5](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).

## Common sticking points

### The voice really sounded like my family. Should I still be suspicious?

- **Cause**: When people hear a familiar voice, they become less suspicious. The FSS's main fraud types also include a method that spoofs the child's number, so even a matching number isn't reassuring.
- **Fix**: If money, verification codes, or app installs come up, hang up first and call back directly using the number you knew, or check with another family member. This is a general recommendation applying the official guidance to family situations.

### I called 112 and a strange person answered

- **Cause**: According to the FSS press release, on a phone with a malicious app installed, even calls to 112 or 1332 can be routed to the scammer.
- **Fix**: If you suspect a malicious app, don't report from that phone. Use someone else's phone or a landline. And turn off the phone or put it in airplane mode.

### I already tapped the link, but nothing happened

- **Cause**: Malicious apps may not show any signs.
- **Fix**: Even if you didn't follow the screen telling you to install an app, if you're worried, change your passwords with reference to [tutorial 5](/basics/05-privacy-safety/), and consult KISA 118 or the FSS 1332. If you installed an app, follow the steps in number 6 above.

### Gemini's check said "no watermark found"

- **Cause**: This check only recognizes content made with Google AI. The help page also says it may have been made with another AI.
- **Fix**: Don't use it as proof that it's "not AI." Also check the source, search by image, and look at the original file. We covered fact-checking methods in [tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

### Can I get my money back if I report it?

- **Cause**: Whether stolen funds can be refunded differs case by case.
- **Fix**: This tutorial doesn't promise whether or when a refund is possible. FSS guidance says to apply for damage relief at a branch with supporting documents after requesting a payment freeze, so going through that procedure quickly is the first step. Ask your financial institution and the FSS (1332) for detailed conditions.

## Practical tips

- **Save 1394, 112, 1332, and 118 in your phone ahead of time** (for users in Korea). In an emergency, you won't have time to search.
- For parents or older relatives, rather than reading this tutorial to them, just agree on one thing: "**If money comes up, hang up first and call me back.**"
- We couldn't confirm family code words in official guidance, but agreeing on an easy way to verify with your family isn't harmful in itself. Just keep it separate in your mind so you don't treat it as an official recommendation.
- When you post photos and videos to social media, look once more at whether faces appear large and how far the visibility extends.
- Methods in this area change quickly. Opening Phishing Safe SOS's "phishing scenarios" or FSS press releases from time to time shows the latest cases.
