<Summary>
- AI **can't read your mind.** It only knows what you write.
- There are four things to remember: **purpose, situation, the shape you want, and the reason.**
- The first answer is a draft. **Refine it with follow-up questions.**
- Here's one yardstick: could a brand-new coworker who knows nothing about the task get the work done from what you wrote?
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Turn a vague question into a specific one.
- Know what to fix when you don't like an answer.
- Keep the conversation going so answers fit your situation better and better.

## Concepts to know first

A **prompt** is what you type to the AI, meaning your question or request. It isn't a difficult technique. It's the same as writing a message asking a person to do something.

Claude's official guide says to treat the AI like "a brilliant new employee who doesn't yet know how your company does things." It also gives this test: **if you showed your request to a coworker with almost no context and they'd be confused, the AI will be confused too.**

For example, if you just tell the new hire at the next desk "write a report," they'd ask who it's for and how many pages. Instead of asking back, AI often guesses and writes something. So it's faster to tell it up front.

<Aside type="note" title="It's the same across tools">
This tutorial is based on Claude's official guide and getting-started help page. These are the basics of asking for work in writing, so they mostly apply to ChatGPT and Gemini too, but we didn't cross-check those two services' official guides.
</Aside>

## Step-by-step guide

<Checklist items={[
  'I picked a vague question and added a purpose and situation',
  'I wrote down the shape of the answer I want (length, list, and so on)',
  'I added a one-sentence reason',

]} />

<Steps>

1. **Start simple**

   You don't have to write it perfectly from the start. The official getting-started guide also says to start with a simple question and refine it gradually.

   <PromptBox>
   Recommend some indoor activities for this weekend.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Give the purpose and situation**

   Write who it's for, why, and under what conditions, and the answer fits you much better.

   <PromptBox>
   Recommend indoor activities for this weekend with a 7-year-old and a 4-year-old. It's raining so we can't go out, and I'd like activities we can do with things we already have at home.
   </PromptBox>

3. **Say the shape you want**

   Write how many items you want and in what form. The official guide also says to be specific about output format and conditions.

   <PromptBox>
   Using those conditions, recommend 5 activities. For each one, give the name, supplies, and time needed in one line.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Add the reason**

   Rather than only saying "don't do X," tell it **why**, and the AI understands your intent. Also, "do it this way" works better than "don't do that."

   <PromptBox>
   Explain it in plain, easy words without hard vocabulary. I'm going to read it aloud to my parents as is.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Fix it with follow-up questions**

   If the first answer falls short, don't start over. Just keep talking. Ask for one change at a time.

   <PromptBox>
   I like activity number 3. Give me more detail, step by step. Only use supplies we already have at home.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Be honest when you don't know**

   If you're not sure about your own situation, you can have the AI ask you questions.

   <PromptBox>
   What should I tell you so you can help me more accurately? Ask me just 3 questions.
   </PromptBox>

</Steps>

## Quick reference

| If the answer is... | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| Too generic and ordinary | Give more about your situation and purpose |
| Too long or too short | State the length as a number (for example, 5 lines, 3 items) |
| The tone is off | Tell it who will read it |
| Heading in the wrong direction | Point out the wrong part and ask again in the same conversation |
| Seems wrong | See [tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/) |

## Common sticking points

### I wrote a lot but the answer is still poor

- **Cause**: The problem isn't length but which information is missing. Often there's lots of background but no description of the shape you want.
- **Fix**: Check whether any of the four (purpose, situation, shape, reason) is missing.

### I don't know what to ask

- **Cause**: What you want may not be clear to you yet.
- **Fix**: Have the AI ask you questions, as in step 6. It's fine to start with just what you know.

### I kept going and it seems to have forgotten what came before

- **Cause**: In long conversations, earlier parts can be reflected less.
- **Fix**: Restate important conditions briefly once more. Opening a new conversation and starting with a clean summary also works.

### Same question, different answer every time

- **Cause**: AI can word things differently even for the same question. That's not unusual.
- **Fix**: Writing the format and conditions specifically makes the answers vary less.

## Practical tips

- One sentence is fine at first. It's faster to look at the answer and fix it.
- Save questions that worked in a notes app and change just a part of them next time.
- Make it a habit not to enter personal information such as names, contact details, and account numbers. Tutorial 5 covers this in detail.
