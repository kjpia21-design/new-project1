- AI **sometimes states wrong information with confidence.** This is called a hallucination.
- **Don't treat any single AI as your only source.** Anthropic's help page says the same.
- **Always check for yourself** numbers, dates, names, quotes, and anything about health, money, or law.

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Understand why AI can be wrong and what to be especially careful about.
- Reduce the chance of wrong answers by changing how you phrase questions.
- Learn a 3-minute routine for checking an important answer.

## Concepts to know first

According to Anthropic's help page, Claude can produce wrong or misleading answers, which it calls "hallucinating." This comes from the limits of today's generative AI, and the reasons it gives include:

- The material it learned from **may be out of date**, so it can get confused about recent events.
- It may produce **quotes that look authoritative** but have no real basis.

The key is the tone. Wrong answers can come out in a confident tone too, so you can't tell right from wrong by tone alone.

<Aside type="caution" title="What needs extra checking">
Anthropic's help page advises carefully verifying any advice where being wrong would cause problems. That includes health, money, law, contracts, citations in exams or papers, and numbers and dates.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Tell it that saying "I don't know" is okay**

   Anthropic's official docs explain that simply giving the AI permission to admit uncertainty can greatly reduce wrong information.

   <PromptBox>
   If any part isn't certain, don't make something up. Say "I'm not sure." And tell me which parts you're not sure about.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Give it material and have it answer only from that**

   If you paste in an article or document and ask about it, make it use only what's in the material. The official docs also describe having the AI use only the material you provide.

   <PromptBox>
   Summarize using only the text below. Don't include anything that isn't in the text, and if something is missing, write "Not in the text."

   [Paste text here]
   </PromptBox>

3. **Ask for the evidence with the answer**

   The official docs describe having the AI attach a supporting sentence to each claim, and drop any claim it can't find support for.

   <PromptBox>
   For each claim in your answer, include the sentence or source that supports it. If you can't find support for a claim, leave it out.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Open the sources and check them yourself**

   If an answer is based on web search results, Anthropic's help page says to check the original sites, because a summary can leave out context. See whether the link really opens and whether the content matches what the AI said.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Clicking a source in an answer to check the original" officialUrl="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8525154-claude-is-providing-incorrect-or-misleading-responses-what-s-going-on" officialLabel="See it in the Anthropic Help Center" />

5. **Ask again and compare**

   The official docs suggest asking the same question several times and comparing whether the answers differ. If the answers differ every time, it may be made up. You can also have it verify with a follow-up question.

   <PromptBox>
   Check your last answer yourself and tell me if any part might be wrong.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Cross-check against another trustworthy source**

   For important content, check once more with an accountable source such as a public agency, an official site, or an expert. The AI's own self-check isn't perfect either.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
Anthropic's official docs also say these methods greatly reduce wrong information but **can't eliminate it entirely**. For important decisions, always check for yourself.
</Aside>

## Quick reference

| Content | How much to check |
| --- | --- |
| Ideas, drafts, polishing tone | Reading it over before you use it is enough |
| Numbers, dates, names of people and companies | Verify against the original |
| Quotes, books, papers, legal provisions | Look up whether they actually exist |
| Health, money, law, contracts | Always confirm with an expert or official agency |
| Recent news | Check the latest sources yourself |

## Common sticking points

### I opened a link the AI gave me and the page doesn't exist

- **Cause**: The AI may have made up a source that looks like evidence but doesn't exist.
- **Fix**: Instead of the link, search directly by title or organization name. If you can't find it, don't trust that content.

### When I pushed back, the AI changed its answer right away

- **Cause**: Even when it was right, it may apologize and change its answer when questioned. A changed answer alone doesn't tell you which side is correct.
- **Fix**: Either way, verify with official sources.

### Checking takes too long

- **Cause**: You're trying to check every sentence.
- **Fix**: Pick out only the parts where being wrong would be a problem (numbers, names, dates, conclusions) and check those.

### I asked about something recent and got a strange answer

- **Cause**: The material it learned from may be out of date.
- **Fix**: See whether there's a web search feature you can use, and read news on the original site.

## Practical tips

- Use AI as **a tool that's strong at drafts and ideas**, and do the final check yourself.
- If you spot a wrong answer, you can report it with the feedback (thumbs-down) button in the tool. Anthropic's help page says so.
- Try asking the same question to another AI using the account from tutorial 2, and you'll see the differences.
