<Summary>
- An AI chatbot is **a helper that answers in writing when you ask in writing**. It's similar to a search box, but you can make requests conversationally and ask it to revise its answers.
- **A free account is enough** at first. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini can all be started for free. (As of 2026-10-10; limits and features keep changing.)
- It can also be wrong. **Always double-check** anything important, and **don't enter sensitive information** such as national ID numbers.
- Follow this guide and you can ask your first question and do your first rewrite within 30 minutes.
</Summary>

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **AI chatbot** | A program that answers in sentences when you talk to it in sentences, like a person. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are well-known examples. |
| **Question (prompt)** | The request you send to the AI. It isn't fancy language, just a sentence asking for something. |
| **Conversation (chat)** | A single thread of questions and answers. It remembers what came before, so you can keep building on it. |
| **Free / paid plan** | Free plans limit how much you can use per day or per few hours. Paid plans have more generous limits. |

### What AI is good at, and what to watch out for

| Good at | Watch out for |
| --- | --- |
| Polishing writing, drafting emails and text messages | It can state facts that are **plausible but wrong** |
| Explaining hard content in simple terms | Recent news and numbers may be wrong or out of date |
| Summarizing long text, brainstorming ideas | Don't enter personal information or company secrets |
| Translating and changing tone | For medical, legal, and money matters, check with experts and official sources |

<Aside type="caution" title="AI can be wrong">
AI can give wrong answers with total confidence. When you're just starting out, use it with the mindset of "I get a draft, and I fix it." You'll find how to check answers in [Can you trust AI answers?](/basics/04-fact-checking/), and how to use AI safely in [Using AI while protecting personal and company information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Free limits aren't set as exact numbers">
The services don't officially publish exactly how many times a day you can use a free account (for Claude, as of 2026-10-13; Anthropic's help pages only say that limits vary with conversation length, features, and model). When you hit a limit, you'll see a message, and you can use it again after some time passes. Prices and features change often, so check each official page.
</Aside>

## Step-by-step guide

<Steps>

1. **Pick just one service (3 min)**

   One is enough at first. All three work in Korean as well as English, and all can be started for free.

   - If you already use a Google account, Gemini is the easiest.
   - Any of them is fine, so pick the one you like. You can switch later.
   - You can see the differences between services in [Comparing Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini](/chat/05-compare-assistants/).

2. **Sign up and log in (10 min)**

   The next guide, [From sign-up to your first question](/basics/02-sign-up-first-question/), walks through sign-up for each service step by step. For now, just get as far as logging in.

3. **Send your first question (5 min)**

   Type in the input box at the bottom of the screen and press the send button (or Enter). A low-pressure question is a good place to start.

   <PromptBox>
   I'm trying AI for the first time. Suggest 3 simple side dishes I can make in 15 minutes tonight. I have eggs, tofu, and kimchi.
   </PromptBox>

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder
     caption="Type your question in the input box at the bottom and send it"
     officialUrl="https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/use-chatgpt"
     officialLabel="See how to start a conversation in the OpenAI docs"
   />

4. **Ask for a revision (5 min)**

   It's fine if you don't like the first answer. You don't need a perfect question from the start. Just ask for changes right away.

   <PromptBox>
   Explain the second side dish in more detail, with numbered steps. Write it so someone who has barely cooked before can follow along.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Try it on your own task (5 min)**

   This time, ask for help with something you really need. For example:

   <PromptBox>
   Polish the text message below so it sounds polite but not stiff. "The time for tomorrow's meetup changed. Come at 8, not 7."
   </PromptBox>

6. **Start a new conversation (2 min)**

   When the topic changes, it's best to open a new conversation. Look for a button like "New chat" on the screen and click it. Your earlier conversations stay in the list, so you can reopen them.

</Steps>

## Quick reference

| What you want | Try saying |
| --- | --- |
| Explain simply | "Explain it so even an elementary school student could understand" |
| Make it shorter | "Cut it down to three lines" |
| Change the tone | "Rewrite it more politely / more casually" |
| Fix a mistake | "I think number 2 is wrong. Show me your sources" |
| When you're not sure | "What else do I need to tell you to answer this question?" |

## Common sticking points

### The answer comes out in a language I didn't expect

- **Cause**: Your question may be in another language, or the service may have guessed the language wrong.
- **Fix**: Add one line such as "Please answer in English."

### The answer is too long or too hard

- **Cause**: You didn't say what form you wanted it in.
- **Fix**: Tell it the length and difficulty, such as "within five lines" or "in plain language."

### It seems to have forgotten what I just said

- **Cause**: You may have opened a new conversation, or the conversation may have gotten very long.
- **Fix**: Keep going in the same conversation, and repeat important conditions once more.

### I see a message saying "You've reached your usage limit"

- **Cause**: You've used up the free plan's limit.
- **Fix**: You can use it again after the time shown. Claude is described as refreshing its limit in 5-hour windows (as of 2026-10-10). If you're in a hurry, you can use another service.

### It confidently states wrong answers

- **Cause**: AI can make up plausible answers even for things it doesn't know.
- **Fix**: Double-check important facts on official sites or other reliable sources.

## Practical tips

- **Say a little at a time, and say it often.** Don't try to get it perfect in one go; refine it as in a conversation. Claude's official getting-started guide also advises starting with a simple question, being specific, and refining with follow-up questions.
- **Give it the situation.** Tell it who the writing is for and what it's for, and the results get much better.
- **Leave out personal information.** Mask details like your name, address, and account numbers before asking.
