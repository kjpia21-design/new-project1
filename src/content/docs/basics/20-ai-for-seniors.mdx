<Summary>
- This is written for **you, the son, daughter, or grandchild** who is helping. Sit beside them, let them hold the phone, and keep the first session short.
- **Make it easy to see and say**: raise the phone's text size, then try a voice conversation. Claude's voice mode is a beta feature on all plans, and Gemini Live is documented for Android phones and tablets.
- **Start with one real question** from their own day, not a tour of features.
- **Teach three habits**: AI can be wrong, never type in passwords or card numbers, and a voice or video asking for money may be fake. Call a family member on a number you already know.
- Plans and features change. Details below are as of 2026-11-04 from the official pages listed in Sources.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Prepare a phone so text is easier to read and a voice conversation is available.
- Pick a first question that shows the value quickly and gently.
- Explain, in plain words, what not to share and how to spot a scam.
- Leave behind a short card of rules and a few questions they can reuse.

## Concepts to know first

You should already know the basics: [tutorial 1](/basics/01-first-30-minutes/) covers a first session, and [tutorial 7](/basics/07-ai-on-phone/) covers installing the apps and voice. If you are helping a person with low vision or hearing difficulty, also see [tutorial 19](/basics/19-ai-accessibility/). For children, see [AI for families](/basics/14-ai-for-families/) instead.

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Helper** | You: the person who installs, explains, and stays within reach. |
| **Voice mode / Live** | Talking and listening instead of typing. |
| **Dictation** | Your speech is turned into typed text, with no spoken reply. |
| **Scam** | A message or call that pretends to be someone trusted to get money or secrets. |

## Step-by-step guide

<Checklist items={[
  'The text on their phone is large enough for them to read comfortably',
  'We tried one spoken question and one typed question',
  'They know what never to type into an AI app',
  'They know the scam rule: stop, then call a family member on a known number',
  'We wrote down two or three questions they can reuse',

<Steps>

1. **Choose the place and the pace**

   Pick a calm time with no hurry. Plan for 20 to 30 minutes and one goal. Let them hold the phone and touch the screen, even when it is slower. If they get tired, stop and continue another day.

2. **Make the screen easy to read first**

   On Android, open Settings, search "Font size" and "Display size", and move the sliders. On iPhone, look under Settings, Accessibility, Display & Text Size. Ask them to read a sentence aloud and adjust until it is comfortable. The Android page says the font setting does not apply to some apps, so check inside the AI app too.

3. **Install one app, not three**

   Choose the one you can support well. Sign in together using an account they control, and keep the password in a place they can find, not in your head. Use [tutorial 2](/basics/02-sign-up-first-question/) for sign-up. Do not pay for a plan during the first session; see [tutorial 8](/basics/08-free-vs-paid/).

4. **Try a spoken question**

   Many people find talking easier than typing. In Claude, tap the sound wave icon next to the microphone. In Gemini on Android, tap Live. Ask them to say a question about their day. If the AI interrupts or mishears, Claude's help page suggests a quieter place, push-to-talk, and checking microphone permission.

   <PromptBox>
   I am helping my mother try an AI assistant for the first time. Please answer in short sentences with everyday words. Give one idea at a time and then ask if she wants more.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Ask a real first question**

   Choose something from their own life, such as a recipe for what is in the fridge, what a word on a letter means, or how to write a short message to a grandchild. Avoid questions where a wrong answer could hurt them.

   <PromptBox>
   Please explain this letter in simple words and tell me what it is asking me to do. I will read the original again before I act on anything.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Teach what never to type**

   Say it simply: never type passwords, bank or card numbers, ID numbers, or the codes sent by text message. Do not photograph documents with those details. See [tutorial 5](/basics/05-privacy-safety/) for the longer list.

7. **Teach the scam rule**

   AI can also be used to make a fake voice or video of someone they love. A call asking for urgent money is the warning sign, even if it sounds like family. The rule: hang up, then call that person back on a number you already have. Agree a family code word in advance if it helps. See [tutorial 10](/basics/10-ai-scams-deepfakes/).

8. **Teach "check before you act"**

   The AI can be confident and wrong. For health, money, legal, or safety matters, they should ask a doctor, pharmacist, bank, or a family member. [Tutorial 4](/basics/04-fact-checking/) shows how to check an answer.

   <PromptBox>
   Before you answer, tell me if you are not sure. Then tell me who I should ask to double-check this, such as a doctor, a pharmacist, or my bank.
   </PromptBox>

9. **Leave a small card behind**

   Write three lines on paper: what the app is for, what never to type, and who to call when unsure. Add your phone number. Offer to check in after a week.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
Respect their independence. Ask what they want to try rather than what you think they need, and do not take over the phone. Never set up the AI to act on their behalf (for example, to contact others or pay) without a clear discussion with them.
</Aside>

## Quick reference

| I want to... | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Make the text bigger | Android: search "Font size". iPhone: Settings, Accessibility, Display & Text Size |
| Let them talk instead of type | Claude voice mode, or Gemini Live on Android |
| Start with something safe | A recipe, a word's meaning, a short message to family |
| Avoid scams | Hang up, then call back on a number you already know |
| Avoid oversharing | No passwords, card numbers, or text-message codes |

## Common sticking points

### They do not understand the answer

- **Cause**: The reply was long or used unfamiliar words.
- **Fix**: Use the short-sentence prompt in step 4, and ask for one idea at a time.

### The app does not hear them

- **Cause**: Background noise, a weak connection, or microphone permission.
- **Fix**: Move somewhere quiet, try push-to-talk in Claude, and check the microphone permission. These are the steps Claude's help page lists.

### They are afraid of breaking something

- **Cause**: New technology and fear of mistakes or scams.
- **Fix**: Say that a chat is only text on a screen and cannot spend money by itself. Then repeat the rule about never typing card numbers.

### They trusted a wrong answer

- **Cause**: The AI sounded sure.
- **Fix**: Ask them to show you the answer. Check it together with an official source, as in tutorial 4.

## Practical tips

- Keep the first session to one app and one goal.
- Write your reminders on paper, in large print.
- Check in after a week, then once a month.
- This page is a family guide, not medical, legal, or financial advice.
