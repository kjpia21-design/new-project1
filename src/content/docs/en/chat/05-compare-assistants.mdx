## What you'll be able to do

- Build comparison criteria and a scorecard that fit your work.
- Fairly test the three services with the same prompt.
- Know which items on pricing, limits, and privacy policy to verify on official pages.
- Pull together the reasoning you need when proposing a tool to your team.

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Plan tier** | Subscription levels such as free, personal paid, team, and enterprise. Names and features differ by service. |
| **Usage limit** | The cap on how much you can use within a period of time. The exact numbers are often not published. |
| **Model training policy** | The rules on whether your conversations are used to improve the service, and whether you can turn that off. |
| **Context window** | The amount of text the AI can see at once. |

## Step-by-step

<Steps>

1. **Choose the tasks to compare**

   Pick the 2 or 3 jobs you'll hand to AI most often in your day. Examples include summarizing meeting notes ([Tutorial 2](/en/chat/02-summarize-documents/)), analyzing tabular data ([Tutorial 3](/en/chat/03-data-analysis/)), and drafting a report ([Tutorial 4](/en/chat/04-write-reports/)). Use samples with personal information and confidential details masked.

2. **Build a scoring rubric**

   <PromptBox label="Request to build a scorecard (paste into any service)">
   I want to compare AI services for work. The tasks are [summarizing meeting notes, analyzing sales CSV, drafting a proposal]. Build a scorecard for evaluating the outputs. The criteria are accuracy, instruction following, format, tone, and time needed for edits, each scored 1 to 5, with a one-line scoring guideline for each.
   </PromptBox>

3. **Test with the same prompt**

   Build a prompt once using the 6-slot framework from [Tutorial 1](/en/chat/01-prompt-basics/), and paste it into all three services without changing a single character. If the model picker or features (web search, file analysis) differ by service, note which settings you used.

   - Mixing paid and free tiers makes for an unfair comparison. Compare at the same price level.
   - Even within the same service, results can vary with the model and settings.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Layout for comparing three services with the same prompt" />

4. **Evaluate the results**

   Scoring without knowing which service produced which result reduces bias. Ask a coworker to score them too. Check accuracy against the original material.

5. **Check cost and limits**

   Check the following on each of the three services' official pricing pages. Summaries in web search results and tables on blogs can be outdated or inconsistent.

   - The monthly price of the plan that fits your workload, and any annual billing discount
   - What unit the usage limit applies in, and what happens when you exceed it
   - Whether the features you'll use (file analysis, web search, file creation, app integrations) are included in that plan
   - Pricing in Korean won (KRW), tax, payment methods, and Korean-language support (for users in Korea)
   - Whether you need a team or enterprise plan

6. **Check security and training policies**

   If you'll be handling company data, this step matters more than price.

   - Whether your conversations are used for model training, and whether you can turn that off
   - Policy differences between personal accounts and team or enterprise accounts
   - Admin features (permissions, audit logs) and data retention settings
   - The list of services your company's security guidelines allow

</Steps>

## How do we build "use this for that" recommendations?

This site has a principle of publishing **only recommendations backed by official documentation.** Because we couldn't check ChatGPT's upload, usage, and training policies to the same standard, we can't compare all three services under fully equal conditions, so we don't publish task-by-task rankings with service names attached. Instead, here are decision rules by situation.

| Situation | Decision rule |
| --- | --- |
| Your company already uses certain work tools | Test the service that integrates with those tools first. Check the scope of integration in each service's official help pages |
| You handle customer information or internal documents | Check the training policy of the organization plan in official docs, and get your security lead's approval |
| You often work with long documents | Compare upload and context limits. Claude allows 20 files per chat, 1,000 PDF pages, and up to 1M tokens depending on the model; Gemini offers 1M tokens on AI Pro and Ultra |
| You need Excel or Word files as output | Test file-format output yourself. Claude can create .xlsx .docx .pptx and PDF files |
| You use it heavily | Check the limit units and what happens when you exceed them in official docs. Claude has a 5-hour session limit and a weekly limit ([C2](/en/claude/c02-plans-limits/)) |
| You're not sure | Decide using the side-by-side test in "Step-by-step" above |

## Common sticking points

### Every blog lists different prices

- **Cause**: Prices and plan names change often and vary by region. Many posts are outdated.
- **Fix**: Trust only the current values on the official pricing pages. We've dated the tables on this site too (2026-10-10).

### Results from each service were inconsistent in one test

- **Cause**: Even within the same service, results can differ a little from run to run.
- **Fix**: Run each of your 2 or 3 work samples more than once and judge by the overall impression.

### I ended up comparing free with paid

- **Cause**: The models and features in free and paid tiers differ by service.
- **Fix**: Compare at the same price level, and record which plan you tested with.

### My company has already decided which tools to use

- **Cause**: Security policies sometimes limit which services are allowed.
- **Fix**: Don't upload work data with a personal account. First check the allowed list and whether an organization account is available.

## Practical tips

- **Don't get locked into one tool.** The prompt framework ([Tutorial 1](/en/chat/01-prompt-basics/)) works across tools. If you collect your templates, switching is easy.
- **Re-test every quarter.** Models and plans change often.
- **Try the free tier before you pay.** Check the refund rules on the official page before paying. Claude's pricing page says customers in the EEA and the UK have a 14-day withdrawal period.
- **If you choose Claude**, see [C1](/en/claude/c01-overview/) for choosing a model and [C2](/en/claude/c02-plans-limits/) for plans and limits.
