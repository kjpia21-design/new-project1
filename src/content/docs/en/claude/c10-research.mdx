<Summary>
- **Web search** is for fact-checking questions that one or two searches can answer. Every response comes with citations.
- **Research** searches many times, step by step, and gives you a report with citations. It's available on the web, desktop, and mobile on paid plans (Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise), and web search must be turned on.
- The official help says to use Research for "comprehensive investigations that take 5 or more tool calls and several minutes" (wording as of 2026-10-26).
- Both features count toward your usage limits. Research in particular can use up your limit faster.
- The most important habit is clicking the citation links to check the sources yourself.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do

- Pick the right approach for the size of your question: regular chat, web search, or Research.
- Turn on Research and get a report from a narrowed-down question.
- Verify sources through citation links, and fill gaps with follow-up questions.
- Run research while saving your usage.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Web search** | A feature that answers based on live web content. If you give it a URL, it can also fetch the whole page. |
| **Citation** | A link showing which web source an answer came from. Included in every web search response. |
| **Research** | A feature where Claude searches many times, deciding for itself what to look into next based on earlier results. It also draws on your connected Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Docs along with the web. |
| **Extended thinking** | For tasks that need deep reasoning without fresh web information (math, debugging, and so on). It's separate from web search. |

### Which approach should you pick?

| Type of question | Right approach | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A factual question one or two searches can answer | Web search | "What's the weather in Seoul today?", "What was yesterday's game score?" |
| A problem that needs deep reasoning without fresh information | Extended thinking | Math problems, code debugging |
| A comprehensive investigation that gathers, compares, and organizes many sources | Research | Competitor comparisons, summarizing policy changes |

The official help describes web search as for "simple factual questions that can be answered with one or two tool calls" and Research as for "comprehensive investigations that take 5 or more tool calls and several minutes." It also says that using Research together with extended thinking can give you a longer, more thorough report.

### Supported models

Web search works with several models, including Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, and Fable 5.1 (as of 2026-10-10). For a model comparison, see [C1. Claude at a glance](/en/claude/c01-overview/).

<Aside type="note" title="The unified Claude experience looks different">
In the new unified Claude experience, there's no separate web search toggle, and Claude searches automatically when it decides that would help. You start Research by typing `/deep-research` or using the "+" button (as of 2026-10-10). The layout can vary by account and app version.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Research limits are the same as regular chat">
The official help explains that Research uses the same limits as regular conversations, and since it pulls in many sources, it can use up your limit faster (confirmed 2026-10-13). A cap on duration, a cap on count, and Research-specific limit numbers aren't officially disclosed. Check your own usage on the Settings > Usage screen.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Check web search**

   Click "+" in the input box and check that Web search is ticked. Research only works with web search turned on. In the unified experience, there may be no toggle. On a Team/Enterprise account, the organization Owner has to turn it on first under Organization settings > Capabilities.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Choosing web search and Research from the input box '+' menu" officialUrl="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11088861-use-research-on-claude" />

2. **Turn on Research**

   Select "+" > Research and it turns blue. Click again to turn it off. In the unified experience, you can also start with `/deep-research`. It's available on the web, desktop, and mobile on paid plans. Research runs on Opus 5.5 regardless of the model you picked for the conversation, and is said to fall back to Opus 4.8 when needed. On mobile, it searches only the web and skips connectors (as of 2026-10-19).

3. **Send a narrowed-down question**

   Putting the topic, criteria, and the form of result you want in one request makes the research direction clearer.

   <PromptBox>
   Summarize how South Korea's electric vehicle subsidies change in 2026, based on government announcements and news coverage. Put what changed in a table, and flag separately any places where the government announcement and news reports differ. At the end, also tell me what you couldn't confirm.
   </PromptBox>

   Research gives you an answer with citations within "a few minutes." You can wait while it works or do something else.

4. **Check sources through the citation links**

   Pick a few important numbers or claims in the report and click their citation links. This is the step where you confirm for yourself that the original really says that. Don't skip it if you'll use the report externally.

5. **Search your own materials too (optional)**

   If you've connected Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Docs, you can have Claude research your own materials too. For connecting, see [C7. Connecting Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Notion with connectors](/en/claude/c07-connectors/).

   <PromptBox>
   Also look in my Drive and Gmail for materials related to this topic, and put them together with what you found on the web. Mark which content came from my materials and which came from the web.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Refine with follow-up questions**

   Point out what's missing and ask again. Continuing in regular chat instead of rerunning Research from scratch saves usage.

   <PromptBox>
   From the report above, pick out the claims that have only one source. Search further to see whether there are other sources that support each of those claims, and tell me what you find.
   </PromptBox>

</Steps>

## Quick reference

### Web search vs. Research

| Item | Web search | Research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | Factual questions | Comprehensive investigation |
| Scale | One or two tool calls | 5 or more, several minutes (per the official help) |
| How to turn on | "+" > Web search (automatic in the unified experience) | "+" > Research or `/deep-research` |
| Using your materials | If you give a URL, it fetches the whole page | Uses Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Docs along with the web |
| Result | An answer with citations | A report with citations |
| Requirements | Account settings (on Team/Enterprise the Owner must turn it on first) | Paid plan + web search turned on |
| Usage | Counts toward your limit | Counts toward your limit, can be used up faster |

### Usage and admin settings

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Counted toward limits | Both web search and fetching count toward your limit. Fetching long texts uses a lot of context. |
| Free accounts | There's a usage limit, but the number and reset method aren't officially disclosed. The 5-hour session limit applies to paid plans ([C2](/en/claude/c02-plans-limits/)). |
| Overage | Extra usage (usage credits) applies to Research too. Research sessions burn through tokens quickly. |
| Image search | Shows images from search results with source links. Powered by Bing. |
| Location | Your approximate location may be inferred from your IP. |
| Connector tools | Research calls connector tools without additional approval. It can't use local MCP servers. The mobile app skips connectors and searches only the web (with no separate warning), so to include connectors, start from the web or desktop. There's a recommendation to turn off write-capable tools beforehand. |

For the overall structure of limits, see [C2. Plans and usage limits](/en/claude/c02-plans-limits/).

## Where people get stuck

### I don't see the Research menu

- **Cause**: You may be on the free plan, web search may be off, or on Team/Enterprise the Owner may not have turned the feature on.
- **Fix**: Check that you're on a paid plan and that web search is on. For an organization account, ask your administrator to check Organization settings > Capabilities. In the unified experience, try typing `/deep-research`.

### The report seems to lack sources or get something wrong

- **Cause**: Even with citations, the summarizing process can render the original differently.
- **Fix**: Click the citation links and check the original yourself. You can also ask in a follow-up, "Show me the exact sentence from the source for this claim."

### My limit is dropping faster than usual

- **Cause**: Research pulls in many sources, so it can use up your limit faster than regular chat.
- **Fix**: First consider whether web search is enough for a factual question. Turn off web search in conversations that don't need it.

### My Gmail and Drive materials aren't reflected

- **Cause**: They may not be connected, or your question may not have told Claude to look in your materials.
- **Fix**: Check the connector connections, and ask clearly: "Also look for related materials in my Drive/Gmail."

### A tool that uses a local MCP server doesn't work in research

- **Cause**: Research can't use local MCP servers.
- **Fix**: Use a remote connector. See [C7. Connecting Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Notion with connectors](/en/claude/c07-connectors/).

## Practical tips

- **Put criteria in your question.** Setting a scope like "based on government announcements and news coverage" or "only sources from 2025 onward" makes the results far more useful. For prompting tips, see [C4. Prompting well](/en/claude/c04-prompting/).
- **Ask for a table.** For comparison and summary research, asking for a table makes the gaps visible at a glance.
- **Have Claude list what wasn't confirmed.** Adding "also tell me what you couldn't confirm" cuts down on sentences that assert too much.
- **Turn off write-capable connectors ahead of time.** Research calls connector tools without additional approval. If you only need read access, turn off the write tools.
- **Use web search for factual questions.** Using Research for a question one or two searches can answer just uses more of your limit.
- **Treat the report as a draft.** Before using it for an important decision, open the key sources and check them yourself.
