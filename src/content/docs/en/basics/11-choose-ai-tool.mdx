<Summary>
- **Start from "what you want to do," not from a tool name.** For writing, summarizing, and asking questions, one chat AI handles most of it. When the output is a fixed kind of thing, such as translation, slides, images, or code, a dedicated tool can be more convenient.
- This tutorial is a **guide**. We don't rank tool quality. The same tool can feel different depending on your work and taste.
- Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini can all be **started for free** (per the official pricing pages as of 2026-10-31). Free plans have usage limits.
- At first, **pick just one chat AI** and use it for a week, and add a dedicated tool only when you hit something it can't handle.
- Before entering company documents or personal information, check the standards in [tutorial 5](/en/basics/05-privacy-safety/) first.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Narrow down on your own, "What kind of AI fits my work?"
- Find right away which tutorial on this site to read for each thing you want to do.
- After choosing a tool, point out what to check (pricing, limits, information safety).

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Chat AI** | An AI that answers in writing when you talk to it. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are the main examples. It handles writing, summarizing, questions, and organizing ideas broadly. |
| **Dedicated tool** | A service tuned for one kind of output, such as translation, slides, or music. Output formats and exporting are more convenient, but you end up using more places. |
| **AI inside work tools** | AI built into Excel, Word, email, or document tools. It's handy when your files are already in there. |
| **Agent** | An AI that goes beyond answering questions to do multi-step work for you. You need to take extra care with permissions and safety. |
| **Free plan** | Basic use that doesn't cost anything. There's a limit on how much you can use. We compare plans in [tutorial 8](/en/basics/08-free-vs-paid/). |

<Steps>

1. **Write down 3 things you want to do**

   Write what you'll actually do this week, like "draft a report," "translate an English email," or "make slides." That makes it much easier to choose than a vague "I want to use AI well."

2. **Find your task in the guide table below**

   The middle column shows **candidates worth trying**. It isn't a ranking, so open whichever tool you want to read about first. The tutorial in the right column continues from sign-up through how to use it.

3. **Pick one chat AI and use it free for a week**

   If choosing is hard, pick whichever of Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini is easiest to sign up for. A service you already have an account with is the fastest. Follow [tutorial 2](/en/basics/02-sign-up-first-question/) for the sign-up steps. If you want to compare the three with the same question, [chat tutorial 5](/en/chat/05-compare-assistants/) helps.

4. **When you hit something it can't handle, add a tool for that task**

   For example, if a chat AI's translation feels awkward, add a dedicated translation tool; if you need recent information with sources, add AI search. If you start using several at once, you'll lose track of what you put where.

5. **Check safety before you enter anything**

   Company materials, customer information, and personal information such as national ID numbers each have different storage and training terms in every tool. Check the standards in [tutorial 5](/en/basics/05-privacy-safety/) and your company's AI usage policy first.

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### There are too many tools and I can't choose

- **Cause**: If you try to find the right tool for each task from the start, the candidates grow endlessly.
- **Fix**: Start with one chat AI. Have it do the same task, and when you see where it falls short, look for a dedicated tool in the table.

### I keep wanting to subscribe to several paid plans

- **Cause**: Each tool's pitch catches your eye.
- **Fix**: Use free for a week, and only if you hit the limit often, pay for just one service for a month. See [tutorial 8](/en/basics/08-free-vs-paid/).

### The "best AI" rankings I saw on blogs don't agree

- **Cause**: Rankings vary with how they were tested, when, and what work the person does.
- **Fix**: Compare directly with one of your own tasks. The method is in [chat tutorial 5](/en/chat/05-compare-assistants/).

### It's the same AI but the answer differs from yesterday

- **Cause**: AI can answer a little differently each time even for the same question, and services are updated often.
- **Fix**: For important tasks, write the requirements and format specifically ([tutorial 3](/en/basics/03-asking-well/)), and check the results yourself ([tutorial 4](/en/basics/04-fact-checking/)).

## Practical tips

- **Start with one tool per task.** It's easier to remember what you put where.
- **Give the same task to two tools and compare.** Trying it on your own work is more accurate than trusting a ranking.
- **Take another look once a quarter.** Plans and features change often.
- **If you'd like bundles suited to your job,** see the role-based guides such as [office, planning, and management roles](/en/roles/office/), [students and job seekers](/en/roles/student/), and [solo business owners](/en/roles/solo-business/).
- **If you don't know where to start reading,** the [beginner starting point](/en/start/beginner/) has a reading order.

If choosing is hard, you can also ask AI like this. Don't write company names or personal information.

<PromptBox>
I'm [job or situation], and the things I want to do with AI this week are 1) [task 1], 2) [task 2], 3) [task 3]. I've barely used AI tools. For each task, tell me what kind of AI tool (chat AI, AI search, dedicated translation tool, and so on) fits and why. For tool prices and limits, only say what you're certain of, and if you don't know, tell me to check the official page.
</PromptBox>
