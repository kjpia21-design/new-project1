## Concepts to know first

### What is Muse?

Meta describes Muse as a "personal AI agent." Unlike a chatbot that answers questions, **you state a goal and it makes a plan and carries it out directly**. Examples include opening a browser, filling out forms, negotiating, sending email, and booking travel. It keeps working after you close the app and notifies you again when things change or it needs your approval.

It uses Meta's Muse Spark model (the safety post refers to Muse Spark 1.3). The names are easy to mix up, but **Muse Spark is the model name, and Muse is the agent service that uses this model**.

### Who made it, and when?

| Date | What happened | Source |

| 2026-09-08 | Meta announced Muse. Rolled out in the US on iOS, Android, and muse.ai | Meta Newsroom |
| 2026-09 (Connect 2026) | AI glasses support planned, voice mode, more connectors, a dedicated Muse email address planned | Meta Newsroom |

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Muse Secure VM** | A dedicated cloud computer given to each user. It's an isolated Linux machine with a full browser, and you can use it alongside Muse and step in at any time. |
| **Sentinel** | A separate agent that runs on the same computer, isolated from Muse. It's the only party that authorizes connector actions and outbound network traffic, and it asks you when needed. |
| **Connector** | A connection between Muse and external services such as email, calendar, Instagram, and Shopify. You set the access scope for each service. |
| **Muse Spark** | The Meta model that powers Muse. |
| **Link (Stripe)** | Muse's payment method. It issues a one-time card number for each payment. |
| **Muse Confidential VM** | A plan to encrypt the entire VM with a key only you hold, so even Meta can't access it. It's planned for later this year and is **not available yet**. |

### How is it different from a chat AI?

Boiled down, Meta's explanation is this: a chatbot answers one question, while an agent **carries out multi-step work on its own and keeps running after you close the app.** That's convenient, but **the damage is bigger when it does something wrong.** We cover this difference in more detail in [Using Personal AI Agents Safely](/en/agents/05-agent-safety/).

## What it can do

Everything here comes from official Meta materials. Actual behavior may vary by region, account, and time.

- **Hand off tasks**: Things like organizing and sending email, bookings, shopping, schedule management, and cutting costs. Give it a big goal (for example, selling a car for more, lowering a bill, or adjusting a workout plan to life changes) and it makes a plan and coordinates.
- **Browser tasks**: Searching, browsing sites, filling out forms, and making purchases. You can also watch the screen and take over control yourself.
- **Always-on**: Works on schedules and events even with the app closed, and notifies you "only when something is meaningfully new or needs your input." It sometimes sends proactive suggestions, which you can reduce or turn off.
- **Memory**: It remembers what you've told it once and suggests things proactively. For example, it turns recipe Reels you saved on Instagram into a shopping list. You can read and edit the memory file (MEMORY.md) yourself, and you can ask it to "forget."
- **Creating outputs**: It makes documents, PDFs, web pages, and interactive outputs (Artifacts) such as expense trackers or study guides.
- **Goal tracking**: In the Goals tab you can see the goals and plans Muse is tracking, and in the Ideas tab you can get suggestions.
- **Connected services**: It connects to email, calendar, Instagram, and more. Some services are named in official posts, as in the table below, and the full list appears in the Muse app settings. For services with an API or CLI, Muse can even build a custom connector itself.

| Category | Connected services named in official posts (partial) |
| --- | --- |
| Small Business post (9/29) | Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, Zoom, Facebook and Instagram business accounts |
| Connect post (September) | Shopping sites like Walmart, Best Buy, Sephora, Ulta, and Wayfair, plus Instacart, GitHub, and Shop Pay and PayPal added as payment methods. Expedia is "coming soon" |

## Payments, permissions, and privacy: what to watch for

### Payments

- Muse pays through **Stripe Link**. A **one-time card number** is generated for each purchase, so your real card details aren't visible to the merchant or the agent.
- Meta describes Muse as "the first AI agent covered by Link's buyer protection." It includes things like compensation for damaged or lost items, price drops, and fee-free returns, but **only for eligible purchases**. You'll need to check which purchases are eligible.
- On sites where a payment method is already saved, Meta says Muse detects the checkout screen and **asks you to confirm the purchase details each time**.
- The terms say not to take state-changing actions like financial transactions "without prior review and approval." They also say to be careful about granting permission in advance (pre-approval).
- There's a clause saying **you're responsible** for purchases you approved by mistake and for consequences of Muse's wrong judgments. Meta's liability cap in the terms is set at $250 or the amount you paid in the last 12 months, whichever is greater.

### Permissions

| Safeguard | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Approval cards | Sensitive actions like sending email and purchases ask for approval first. Approval requests come as a separate window in the app, not in your conversation with Muse. |
| Approval scope | You can choose just this once, always allow, or deny. Technical docs also describe allowing per session, task, or time period. |
| Separate read and write | If the service supports it, you can grant "read-only" and "send as well" separately. For email, you can choose read-only access. |
| Email protection | The email connector filters out one-time verification codes, password reset links, and login links. |
| Activity log | You can see what Muse has done and plans to do in the activity log. You can change permissions or disconnect at any time. |
| Credentials | Passwords go to a secure store that Muse can't see, and are entered into the browser only when needed. |

By default, normal web browsing is allowed and it **stops at actions that are hard to undo**. You can make this stricter or looser. We recommend starting with the stricter setting.

### Privacy

- **AI training is on when you first start.** You can turn it off under Settings > Data controls, and the Muse privacy notice says turning it off also applies to earlier interactions. Even when it's on, Meta says it removes some personal information such as names, emails, phone numbers, and Social Security numbers and separates the data from your account.
- Meta says it **doesn't share conversation and VM data with Meta's ad systems**. However, Meta also explains that because Muse's web browsing looks like your own activity, having it visit a shopping site, for example, can influence the ads that business shows.
- Deletion: You can delete messages, side chats, and VM files, and "Reset Muse" erases all your Muse data. Muse may still remember what it learned even after deletion, so the guidance is to use the Forget feature.
- Meta itself states that **the current architecture doesn't prevent Meta from accessing data when needed**. Muse Confidential VM, which cryptographically blocks that access, is planned for later this year and isn't available yet.
- Meta acknowledges that Muse also makes mistakes and can be attacked through the data it reads (prompt injection). So it says it built a structure (Sentinel, isolation, approvals) that "assumes it can be attacked" and limits the damage. This structure is Meta's own description, and we didn't verify independent validation results in this guide.
- Some media and communities have reported privacy controversies. We don't cover specific claims here because they haven't been verified.

## How it differs from other agents

These are Muse's characteristics as read from official materials. They describe differences in approach, not which is better.

- It's **for general users**. It's built so you can use it like sending messages, without coding or setup, and you can even talk to it inside WhatsApp.
- It highlights a **dedicated cloud computer per user and a separate Sentinel architecture**. The phrase "protections other agents don't offer" is Meta's claim, and we didn't verify it by direct comparison.
- **Connections to Meta apps like Instagram and Facebook, and to shopping services** are presented as strengths.
- The default is a **free-plus-usage-limit** model.

## Common sticking points

### Muse doesn't show up in the app store, or I can't sign up

- **Cause**: Korea isn't an officially supported region.
- **Fix**: Wait for the official launch. We don't recommend signing up through workarounds. It could violate the terms.

### I want to check the plans, but they seem to differ by region

- **Cause**: The Meta Help Center says Muse and subscriptions are in limited testing and benefits may vary by region and account.
- **Fix**: Check what's shown during sign-up (onboarding) before you subscribe.

### Can I trust numbers I saw in the news (download counts, free limits)?

- **Cause**: Figures like download counts or free weekly tokens often come from a single outlet or a tester's report.
- **Fix**: Treat figures without an official source as reference only. We haven't included them in this guide either.

## Practical tips (for when you use it in a supported region)

- Give it **read permissions first**. Add write permissions once you're comfortable.
- **Turn on payments last.** Check the amount and the item on the purchase approval screen yourself every time.
- **Keep connections to a minimum.** Connect only the services you need and disconnect ones you don't use.
- **Open the activity log once in a while.** You can see what Muse has done and what it's about to do.
- **Check the AI training setting** at the start. It's on by default.
- **Don't stay logged in on a shared device.** It can access your dedicated VM and connected services.
