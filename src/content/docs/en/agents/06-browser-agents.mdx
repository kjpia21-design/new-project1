<Summary>
- A **browser agent** is an AI that reads web pages, clicks, types, and navigates to do work for you. All three companies are building one, but **the names and setups differ.**
- There are two main approaches: **using your own browser** (it uses your logged-in sites as they are, but the risk grows too) and **using a separate browser in the cloud** (it's separate from your logins, but you have to step in yourself when logging in).
- Everything is as of 2026-10-15 and read directly from official docs. For plans and regions we used **only what the docs state**, and we don't rank quality. Cells we couldn't confirm are marked as such.
- The common principles are the same: **you approve payments, sending messages, and deletions; you don't type logins and passwords into chat; and you don't trust instructions inside web pages.**
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Tell apart **which approach** each of the browser agents from Claude in Chrome, ChatGPT, and Gemini uses.
- Know **where in the official docs to check** what you can use on your plan, device, and region.
- Judge which tasks to hand off and which not to, and choose safe settings.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Browser agent** | An AI that looks at web pages and clicks, types, and navigates for you. It goes beyond looking things up to actions like filling out forms and making reservations. |
| **Your-own-browser approach** | It attaches to your Chrome or similar as an extension and works there. It can use the sites you're logged in to as they are. |
| **Cloud browser approach** | A separate browser running on the AI service's servers. It doesn't use your tabs, cookies, or saved passwords. |
| **Prompt injection** | An attack that hides instructions meant to trick the AI in a web page or email. It matters especially for browser agents because they keep reading unfamiliar web content. |

<Aside type="note" title="Product names differ from doc to doc">
What we'd call the "ChatGPT agent" in this guide is currently described in the official user docs (learn.chatgpt.com) as split into the **Browser** (built-in browser), the **browser extension**, and the **cloud browser in ChatGPT Work**. For Gemini, **Tasks** use **Chrome auto browse** or a remote browser. Names change often, so look at the labels on screen along with the official docs.
</Aside>

### 1. Do you really need browser control?

If you only need to read and summarize a page, regular chat or search is enough. Browser control is slow and can produce errors. For services like Gmail and Drive, connectors are faster and more accurate (see [C7](/en/claude/c07-connectors/) and "Which tool should I use?" in [C11](/en/claude/c11-chrome-computer-use/)).

### 2. Your own browser, or a cloud browser?

| When | Matching approach |
| --- | --- |
| I need to work on a work site I'm already logged in to | Your-own-browser approach (Claude in Chrome, the ChatGPT extension, Gemini's Chrome auto browse) |
| It has to keep running when my computer is off | Cloud browser approach (ChatGPT Work, Gemini remote browser) |
| I don't want it touching my tabs, cookies, or saved passwords | Cloud browser approach or the ChatGPT built-in browser (separate profile) |

### 3. Make your first task read-only

<PromptBox>
Compare the three pricing plans on this page in a table. Don't click or type anything on the page; just read.
</PromptBox>

Once you're comfortable, widen the scope one step at a time. Asking it to stop right before payment or submission and show you is also a good approach.

<PromptBox>
Compare these flight search results against my criteria (dates, budget cap). Stop right before the payment or personal information screen, and tell me which options you picked and why.
</PromptBox>

### The safety principles are shared

| Risk | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Hidden instructions** | All three companies tell you to treat web content as an untrusted context. Watch it closely on unfamiliar sites. |
| **My logged-in accounts** | With the your-own-browser approach, the agent can use every site you can access. Don't hand off banking, healthcare, or government sites. |
| **Irreversible actions** | Check payments, submissions, and deletions yourself. Even with approval features, they aren't perfect. |
| **"Always allow" settings** | Choose settings like Always allow and Allow for all sites carefully. The ChatGPT docs mark Allow for all sites as a high-risk setting. |
| **Passwords** | Don't type them in chat; use each service's secure input flow or enter them yourself. |

A broader checklist is in [Part 5, Using Personal AI Agents Safely](/en/agents/05-agent-safety/), and Claude in Chrome's permissions are in [C11](/en/claude/c11-chrome-computer-use/).

## Common sticking points

### I don't see the menu or feature

- **Cause**: It could be your plan, device, account type (work and school accounts are restricted), or a phased rollout. Gemini Tasks need a personal account, and site login in Work is noted as not available on Enterprise and Edu.
- **Fix**: Update the app and check the conditions in the table above one by one. The docs say that during a rollout it may not show up right away even if your plan fits.

### A site blocks it

- **Cause**: Some sites block automated browsers or demand a CAPTCHA (stated in the ChatGPT docs).
- **Fix**: Do that step yourself or use another method. Don't try to get around it.

### What happens if my computer turns off?

- The **your-own-browser approach** needs your computer and browser to be on. The Gemini docs also say a remote browser can be used if your device or Chrome is off. Claude in Chrome's background tasks work only while Chrome is open ([C11](/en/claude/c11-chrome-computer-use/)).
- The **cloud browser approach** continues even if you close your computer, but it stops at steps that need a login or confirmation and waits for you.

## Practical tips

- **Widen the scope in the order read, draft, then execute.** For the first few days, hand off only lookups and comparisons.
- **If you don't need logged-in sites, a cloud or separate browser is better**, which is a structural advantage. The ChatGPT docs also say Work's browser doesn't use your tabs, cookies, or saved passwords.
- **Be careful with scheduled browser tasks.** The Gemini docs say you may not be able to stop it during a scheduled run, so don't schedule sensitive tasks.
- **Clean up site permissions regularly.** Review the extension's allowlist from time to time and remove sites you don't use.
- **Check copyright and terms.** Don't redistribute text or images the agent pulled as is ([Basics Part 9](/en/basics/09-ai-copyright-ethics/)). A site's terms may prohibit automation.
- **Features change fast.** The figures and names in this guide are as of 2026-10-15.
