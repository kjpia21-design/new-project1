<Summary>
- All three services work the same way: **you turn AI on during the meeting, and a summary appears afterward**. The names differ: Meet calls it **Take notes for me**, Zoom calls it **Meeting summary** (Zoom AI Companion), and Teams uses **Copilot** (the Recap tab).
- Almost all require a **paid or work plan**. Meet needs an eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan, Zoom needs a paid Workplace plan, and Teams needs Microsoft 365 plus a Copilot license (as of 2026-10-15).
- Korean: Meet (notes and transcription) and Zoom (Meeting summary) have Korean on the supported-language lists in their official documentation. Teams has Korean on the Copilot supported-language list, but we couldn't confirm the support scope for the meeting summary feature alone.
- **Tell attendees before you turn it on.** All three services show an indicator to participants when it starts, but the service doesn't obtain consent for you.
- Summaries can be wrong. Compare numbers, owners, and deadlines against the original before sharing.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do

- Know **where to turn on** AI meeting notes in the meeting service your team uses.
- Compare each of the three services' **required plan, Korean support, and where results are saved** at a glance.
- Learn the sequence for handling **consent and notice** before recording or transcribing.
- Reorganize an AI summary into an **action-item table** with Claude or similar.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Recording** | A feature that saves video and sound to a file. |
| **Transcript** | A written record of what was said. It's the raw material for the summary. |
| **AI summary (meeting notes)** | A result where AI organizes a summary, decisions, and to-dos based on the transcript (speech recognition). |
| **Host / organizer** | The person who created the meeting. Most of the permission to turn features on and off belongs to the host (and co-hosts). |
| **Consent requirement setting** | A setting that has an admin require participants' explicit consent (Start/Join, etc.) when recording, transcription, or AI notes begin. According to Meet's documentation, it's off by default. |

## Comparing the three services

| Item | Google Meet | Zoom | Microsoft Teams |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feature name | Take notes for me (Gemini) | Meeting summary (Zoom AI Companion) | Copilot in Teams meetings, Recap tab after the meeting |
| What you need | An eligible Google Workspace edition or Google AI plan | A licensed user on a Zoom Workplace Pro, Pro Plus, Business, Business Plus, Enterprise, Enterprise Plus, or Enterprise Bundle account | Microsoft 365 and a Microsoft Copilot license |
| Korean | Korean is among the 8 spoken meeting languages (one language at a time) | Korean is on the Meeting summary supported-language list | Korean is on the Copilot supported-language list. Specific scope for meeting summaries not confirmed |
| Who turns it on | Organizer/participants (within the organization). If host management is on, only the host and co-hosts | Host and co-hosts (participants can request it) | The organizer sets it in the meeting option **Allow Copilot and Facilitator** |
| Transcript required? | The notes feature itself generates from audio | Optional: check Generate transcript | Transcription is required by default. With the "during the meeting only" option, you can use it during the meeting without a transcript |
| Where results go | The "Google Meet" folder in the organizer's Drive, attached to the Calendar event, a link by email | Email, the meeting group chat (if turned on), Summaries in the web portal | The Recap tab in the meeting chat or Calendar event |
| Notice to participants | Notification to all participants (pencil icon) | An AI-active indicator (the diamond changes color) | A transcription notice. All participants are notified when recording starts |
| Limits | Meetings of 15 minutes to 8 hours recommended | Not available in breakout rooms | Copilot can't be used in meetings created by people outside your organization |

(Sources are each service's official help, as of 2026-10-15. Details can vary by account type and admin settings.)

## Step-by-step

The example is "a 30-minute weekly team meeting." You can follow just the steps for the service you use.

<Steps>

1. **Before the meeting: notify and check settings**

   In the invitation or at the start of the meeting, tell people "We're using an AI summary (recording and transcription) to take meeting notes," and check that no one objects. This notice is something a person has to handle, separate from what the service displays automatically. If an admin has turned on the consent requirement setting, participants will see a choice screen such as Start/Join/Continue. On a work account, the AI feature may be turned off by admin settings, so check ahead of time.

2. **Google Meet: turn on Take notes for me**

   Join the meeting and click **Take notes for me** at the top right of the screen. To turn it on in advance, open the event in Google Calendar and check "Take notes with Gemini" under Video call options > Meeting records. Before it starts, you can choose who receives the notes (all invited guests, within the organization, or only the host and co-hosts) and the notes language. It supports only one language at a time, so for meetings where people mix Korean and English, the notes may come out awkward.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Take notes for me at the top right of Meet and its settings window" officialUrl="https://support.google.com/meet/answer/14754931" />

   When the meeting ends, a notes document is created in the "Google Meet" folder in the organizer's Drive and attached to the Calendar event. The document comes in two tabs: **Quick notes** (key summary and to-dos) and **Full notes** (summary, decisions, to-dos, and details).

3. **Zoom: turn on Meeting summary**

   The host hovers over the Zoom AI icon at the top right of the meeting window to open the panel, checks **Meeting summary**, and clicks **Start Zoom AI**. If needed, also check **Generate transcript**. Participants can ask the host to start it. For a Korean-language meeting, the official documentation advises setting the caption language to Korean.

   <FlowSteps title="Starting a meeting summary in Zoom (host, desktop)" officialUrl="https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0058013" steps={[
     { where: 'Top right of the meeting window', action: 'Hover over the Zoom AI icon', result: 'The in-meeting AI panel' },
     { where: 'AI panel', action: 'Check Meeting summary (optional: Generate transcript)', result: 'Summary is ready' },
     { where: 'AI panel', action: 'Click Start Zoom AI', result: 'AI-active indicator shown to participants' },
     { where: 'After the meeting', action: 'Check in email or Summaries in the web portal', result: 'View, edit, and share the summary' },
   ]} />

   The summary arrives by email, and if the meeting group chat is on, it's posted to Chat too. In the web portal's Summaries page you can view, edit, share, and delete it. For participants to receive the summary automatically, they need to be signed in to Zoom. If you want to leave an important discussion out of the AI, the host unchecks the box or clicks **Stop Zoom AI**.

4. **Teams: turn on Copilot and use Recap**

   The meeting organizer sets **Allow Copilot and Facilitator** under Online meeting options > Copilot and other AI on the meeting scheduling screen. There are three options: use during and after the meeting (transcription required), use during the meeting only, or off. Turning it off also turns off recording and transcription. During the meeting, click **Copilot** in the meeting controls. After the meeting, in the **Recap** tab of the meeting chat, choose Copilot > View prompts > **Recap the meeting**.

   <PromptBox>
   Put the decisions made in this meeting and the to-dos assigned to me into a table. Also list separately any questions that were left unresolved.
   </PromptBox>

   To ask Copilot questions after the meeting, transcription must have been on during the meeting. The official documentation says that with the "during the meeting only" option, voice data from parts that weren't transcribed and the Copilot conversation disappear when the meeting ends.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Opening Copilot from the Recap tab of a Teams meeting chat" officialUrl="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings-events/recap-a-teams-meeting" />

5. **Review the summary**

   AI summaries can be wrong or leave things out. Meet's documentation also says summaries may be incomplete or not generated because of connection problems or short meetings, and Teams' documentation also tells you to check the results. Compare numbers, names, deadlines, and owners against the original (the transcript or your memory) and fix what's wrong.

6. **Reorganize into an action-item table and share**

   Paste the summary text into Claude and convert it to your team's shared format. Before pasting, first check that it's a tool your internal security policy allows (see [Using AI while keeping personal and company information safe](/basics/05-privacy-safety/)).

   <PromptBox>
   Below is a meeting summary made by AI. It may contain errors. Organize it in the following format.
   1. One-line summary
   2. Decisions (numbered list)
   3. Action-item table (owner / deadline / task)
   4. Open items
   5. Parts that need to be checked against the original (numbers, names, deadlines)
   Don't make up owners or deadlines that aren't in the text. Write "TBD" instead.

   (Paste the summary text here)
   </PromptBox>

</Steps>

## Recording and transcription consent, and etiquette

<Aside type="caution" title="Even when the service shows a notice, consent isn't automatically taken care of">
All three services show a notice or indicator to participants when AI features or recording start. Teams' help says "depending on where you are, you may need everyone's permission to record." We can't confirm Korea's specific legal requirements from these official documents. In general, doing the following is safe.
</Aside>

- Tell people twice that you're **using AI meeting notes: in the invitation and at the start** of the meeting.
- If there are outside people (clients, partners), get their **consent in advance, such as by email**. External participants may have different restrictions from participants inside your organization (for example, in Meet, the people who can stop the notes are participants inside the host's organization).
- For sensitive discussions (HR, pay, legal, customer personal information), **pause the AI** or don't turn it on at all, and tell people you've turned it off. Meet's guidance says any participant inside the organization can stop the notes, so confidential discussions can be kept out of the summary.
- Check **who can see the results**. Meet notes depend on sharing settings, and Teams recordings can by default be downloaded and deleted only by the organizer and co-organizers.
- Your company policy and the guidance of your legal and security teams always come first.

## Common sticking points

### I can't click Take notes for me

- **Cause**: According to Meet's help, the organizer's Workspace edition may not have the feature, the admin may not have turned it on, or Smart features may be off. If host management is on, only the host and co-hosts can turn it on.
- **Fix**: Ask your admin which item is turned off.

### I don't see the AI icon in Zoom

- **Cause**: Your account plan, or the Meeting summary setting, may be off. Participants don't have permission to start it.
- **Fix**: Send the host an **Ask host to start Zoom AI** request. If an admin is in the same meeting, a prompt to turn it on right there may appear; otherwise an email request goes to the admin.

### Copilot doesn't know the content after a Teams meeting

- **Cause**: Transcription may not have been on during the meeting. The official documentation says questions after the meeting require a transcript. In meetings created by people outside your organization, Copilot can't be used at all.
- **Fix**: From the next meeting, keep transcription on and check the organizer's options.

### The summary has awkward Korean or mixed-in English

- **Cause**: Meet supports only one language at a time. Zoom adapts when it detects a non-Korean language, but when several languages are mixed, it summarizes in the language most people used. We couldn't confirm Teams' Korean quality.
- **Fix**: Set the meeting language (Meet notes language, Zoom caption language) to Korean, and read and fix the result yourself.

## Practical tips

- Saying the agenda and attendee names once at the start of the meeting makes the summary easier to organize.
- Meet notes may be incomplete for short meetings under 15 minutes. For important short meetings, take notes yourself.
- Review and share the summary **within 24 hours** of the meeting so you can fix it while it's still fresh in memory.
- If you already have a recording file, you can do the same organizing with the methods in [Turning meeting and lecture recordings into text](/media/14-transcribe-recordings/).
- For recurring meetings, keep the organizing prompt above fixed so you get the same format every time.
