## Who this is for

This page is for marketing teams, content managers, brand operators, and in-house editors.

- You run out of content ideas for the week and lose a lot of time staring at a blank screen.
- You keep rewriting the same piece to fit each channel's length and tone.
- Pulling together campaign numbers for a report eats up a whole day.

## Top 5 ways AI helps

### 1. Generate several options for copy and headlines

**What it can do**: Produce 5 to 10 versions of one message in different tones so you can compare them. A person makes the final choice and polishes it.
**Suggested tools**: Whichever of Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini you already use. If you want to save your tone-of-voice guidelines, Claude Projects is convenient.

<PromptBox label="Generate copy options">
You are a copywriter for a [industry] brand.
Product: [product name and one-line description]
Target audience: [age range, situation]
Goal: [e.g., clicks on a new-product announcement]
Requirements: Headlines under 10 words. Write 2 options each in 5 different tones (friendly, humorous, trustworthy, urgent, emotional). Don't use exaggerated claims (best, #1).
</PromptBox>

### 2. Build a social media content calendar

**What it can do**: Lay out a month of topics, formats (carousel, reels, text posts), and posting days in a table.
**Suggested tools**: A chat AI for planning and Canva AI for creating images. For the full process, follow [Create a full set of social content with Canva AI](/media/12-canva-sns/).

<PromptBox label="Content calendar">
Make a 4-week Instagram content calendar for [brand/channel description] as a table.
Columns: week, day, topic, format, key message, call to action
Requirements: 3 posts per week; 50% informational, 30% reviews and case studies, 20% promotional.
Events this month: [event or season]
</PromptBox>

### 3. Newsletter and blog drafts, and converting for each channel

**What it can do**: Split one long piece into a blog post, a newsletter, and short social posts. AI writes the draft; a person checks the facts and tone.
**Suggested tools**: A chat AI. For report-style writing, the approach is the same as in [Write and polish report and proposal drafts](/chat/04-write-reports/).

<PromptBox label="Convert for each channel">
Turn the blog post below into three versions.
1) Newsletter body (under 100 words, with button text at the end)
2) Instagram caption (5 hashtags)
3) LinkedIn post (professional tone, under 150 words)
Don't add any numbers or facts that aren't in the post.

[Paste the blog post]
</PromptBox>

### 4. Analyze customer voice and reviews

**What it can do**: Group reviews, open-ended survey answers, and inquiries to pull out the most common complaints and compliments.
**Suggested tools**: For Excel and CSV analysis, use the approach in [Let AI analyze your Excel and CSV data](/chat/03-data-analysis/).

<PromptBox label="Group the reviews">
Below are [number] customer reviews. Read them and summarize the following.
1) The top 5 themes of praise, with a representative quote for each
2) The top 5 themes of complaints, with a representative quote for each
3) 3 ideas we could use in our next campaign messaging
Leave out personal information such as customer names.

[Paste the reviews]
</PromptBox>

### 5. Draft a campaign performance report

**What it can do**: Give it a table of numbers and it organizes the key changes, hypotheses about causes, and next actions in a report format.
**Suggested tools**: A chat AI. Always check the calculations against the original data.

<PromptBox label="Performance report draft">
Below is last month's campaign performance table. Write a summary I can report to my manager.
Structure: one-line conclusion / 2 things that went well / 2 things that fell short / hypotheses about causes (label them as assumptions) / 3 actions for next month
Don't make up numbers that aren't in the table, and if a calculation is needed, show how you did it.

[Paste the table]
</PromptBox>

## What to watch out for

<Aside type="caution" title="Check these before you publish">
- **Copyright and commercial use**: Images, music, and text made by AI come with terms of use that vary by tool. Don't use output that resembles real people, characters, or other brands.
- **Facts and numbers**: AI can plausibly make up statistics, efficacy claims, and comparisons. Check sources yourself. Advertising language must also follow your company's review standards. The method is in [Can you trust AI answers?](/basics/04-fact-checking/).
- **Customer information**: When you paste in reviews or inquiries, remove names, contact details, and order numbers. Please read [Using AI while protecting personal and company information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/) first.
- **Auto-posting agents**: Before you use a tool that is given control of your accounts, check [How to use personal AI agents safely](/agents/05-agent-safety/).
</Aside>
