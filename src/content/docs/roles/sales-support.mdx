## Who this is for

This page suits people who spend the day talking and writing to customers, such as sales reps, account managers, and customer support staff.

- Replying to emails and inquiries takes up much of the day.
- Notes from customer meetings and calls need to be written up afterward.
- Proposals and follow-ups are always similar, yet each one takes time.

## Top 5 ways AI helps

### 1. Summarize email threads and draft replies

**What it can do**: Summarize a long thread and draft a reply in the tone you need.
**Suggested tools**: See [Organizing Your Inbox with AI: Gemini in Gmail, Copilot in Outlook, and Claude Connectors](/automation/38-email-ai/).

<PromptBox label="Reply draft">
Below is an email thread with a customer. Do the following.
1) Summarize the thread in 3 lines.
2) List what the customer is asking for.
3) Write a reply draft: polite but concise, within 5 sentences.
Don't promise dates or prices that aren't in the thread. Mark them as [to be confirmed].

[Paste thread]
</PromptBox>

### 2. Organize meeting notes and pull out to-dos

**What it can do**: Extract decisions, owners, and deadlines into a table.
**Suggested tools**: See [Summarizing meeting notes and long documents, and pulling out action items](/chat/02-summarize-documents/) and [Comparing Online Meeting AI](/automation/37-meeting-ai/).

<PromptBox label="Organize meeting notes">
Below are my notes from a customer meeting. Organize them in this format.
1) The meeting's purpose in one line
2) What the customer asked for
3) Action items table: task, owner, deadline
If an owner or deadline isn't in the notes, write "TBD" and don't make one up.

[Paste notes]
</PromptBox>

### 3. Draft proposals and presentations

**What it can do**: Set up an outline and write a draft, then turn it into slides.
**Suggested tools**: See [Drafting and Polishing Reports and Proposals](/chat/04-write-reports/) and [Make Presentations with AI](/media/25-ai-presentations/).

<PromptBox label="Outline a proposal">
I'm going to write a proposal on [offering] for [customer type].
Purpose: [what I want the customer to decide]
First, suggest a table of contents and a key sentence for each section. Once I confirm, we'll draft section by section. Don't include any numbers that aren't in the materials I give you.
Reference materials: [paste materials]
</PromptBox>

### 4. Catch up on team messages

**What it can do**: Summarize conversations and search for answers inside your work messenger.
**Suggested tools**: See [Adding AI to Your Work Messenger: Slack AI and Claude Tag](/automation/41-slack-ai/).

<PromptBox label="Catch up on a thread">
Below is a long team conversation. Summarize it in 5 lines, then list the decisions and the open questions separately.

[Paste conversation]
</PromptBox>

### 5. Automate routine follow-ups

**What it can do**: Connect email, sheets, and notifications so repeated steps run on their own.
**Suggested tools**: See [Automating Email → Sheet → Notification with Zapier/Make](/automation/18-zapier-make/) and [Connecting Gmail and Calendar to AI for a Morning Briefing](/automation/17-morning-briefing/).

<PromptBox label="Morning to-dos">
Look at today's schedule and the emails that have come in since yesterday, and organize the following.
1) The 3 things I must do today
2) Emails that need a reply, and their priority
Only read my email. Don't send or delete anything.
</PromptBox>

## Copy-ready prompts for sales and support

Copy a prompt, replace every [bracket] with your own details, and check the result before you use it. Don't paste personal or confidential data; see [Using AI While Protecting Personal and Company Information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).

### Follow-up emails

<PromptBox label="After a meeting">
Write a follow-up email after a meeting with [customer role, not name].
What we discussed: [3 bullet points]
Agreed next step: [action, owner, date]
Tone: professional and warm, under 150 words. Add 3 subject line options. Don't add commitments I didn't list.
</PromptBox>

Tip: More email tone options in [Write Better Emails and Messages with AI](/chat/42-emails-and-messages/).

<PromptBox label="Gentle nudge">
I sent [what] on [date] and haven't heard back. Write 2 short follow-up versions: one light and friendly, one more direct.
Goal: [get a reply / confirm a date / get a decision]. Don't sound pushy or guilt-trip them. Include one easy question they can answer in a few words.
</PromptBox>

Tip: Ask a follow-up to adjust the tone; see [Get Better Answers with Follow-Up Questions](/basics/15-follow-up-questions/).

<PromptBox label="Meeting notes to action list">
Turn the meeting notes below into: a 3-line summary, an action table (task, owner, deadline), and a short email I can send to attendees.
If an owner or date isn't in the notes, write "TBD". Names removed.

[Paste notes]
</PromptBox>

Tip: See [Summarizing Meeting Notes and Long Documents](/chat/02-summarize-documents/).

### Objection handling prep

<PromptBox label="Likely objections">
I sell [product or service] to [customer type]. List the 8 objections I am most likely to hear (price, timing, trust, switching cost, and so on).
For each, give a short, honest response and one question I can ask to understand the concern. Don't invent statistics or competitor facts.
</PromptBox>

Tip: Structure your request with [Writing Work Prompts](/chat/01-prompt-basics/).

<PromptBox label="Role-play practice">
Play a skeptical customer: [description]. I will pitch [offer]. Raise one objection at a time and wait for my answer.
After 5 rounds, give feedback: what worked, what sounded vague, and one thing to try next time.
</PromptBox>

Tip: Same practice approach as [Polishing Cover Letters and Resumes with AI, and Practicing Interviews](/chat/40-resume-interview/).

<PromptBox label="Call prep sheet">
Prepare me for a call with [company type or role]. What I know: [public facts only].
Give me: 5 questions to ask, 3 likely concerns, and a 2-sentence opening. Label anything you assumed as an assumption.
</PromptBox>

Tip: Check facts with [Can You Trust AI Answers? How to Fact-Check](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

### FAQ and support replies

<PromptBox label="Support reply">
Write a support reply to the message below. Be empathetic, give clear steps, and end by asking if it solved the issue.
Facts and steps I can confirm: [steps from our guide]
If the message needs something not in my facts, say we will check. No personal details.

[Paste message with names removed]
</PromptBox>

Tip: Build reusable replies with [Build a Customer FAQ and Reply Templates with AI](/automation/52-customer-faq/).

<PromptBox label="Turn tickets into FAQ">
Below are anonymized customer questions. Find the 5 most common topics and write an FAQ entry for each: a plain question and a short answer.
Use only the answers I provide: [approved answers]. Mark any entry that needs a human decision.

[Paste questions]
</PromptBox>

Tip: Template ideas in [Build Reusable Document Templates with AI](/automation/50-document-templates/).

<PromptBox label="Soften a hard no">
I need to tell a customer: [what we can't do or what went wrong].
Write a reply that is honest, doesn't over-apologize, explains the reason in one sentence, and offers this alternative: [alternative].
Give me one formal and one casual version.
</PromptBox>

Tip: Tone control is covered in [Write Better Emails and Messages with AI](/chat/42-emails-and-messages/).

## What to watch out for

<Aside type="caution" title="Before you paste in customer information">
- **Customer and company information**: Don't casually paste in customer lists, contracts, or personal details. Check your company's AI usage policy first, and mask names and contact details. See [Using AI while protecting personal and company information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).
- **Promises and numbers**: Check prices, dates, and terms in any draft against the original before sending. See [Can you trust AI answers?](/basics/04-fact-checking/).
- **Send and delete permissions**: Grant read access first, and decide carefully about send and delete permissions. See [How to use personal AI agents safely](/agents/05-agent-safety/).
- **Recordings**: Check consent and company rules before using meeting recording and summary features. See [Comparing Online Meeting AI](/automation/37-meeting-ai/).
</Aside>
