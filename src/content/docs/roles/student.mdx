<Summary>
- AI helps most when you use it not as a **tool that writes answers for you**, but as a **personal tutor who checks whether you've understood**.
- You can save a lot of time on studying, writing feedback, and job prep (cover letters and interviews).
- Whether you can use AI on assignments **differs by school and course.** Check the guidelines first, and disclose it if you used it.
- Don't put personal information such as your address, phone number, or national ID number in your resume or cover letter.
</Summary>

## Who this is for

This guide suits college students, high school students, and people preparing to get a job or change jobs.

- You find it hard to understand class material and don't have anyone good to ask.
- You don't know where to start with cover letters and interview prep.
- You worry that using AI might count as cheating.

## Top 5 ways AI helps

### 1. Get hard concepts explained in plain words

You can ask for a concept you don't understand to be explained again at your level. A chat AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) is enough.

<PromptBox>
Explain "[concept]" from [subject] at a [high school / college freshman] level. Give one everyday analogy and also tell me 2 points people commonly confuse. After explaining, ask me 3 questions so I can check whether I understood.
</PromptBox>

### 2. Have it quiz you and learn why you got things wrong

Having AI quiz you stays in memory much longer than reading passively. Write your own answer first, and then ask for the explanation.

<PromptBox>
Here is what I studied. Quiz me on this material, one question at a time. When I answer, tell me whether I was right, and if I was wrong, explain why and the correct concept. Start with easy questions first.

[What I studied]
</PromptBox>

### 3. Get feedback on your writing (instead of asking it to write for you)

If you have your structure and wording checked **after you write** an assignment or essay, your skills improve. Asking it to write the whole thing from scratch can become an academic integrity problem.

<PromptBox>
Below is something I wrote myself. Don't rewrite it for me. Instead, give me a numbered list of where the logic is weak, which sentences are hard to understand, and what could be improved. At the end, ask me 3 questions so I can revise it on my own.

[My writing]
</PromptBox>

### 4. Organize your cover letter and resume

If you list your experiences, it can help organize them into a structure that brings out your strengths. Write **only what you actually did**, and don't include invented achievements.

<PromptBox>
The job I'm applying for is [position], and the skills the company wants are [required skills]. Below are experiences I actually had. Organize each experience in the order situation, action, result, and tell me which skills it shows. Don't make up achievements or numbers I haven't mentioned.

[Experience notes]
</PromptBox>

### 5. Practice mock interviews

You can get likely questions, practice your answers, and receive feedback. If you do it by voice conversation on your phone, you also practice speaking ([Using AI on your phone](/basics/07-ai-on-phone/)).

<PromptBox>
You are an interviewer at [company/position]. Ask me the questions you'd ask an entry-level applicant, one at a time. When I answer, briefly give me 1 strength and 1 thing to improve, then move on to the next question. Do 5 questions in total, and give an overall assessment at the end.
</PromptBox>

## What to watch out for

<Aside type="caution" title="Academic integrity: check the rules first">
How much AI use is allowed differs by school, course, competition, and company hiring process. Check the assignment guidelines or the instructor's announcement first. Even where it's allowed, disclose that you used AI and how, and make sure the result reflects **your own understanding and your own thinking**. Submitting AI-written text as your own can be treated as cheating.
</Aside>

- **Fact-checking**: AI can invent papers or sources that don't exist. Before you cite something, find the original and check it yourself ([how to fact-check](/basics/04-fact-checking/)).
- **Personal information**: Remove your name, contact details, address, and national ID number from cover letters and resumes before you enter them ([privacy guide](/basics/05-privacy-safety/)).
- **Risk of copying**: Sentences AI creates may resemble existing writing. Don't use them as-is; rewrite them in your own words.
- **Don't over-rely on it**: Don't look at the answer first. Try solving it yourself and then check, so the skill actually stays with you.
