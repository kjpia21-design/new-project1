## What you'll be able to do

- Turn "I need a good vacuum" into a short list of criteria and a budget.
- Get a comparison table of candidates, with unknowns marked instead of guessed.
- Read review summaries critically and spot what a summary cannot tell you.
- Know which facts to re-check at the seller before you pay.
- Know what the official pages say about web search for Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini, and what is still unconfirmed.

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Criteria** | The things that decide your choice (budget, size, battery life, warranty). Fix them before comparing. |
| **Must-have vs. nice-to-have** | A must-have rules a product out if missing; a nice-to-have only breaks ties. |
| **Comparison table** | Candidates in rows, criteria in columns. Mark unknown cells as "unknown" rather than guessing. |
| **Web search** | A mode where the AI looks up current pages and shows sources. Names and switches differ by service (see below). |
| **Affiliate link / sponsored result** | A link where the site may earn money if you buy. Not necessarily bad, but it can bias a "best of" list. |
| **Re-check at the seller** | Opening the store or maker page yourself to confirm the current price, stock, specs, and return policy. |

## Step-by-step

<Steps>

1. **Decide what matters**

   Swap the bracketed parts for your own. For general prompt structure, see [Asking Well](/basics/03-asking-well/).

   <PromptBox label="Criteria prompt">
   I want to buy [product type] for [use, e.g. a small apartment / daily commute / a gift for a teenager]. Budget: [amount and currency]. Before suggesting anything, ask me up to 6 questions that would change the choice (size, brand preferences, features I need, where I will use it). Then summarize my must-haves and nice-to-haves in two short lists.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Find candidates, with sources**

   If your service has web search, turn it on or say so in the prompt. Ask for sources and for unknowns to be marked.

   <PromptBox label="Candidates prompt">
   Using my must-haves and budget, suggest 3 to 5 candidate [product type] models available in [country]. For each, give the maker's own product page or another source you used. If you are not sure a model exists or is sold in [country], say so. Do not invent model names, specs, or prices. Mark anything that may change as [CHECK AT SELLER].
   </PromptBox>

   Open the sources yourself. If a model name returns nothing at the maker's site, drop it.

3. **Build the comparison table**

   <PromptBox label="Comparison table prompt">
   Make a table with these candidates in rows and these columns: price range, size or weight, key specs that matter for my must-haves, warranty, and what people commonly complain about. Write "unknown" where you do not have a source. Then tell me which candidate is best for my must-haves and which is the best value, and why in two sentences each.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Read reviews critically**

   Paste a few reviews you copied yourself (leave out names and personal details), or ask the AI how to read them.

   <PromptBox label="Review check prompt">
   Here are reviews I copied for [model]: [pasted reviews]. Summarize the repeated praise and the repeated complaints. Point out reviews that look suspicious (very short, generic, all the same phrasing, or about a different product). Tell me what a review summary cannot tell me, such as long-term durability, and what to look for in the 1-star and 3-star reviews.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Test the trade-offs**

   <PromptBox label="Trade-off prompt">
   If I give up [nice-to-have] to save money, which candidate still meets all my must-haves? And if I raise the budget by [amount], what would I gain? Keep it to a short list and name any assumption you made.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Re-check at the seller before you pay**

   <PromptBox label="Final check prompt">
   Make a pre-purchase checklist for [model]: the exact model number to match, specs to confirm on the product page, what to check about the return window, warranty, delivery time, and the seller (official store or a third party). Add three questions to ask the seller if anything is unclear.
   </PromptBox>

   Then open the store yourself and go through the list. Compare the same model at one or two other sellers. If a price looks too good to be true, see [AI Scams and Deepfakes](/basics/10-ai-scams-deepfakes/).

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### The AI recommends a model I cannot find

- **Cause**: It may have mixed up names or described a model that is not sold in your country.
- **Fix**: Search the maker's site. Ask the AI for a source for each model, and drop any it cannot support.

### The price in the answer is different at the store

- **Cause**: Prices change, and a cited page may be old or from another country.
- **Fix**: Treat the store page as the truth. Use the AI for criteria and comparison, not for today's price.

### Every recommendation looks like an ad

- **Cause**: "Best of" lists can be sponsored or affiliate-driven.
- **Fix**: Ask for the maker's spec page and for complaints, not only praise. Compare more than one source.

### The review summary sounds too positive

- **Cause**: Reviews can be fake or paid, and a summary hides the details.
- **Fix**: Read the 1-star and 3-star reviews yourself and look for repeated, specific problems.

### I am not sure whether web search is on

- **Cause**: Switches and names differ by service and plan.
- **Fix**: Ask for sources in the prompt. If none are given, assume the answer is not current. See the table above and each service's help page.

## Practical tips

- **Fix the budget and must-haves first.** It stops the list from growing.
- **Ask for sources and unknowns.** "Unknown" is a better answer than a guess.
- **Use the AI for thinking, the seller for facts.** Price, stock, and returns come from the store.
- **Compare the same exact model** across sellers, including the model number.
- **Keep private data out.** No card numbers, passwords, or order details in chats.
- **Wait a day for big purchases.** Come back and re-run the final check.
