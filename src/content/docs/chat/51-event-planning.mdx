## What you'll be able to do

- Turn "I should organize something" into a clear event brief: purpose, date, headcount, budget, and constraints.
- Get a task checklist with owners and deadlines, and a budget table you can fill with real prices.
- Draft a run-of-day timeline with buffers, and a backup plan for rain, no-shows, or a late venue.
- Write an invitation and a reminder message you can edit and send yourself.
- List what to confirm with the venue and vendors, and do a short check the day before.

This tutorial is about **planning a gathering.** For trips, see [Plan Trips with AI](/chat/45-travel-planning/). For putting meeting times on a calendar, see [Plan Your Schedule and Meetings with AI](/automation/48-calendar-scheduling/). For choosing between two venues or dates, see [Think Through a Decision with AI](/chat/50-decision-making/).

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Event brief** | A few lines: why you are gathering, who, roughly when, where, and how much you can spend. |
| **Run of day** | A time-ordered list of what happens, from setup to cleanup, with owners. |
| **Budget table** | Each cost line with an estimate, and later the real quote and the amount paid. |
| **Buffer** | Spare time or money for things that run late or cost more than planned. |
| **Backup plan** | What you do if the weather, a no-show, or a venue problem changes things. |
| **Verification list** | The details you must confirm with the venue or vendor before you pay. |
| **Official source** | The venue's, vendor's, or organizer's own site, or a direct reply from them. |

<Aside type="note" title="What we could not confirm">
The pages we read do not say how **fresh or complete** the venue hours, prices, or availability that any assistant gives are. They also do not say that any assistant can **book a venue, send invitations, collect RSVPs, or take payments** for you, and we did not test any such feature. Whether your service can connect to your email or calendar depends on your plan and settings, so check your own app. We did not verify any local permit, venue, insurance, food-safety, or alcohol rules for any place.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Guests' details are other people's data">
A guest list can contain phone numbers, emails, addresses, dietary needs, and health information. Keep the real list in your own spreadsheet, and give the AI only what the plan needs, such as headcount, a count of vegetarian guests, or "one guest uses a wheelchair" without names. Do not paste payment details or deposit receipts. Ask people before sharing their details. See [Using AI While Protecting Personal and Company Information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Write the event brief**

   Start with the facts you know, and let the AI ask about the rest.

   <PromptBox label="Event brief prompt">
   I am planning [type of event] for about [number] people on or around [date]. Purpose: [for example a birthday, a farewell, a team lunch]. Budget: [amount in your currency], which must not be exceeded. Place: [city or area; indoors or outdoors]. Constraints: [for example kids attending, a vegetarian guest, limited parking]. Ask me up to six questions that would change the plan, and do not make a plan yet.
   </PromptBox>

   Answer in your own words. If you find you do not know the purpose or the budget, that is the first thing to settle.

2. **Brainstorm formats and themes**

   <PromptBox label="Brainstorm prompt">
   Based on the brief, suggest five different ways to run this event, each in two lines: the format, roughly what it would cost relative to my budget, and one risk. Include one low-effort option. Do not name specific venues or prices as facts.
   </PromptBox>

   Pick one, or combine two. The AI's ideas are a starting point; the event should still sound like you.

3. **Build the checklist and budget table**

   <PromptBox label="Checklist and budget prompt">
   For the format I chose, make two tables. First, a task checklist with columns: task, who, deadline counted backward from the event date, and status. Second, a budget table with columns: item, estimate, real quote, amount paid. Keep the total within my budget and include a buffer line. Write "guess" next to any estimate that is not based on figures I gave you, and keep my dates and budget figures unchanged.
   </PromptBox>

   Fill in the real quote and amount paid yourself as you go. The estimates are placeholders until you have an official price. This is planning help, not financial advice.

4. **Draft the run of day and a backup plan**

   <PromptBox label="Timeline prompt">
   Write a run of day from setup to cleanup, with start times, what happens, and who is responsible. Add a 15-minute buffer where things commonly run late. Then give me a backup plan for each of these: bad weather, fewer guests than expected, a late delivery, and the venue not being ready.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Write the invitation and a reminder**

   <PromptBox label="Invitation prompt">
   Draft a friendly invitation message for this event. Include placeholders for the date, time, place, and how to reply by [date]. Ask guests to tell me about dietary needs and access needs privately. Keep it under 120 words. Also draft a short reminder to send two days before. Prepare both as drafts only.
   </PromptBox>

   Replace the placeholders, edit the tone, and send from your own account. Do not rely on the AI to send or track replies.

6. **Make the verification list, then check on official sites**

   <PromptBox label="Verification prompt">
   From the plan above, list every detail I must confirm before paying or sending invitations: venue availability and capacity, opening hours, prices and deposits, cancellation terms, rules on outside food or drinks, noise or end-time rules, accessibility, parking, and any permit or insurance needed. For each, name the type of official source to check, such as the venue's site or a direct reply from the venue. Do not state any of these as facts.
   </PromptBox>

   Work through the list on the venue's or vendor's own pages, or ask them directly, and note the date you checked.

7. **Do a day-before check**

   <PromptBox label="Day-before check prompt">
   Here is my final plan with the date, headcount, and run of day. Do a final check: find tasks without an owner or deadline, times that overlap, items missing from the shopping or packing list, and information you could not verify. Then give me a short checklist to go through the day before and a message I can send to helpers.
   </PromptBox>

   Review the result yourself before relying on it.

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### The plan ignores my budget

- **Cause**: The budget was not stated as a hard limit, or the AI had no cost information for your area.
- **Fix**: Say the budget must not be exceeded and ask it to keep the figures unchanged. Replace its estimates with real quotes.

### The venue details look specific but I cannot find them

- **Cause**: AI can produce confident-looking names, hours, and prices that are wrong or out of date.
- **Fix**: Ask it not to name venues or prices as facts, or ask for web search and sources if your service offers it. Open the cited page and confirm on the venue's own site or by asking them.

### The invitation sounds like a template

- **Cause**: It did not know your tone or your group.
- **Fix**: Add two or three sentences you have written before, and ask it to match that voice. Edit before sending.

### I do not want to paste my guest list

- **Cause**: The list has other people's details.
- **Fix**: Give counts and needs without names, and keep the real list in your own file. Ask permission before sharing anyone's details.

### The timeline is too tight

- **Cause**: The AI may not know how long setup, travel, or serving really takes for your group.
- **Fix**: Tell it your own time estimates, ask for buffers, and walk through the timeline once yourself.

### The event involves alcohol, many people, or children

- **Cause**: Rules, insurance, and safety needs vary by place and venue.
- **Fix**: Ask the venue and your local authority what applies. The AI can help you list questions, but it cannot tell you what is required where you live.

## Practical tips

- **State the budget as a limit.** Say it must not be exceeded, and keep a buffer line.
- **Ask the AI to mark guesses.** A price with no source is a prompt for you to check.
- **Keep drafts as drafts.** Edit and send messages yourself.
- **Keep guest data out of the chat.** Counts and needs are usually enough.
- **Confirm with the venue.** Official sites and direct replies beat any AI summary.
- **Match effort to size.** A small dinner needs a quick list; a larger event needs the full set of tables and a backup plan.
