<Summary>
- Don't just ask for a summary. Give it a structure: **decisions, action items (owner and deadline), and open issues.**
- If an owner or deadline isn't in the document, tell the AI to **write "needs confirmation" instead of making one up.**
- Ask for **quoted supporting sentences** with the results, and check them against the source.
- For Claude, chat uploads are limited to 500MB per file, 20 files per chat, and PDFs up to 1,000 pages (as of 2026-10-10). - Splitting a long chat into a new one also saves usage.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do

- Turn meeting notes into a summary for your team lead plus an action-item table.
- Pull only the necessary decisions and risks out of a document that's dozens of pages long.
- Verify that a summary matches the source by checking the supporting sentences.
- Carry the summary straight into a follow-up email or announcement.

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Context window** | The amount of text the AI can see at once. The conversation, uploaded material, and answers all fit inside it. |
| **Structured summary** | Instead of a prose summary, you pick the categories (decisions, action items, risks) to pull out. |
| **Source quote** | Getting the sentence from the original along with each item in the summary, so you can see where it came from. |
| **Project** | Claude's workspace for reusing the same material and instructions across multiple chats. |

Claude's context window varies by model. The official help pages say the latest models on paid plans support up to 1M tokens, and others support 500K or 200K tokens (as of 2026-10-10). Part of it is reserved for the response, so the amount you can actually use is a little smaller.

<Aside type="note" title="Uploaded files and limits">
The official help pages don't explain how uploaded files count toward context or usage. It's safer to assume that bigger files use more of your allowance and to use them sparingly.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Prepare and upload your material**

   You can paste meeting notes straight into the chat box. Upload documents as attachments (in Claude, the "+" at the lower left of the input box).

   Claude accepts PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML, ODT, RTF, EPUB, JSON, XLSX, and images (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP). The limits are 20 files per chat, 500MB per file, and 1,000 pages per PDF. If a PDF is 100 pages or fewer, Claude analyzes both text and visual elements; for 101 to 1,000 pages, it analyzes text only. Anything over 1,000 pages is rejected with an "Uploaded file is too large" error. For documents like DOCX, only the text is extracted, so images inside can't be read.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="A chat input box with a file attached" officialUrl="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-upload-files-to-claude" />

   <Aside type="caution" title="Before you upload">
   Delete or pseudonymize information that shouldn't leave your organization, such as customer names, contact details, and contract amounts. Check your company's security guidelines first, too.
   </Aside>

2. **Ask for a summary with a set structure**

   Put long material on top and your request below it. That's the approach the official guide describes.

   <PromptBox>
   [Paste meeting notes]

   Organize the meeting notes above for a report to my team lead. Follow this format:
   1) One-line conclusion
   2) Decisions (numbered list)
   3) Action items: a table with the columns Action / Owner / Deadline / Supporting sentence
   4) Open issues and what to decide at the next meeting
   If an owner or deadline isn't in the notes, don't make one up. Write "needs confirmation."
   </PromptBox>

3. **Check the supporting sentences**

   A summary can look convincing and still differ from the source. Pick a few of the "supporting sentences" in the table and look for them in the original yourself. If you have doubts, ask like this.

   <PromptBox>
   If any items in the table you just made aren't directly supported by the source and you inferred them, mark them separately. For each inferred item, add a one-line reason.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Pull out only what you need from a long document**

   When you give several documents at once, have the AI keep them apart by name to reduce confusion. The official guide describes a structure that separates documents with `<document>` tags.

   <PromptBox>
   <documents>
   <document index="1">
   <source>Contract draft v1</source>
   <document_content>[Content]</document_content>
   </document>
   <document index="2">
   <source>Revised draft v2</source>
   <document_content>[Content]</document_content>
   </document>
   </documents>

   Compare the two documents and list the clauses that changed in a table. Use the columns Clause / v1 / v2 / Reason for change, and briefly quote the original sentence in each cell.
   </PromptBox>

   <Aside type="caution" title="Legal and financial documents">
   For documents like contracts, treat an AI summary as a reference only. For important decisions, go back to the original and check with the person in charge.
   </Aside>

5. **Carry it into the next document**

   Keep the table you just made and use it to build the next deliverable.

   <PromptBox>
   Based on the action-item table above, write a follow-up email to send to the meeting attendees. Group the action items by owner, and at the end, ask for replies on any items left as "needs confirmation." Keep it under 150 words.
   </PromptBox>

   If you like, you can also get the result as a file. With the code execution and file creation feature turned on, Claude can create .docx, .xlsx, .pptx, and PDF files, up to 30MB per file. Where to turn it on is covered in [Tutorial 3](/chat/03-data-analysis/).

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### The summary is too short or misses the key points

- **Cause**: You may not have told the AI who the reader is and what the goal is.
- **Fix**: Name the reader, like "for my team lead" or "to explain to a new hire," and specify the items that must be included.

### Owners or deadlines show up that don't exist

- **Cause**: The AI may have filled in blanks with something plausible.
- **Fix**: Add "if missing, don't make it up; write needs confirmation" to your request, and add a supporting-sentence column.

### The end of a long PDF doesn't seem to be reflected

- **Cause**: The chat may have gotten long, or the PDF may be over 100 pages so only the text was analyzed. Tables and figures can be missed if they aren't extracted as text.
- **Fix**: Upload the document split by chapter, or paste the parts you need separately.

### The upload is rejected

- **Cause**: The format may be unsupported or over the limit. Audio files aren't on the supported formats list.
- **Fix**: Convert to a supported format. XLSX can only be uploaded with code execution and file creation turned on. For recordings, turn them into text first (covered in [Getting recordings as text and organizing them](/media/14-transcribe-recordings/)).

### Answers slow down or the limit drops fast as the chat gets long

- **Cause**: Long chats use more of your allowance.
- **Fix**: Start a new chat when the topic changes.

## Practical tips

- **Summarize within 10 minutes of the meeting.** Your memory is still fresh, so you can fill in the "needs confirmation" items right away.
- **If you reuse the same format, save it in a project.** In a Claude project, you put the summary format in the instructions and reuse it in every chat. On paid plans, capacity expands when there's a lot of knowledge, because only the relevant parts are retrieved. The official help pages advise keeping project instructions short and deleting files you don't use ([C5](/claude/c05-projects/)).
- **Move to a new chat when the topic changes.** Long chats use more of your allowance, and earlier content may get summarized.
- **Have a person do the final check.** A summary is a draft. Before you report it, look over the numbers, dates, and owners once more.
