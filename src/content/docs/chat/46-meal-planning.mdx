<Summary>
- AI is a handy **menu brainstormer**: give it what is in your fridge, your budget, and your household, and it drafts a week of meals, a recipe, and a shopping list in minutes.
- Five jobs to hand over: **say what you have, set the rules, draft the week, build the shopping list, and fit the budget.** Below are 6 prompts you can copy.
- **This is not medical or nutrition advice.** For allergies, intolerances, pregnancy, diabetes, kidney disease, or any other health condition, ask a doctor or a registered dietitian. Do not rely on an AI's ingredient or calorie claims for safety.
- Photo upload is described on each service's official help page: Claude takes JPEG, PNG, GIF, and WebP images; Gemini lets you add images in the same prompt (up to 10 files, subject to availability); ChatGPT lets you attach, paste, or drag an image into the web composer. (as of 2026-11-02)
- We **could not confirm** in the docs how accurately any assistant recognizes food in a photo, or how accurate its calorie, nutrient, or price figures are. Treat those as estimates.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do

- Photograph or list what you have and get recipe ideas that use it.
- Get a week of meals that fits your household, time, and taste.
- Turn the menu into a shopping list grouped by store section, with what you already have removed.
- Fit a menu to a budget, with your own prices marked as the real numbers.
- Know where AI is a draft helper and where you need food-safety rules or a professional.

This tutorial is about **planning meals**. For how to ask well, see [Asking Well: The Basics Worth Remembering](/basics/03-asking-well/). For checking claims before you rely on them, see [Fact-checking](/basics/04-fact-checking/). For tracking what you spend, see [Organize household budgets and receipts with AI](/automation/46-budget-sheet/).

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Constraints** | The fixed facts: number of people, diet rules, allergies, budget, cooking time, equipment (oven, rice cooker, one pan). |
| **Pantry list** | What you already have. Giving it to the AI cuts waste and the shopping list. |
| **Weekly menu** | A day-by-day plan for meals, ideally reusing ingredients across days. |
| **Shopping list** | Ingredients you still need, grouped (produce, dairy, dry goods) so the trip is quick. |
| **Estimate** | A number the AI gives from general knowledge (price, calories, time). It is not measured or verified. |
| **Verify tag** | A marker like [VERIFY] you ask the AI to add to anything that matters for safety or cost. |

<Steps>

1. **Say what you have and set the rules**

   Take a photo with good light, or type the list. Put the rules in the prompt in your own words. Swap the bracketed parts for your own. For general prompt structure, see [Writing prompts](/chat/01-prompt-basics/).

   <PromptBox label="Constraints prompt">
   I am planning meals for [number] people. Diet rules: [vegetarian / no pork / none]. Allergies or conditions I have told you about: [list, or none]. Weekly food budget: [amount and currency]. Time to cook on weekdays: [30 minutes]. Equipment: [stove, oven, rice cooker]. Before you plan, ask me up to 5 questions that would most change your suggestions. Don't plan yet.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Turn a fridge photo into ideas**

   Attach the photo, then ask for a list first, so you can fix mistakes before any recipe is written.

   <PromptBox label="Fridge photo prompt">
   Here is a photo of my fridge and pantry. First, list the foods you can see and mark any you are unsure about with a question mark. Don't guess quantities. Then wait for me to correct the list. After that, suggest 5 dinners that mostly use these items, each in one line.
   </PromptBox>

   Correct what the AI got wrong. If a photo is blurry or crowded, a typed list works better. We did not verify how accurately any assistant reads food in photos.

3. **Draft the weekly menu**

   <PromptBox label="Weekly menu prompt">
   Using my corrected list and constraints, draft a 7-day dinner menu (and lunches that use leftovers). Reuse ingredients across days to reduce waste. Put the quickest meals on busy days. Put a [VERIFY] tag next to any step involving allergens, raw meat or fish, reheating, or storage times, and don't state food-safety rules as facts.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Get one recipe at a time**

   <PromptBox label="Recipe prompt">
   Write the recipe for [dish] for [number] servings. Give ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, and total time. Tell me which ingredients can be swapped and what the swap changes. Mark anything about allergens or doneness temperature with [VERIFY].
   </PromptBox>

   Cook it once and tell the AI what to change ("too salty", "took 50 minutes"). Ask it to rewrite the recipe with your notes.

5. **Build the shopping list**

   <PromptBox label="Shopping list prompt">
   From this week's menu, make a shopping list. Remove what is already in my pantry list. Group by store section (produce, meat and fish, dairy, dry goods, frozen). Merge duplicates and show total amounts. Add a short "optional" section for nice-to-haves.
   </PromptBox>

6. **Fit the budget**

   <PromptBox label="Budget prompt">
   My weekly food budget is [amount]. I will paste the prices I see at my store: [item: price, ...]. Use my prices for those items. For anything missing, don't invent a price; mark it "price needed". Add up the total, then suggest the 3 swaps that cut the cost the most while keeping the meals similar.
   </PromptBox>

   Prices the AI states without a source are guesses. Use the real prices from your store or its app. This is planning help, not financial advice.

</Steps>

## Quick reference

| What you want | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| Ideas from what you have | Fridge photo prompt: list foods first, correct, then suggest |
| Less food waste | Pantry list plus "reuse ingredients across days" |
| A quick weeknight plan | Set cooking time and equipment in the constraints prompt |
| A short shopping trip | Shopping list prompt: remove what you have, group by section |
| A realistic budget | Paste your own prices, mark missing ones "price needed" |
| Allergy or health question | Ask a doctor or registered dietitian, not the AI |
| Safer answers | Ask for [VERIFY] tags, then check labels and official food-safety guidance |

## Common sticking points

### The AI misread my fridge photo

- **Cause**: Blurry, crowded, or poorly lit photos are hard to read, and similar-looking items get mixed up.
- **Fix**: Ask for the list first and correct it. Use a clearer photo, or type the list instead.

### The menu uses ingredients I dislike or can't eat

- **Cause**: The constraints were missing or too vague.
- **Fix**: Add a "never use" list and repeat allergies in every new chat. Check labels yourself either way.

### The calorie or nutrient numbers look too precise

- **Cause**: The AI is giving general estimates, and the help pages we read do not describe a verified nutrition feature.
- **Fix**: Treat the numbers as rough. For medical or weight-related goals, ask a doctor or registered dietitian.

### The shopping list is too long or duplicates items

- **Cause**: The AI didn't know what you already have, or the menu uses many different ingredients.
- **Fix**: Give the pantry list and ask to "merge duplicates and prefer ingredients used in at least two meals".

### The prices are way off

- **Cause**: The AI doesn't know your store, city, or this week's sales.
- **Fix**: Paste your own prices and ask it to use only those. Mark missing ones "price needed".

## Practical tips

- **Start from what you have.** It is the quickest way to cut waste and cost.
- **Repeat allergies in every new chat.** Don't assume the AI remembers.
- **Cook it once, then tell the AI.** Feedback makes the next recipe better.
- **Plan a leftover night.** Ask for a menu with one "use it up" meal.
- **Keep the photo plain.** Frame the shelves, not faces or mail.
- **Ask a person for high-stakes diets.** A doctor or dietitian can do what an AI cannot.
