<Summary>
- An AI assistant can act like a **patient tutor**: it explains at your level, asks you questions, gives practice, and answers "why" as many times as you like. It can also be wrong, so you stay in charge of checking.
- Five habits: **state your goal and level, ask for a short plan, learn by explaining back, get quizzed, and verify with a trusted source.** Below are 6 prompts you can copy.
- Learning modes exist but differ by service. Anthropic describes a "Learning mode" in Claude for Education that guides with questions instead of giving answers (announced 2025-04-02). ## What you'll be able to do

- Turn a vague goal ("I want to understand investing basics, or statistics, or a language") into a small plan.
- Make the AI teach by asking you questions instead of only handing over answers.
- Practice with quizzes, flashcards, and explain-it-back checks.
- Spot when an explanation may be wrong and check it against a reliable source.
- Know what the official pages say about learning modes for Claude and Gemini, and what is still unconfirmed.

This tutorial is about **learning a new subject**. For how to ask well, see [Asking Well: The Basics Worth Remembering](/basics/03-asking-well/). For checking answers, see [Can You Trust AI Answers? ## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Tutor mode** | Asking the AI to teach step by step and ask you questions, rather than giving the whole answer at once. |
| **Learning mode / Guided Learning** | A feature some services offer for this style of teaching. Names, places, and availability differ by service (see below). |
| **Socratic questioning** | Teaching by asking questions ("What evidence supports your conclusion?") so you work out the answer yourself. |
| **Explain it back** | You explain the idea in your own words and the AI points out gaps. A quick test of whether you understood. |
| **Retrieval practice** | Trying to recall something from memory (quiz, flashcard) before looking at the answer. |
| **Verify** | Checking an explanation against a textbook, official source, or teacher before you rely on it. |

<Aside type="caution" title="Wrong explanations and your own rules">
An AI tutor can state wrong facts with confidence, mix up steps in math, or invent sources. For anything that matters (exams, medicine, law, money, safety), check with a textbook, an official source, or a teacher. Schools and employers may have rules on using AI for assignments; Google's help page also says to check your school's policy. Do not paste private information such as grades, ID numbers, or other people's details. ## Step-by-step

<Steps>

1. **Set your goal and level**

   Tell the AI what you want and what you already know. Swap the bracketed parts for your own. For general prompt structure, see [Writing prompts](/chat/01-prompt-basics/).

   <PromptBox label="Goal and level prompt">
   I want to learn [subject] so that I can [goal, e.g. read a financial statement / pass an intro exam / follow a talk]. My level: [complete beginner / know the basics of X]. I have [3 hours a week] for [4 weeks]. Before you teach anything, ask me up to 5 questions about my background and how I like to learn. Don't start the lesson yet.
   </PromptBox>

2. **Ask for a short plan**

   <PromptBox label="Learning plan prompt">
   Based on my answers, outline a 4-week plan for [subject]: each week's topic, 3 key ideas, and one small practice task. Keep it to what a beginner needs for my goal, and name what you are leaving out. Tell me which parts are well-established and which are debated or change over time.
   </PromptBox>

   Trim the plan to fit your time. If your service has a learning mode (see the table above), you can switch it on here; otherwise the next step gives the same effect.

3. **Learn by being asked, not told**

   <PromptBox label="Tutor mode prompt">
   Act as my tutor for week 1 of [subject]. Teach one small idea at a time. After each idea, ask me one question to check my understanding and wait for my answer. Don't give the answer until I try. If I'm wrong, give a hint first, then explain. Use simple words and one everyday example per idea.
   </PromptBox>

   Answer in your own words, even if you are unsure. A wrong attempt tells the tutor (and you) where the gap is.

4. **Explain it back**

   <PromptBox label="Explain-back prompt">
   Here is my explanation of [concept] in my own words: [your explanation]. Tell me what is correct, what is missing, and what is wrong. Then ask me one harder follow-up question.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Get quizzed and revisit**

   <PromptBox label="Quiz prompt">
   Quiz me on what we covered so far: 8 questions, mixed multiple choice and short answer, one at a time. Don't show the answer until I reply. After the quiz, list the topics I missed and make 5 flashcards (question on one side, answer on the other) for them. Tomorrow I will ask you to quiz me again on the missed topics only.
   </PromptBox>

   Gemini's help page also describes asking for practice questions with feedback and hints. Spaced repetition, meaning quizzing yourself again after a day or a week, is something you can ask for in any service.

6. **Verify and mark what to double-check**

   <PromptBox label="Verify prompt">
   List the 5 most important facts, formulas, or rules you taught me today. For each, say how confident you are, and tell me what kind of source I should check it against (textbook chapter, official standard, teacher). Mark with [VERIFY] anything that depends on a country, a date, or a recent change. Don't invent references or page numbers.
   </PromptBox>

   Then check them yourself. See [Can You Trust AI Answers? How to Fact-Check](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

</Steps>

## Quick reference

| What you want | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| A starting plan | Goal and level prompt, then the learning plan prompt |
| To actually understand, not just read | Tutor mode prompt: one idea, one question, wait for my answer |
| To test your understanding | Explain-back prompt |
| To remember it later | Quiz prompt with flashcards for the topics you missed |
| To lower the risk of wrong facts | Verify prompt, then check a textbook or official source |
| A built-in learning mode | Gemini: Add Files, More tools, Guided Learning (web). Claude: Learning mode is described for Claude for Education; ChatGPT: not confirmed |

## Common sticking points

### The AI gives the whole answer right away

- **Cause**: By default, assistants often try to answer fully.
- **Fix**: Use the tutor mode prompt: "one idea, one question, don't give the answer until I try." Or use a learning mode if your service has one.

### The explanation is too hard or too easy

- **Cause**: The AI guessed your level.
- **Fix**: State your level and ask for "explain like I'm new to this" or "go one level deeper". Ask for a different everyday example.

### I think the explanation is wrong

- **Cause**: AI can state wrong facts confidently, especially on recent, local, or very specific topics.
- **Fix**: Ask how sure it is and what to check, then check a textbook or official source. See [Can You Trust AI Answers? How to Fact-Check](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

### I forget everything the next day

- **Cause**: Reading an explanation is not the same as recalling it.
- **Fix**: Use the quiz prompt, wait a day, and quiz yourself again on the missed topics.

### I cannot find the learning mode

- **Cause**: Names, places, and availability differ, and some features roll out gradually or only for certain accounts.
- **Fix**: Check the service's current help page. Meanwhile, the tutor mode prompt works anywhere.

## Practical tips

- **Start small.** One topic per session beats a huge plan you drop.
- **Try before you peek.** Answer first, then read the explanation.
- **Teach it back.** Explaining is the fastest test of understanding.
- **Use a real source too.** Pair the AI with a textbook, course, or teacher.
- **Keep a running list** of what you missed and quiz on it again.
- **Respect the rules.** Use AI to learn, not to do work you are meant to do yourself.
