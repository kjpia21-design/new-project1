<Summary>
- AI is a good **first-draft planner**: it can turn a rough idea into a day-by-day itinerary, a budget table, and a packing list in minutes. It is not a source of truth for anything that changes.
- Five jobs to hand over: **set the constraints, draft the itinerary, build a budget, list what to bring, and make a list of things to verify.** Below are 6 prompts you can copy.
- **Opening hours, prices, transport schedules, and visa or entry rules change.** Treat every such detail the AI gives you as unverified until you check it on the official site of the place, operator, or government. (as of 2026-11-02)
- The official pages confirm that **Claude** can search the web with citations (and may use your approximate location for localized results), that **Gemini** automatically uses public information from Google Maps, Flights, and Hotels when Keep Activity is on, and that **ChatGPT** has a first-party web search tool whose results should be treated as untrusted input.
- We **could not confirm** in the docs how current or complete any assistant's hours, prices, or booking data is. Do not enter payment or passport details into an AI chat.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do

- Describe a trip in a way that lets the AI give useful, realistic suggestions.
- Get a day-by-day itinerary that fits your pace, budget, and group.
- Build a budget table and a packing list you can adjust.
- Turn the AI's claims into a short checklist of things to confirm on official sites.
- Know what web search and map features the official pages describe, and what they do not.

This tutorial is about **planning**. To use AI search more generally, see [Using AI search well](/chat/22-ai-search/). For checking facts before you rely on them, see [Fact-checking](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

## Concepts first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Constraints** | The fixed facts of your trip: dates, budget, group, mobility needs, must-sees. The more you give, the less the AI guesses. |
| **Itinerary** | A day-by-day plan with places and rough timing. Treat it as a draft, not a booking. |
| **Web search** | A feature where the AI looks up current pages and cites them. Without it, the AI answers from what it learned in training, which may be out of date. |
| **Citation** | A link to the page a statement came from. You open it to confirm the claim. |
| **Official source** | The website of the attraction, airline, rail operator, hotel, or the government office that sets the rule. |
| **Verification list** | A short list of the details you must confirm before you pay or travel. |

<Aside type="note" title="What we couldn't confirm">
The official pages we read do not say how **fresh or complete** the opening hours, prices, availability, or transport schedules shown by any assistant are. They also do not say that any assistant can **make bookings or payments** for you in the ways described here, and we did not test any booking feature. Gemini's Connected Apps page mentions actions such as booking reservations for discovered experiences, but which apps and countries support it varies. Check the Connected Apps settings in your own app. We did not verify visa or entry rules for any country.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Keep sensitive details out">
Do not type passport numbers, payment card numbers, booking confirmation codes, or your home address into an AI chat. Share only what the plan needs, such as dates and a rough budget. If you plan with a group, ask before sharing other people's details. For privacy settings and what to keep out of chats, see [Using AI while keeping personal and company information safe](/basics/05-privacy-safety/). Also watch for fake booking sites and too-good-to-be-true offers, see [AI scams and deepfakes](/basics/10-ai-scams-deepfakes/).
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. **Set the constraints first**

   Write down where, when, who, and how much. Then say what kind of trip you want. The AI can only fit what it knows. Swap the bracketed parts below for your own details. For general prompt structure, see [Asking questions well](/basics/03-asking-well/) and [Writing prompts](/chat/01-prompt-basics/).

   <PromptBox label="Constraints prompt">
   I am planning a trip. Destination: [city or region]. Dates: [approx. dates, number of days]. Travelers: [2 adults, 1 child aged 7]. Total budget excluding flights: [amount and currency]. Pace: [relaxed, 2 main activities per day]. Needs: [no long walks, vegetarian]. Must-sees: [list]. Before you plan, ask me up to 5 questions that would most change your suggestions. Don't plan yet.
   </PromptBox>

   Answer the questions, then move on. This step stops the AI from guessing the parts you care about most.

2. **Draft a day-by-day itinerary**

   <PromptBox label="Itinerary prompt">
   Using my answers, draft a day-by-day itinerary. For each day give: morning, afternoon, evening, and the rough travel time between places. Group nearby places together. Add one rainy-day alternative per day. Do not state opening hours, prices, or booking requirements as facts. Instead, put a [VERIFY] tag next to anything I should check on an official site.
   </PromptBox>

   If you have web search on, you can add "Search the web and cite your sources." In Claude, the web search page says you can include "Search the web" in your prompt to make sure it searches. Open the cited links yourself.

   <FlowSteps title="Checking the AI's search is on" officialUrl="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10684626-enable-and-use-web-search" steps={[
     { where: 'In the app (Claude / Gemini / ChatGPT)', action: 'Ask for current information or sources, for example "Search the web for current opening hours and cite your sources"', result: 'Look for citations or a search indicator in the reply' },
     { where: 'Claude, if there is a toggle', action: 'Click the + button in the lower left of the chat and look for Web search', result: 'A checkmark means it is on. With the new Claude experience there is no toggle' },
     { where: 'Gemini', action: 'Open Customize, then Connectors, to see the Connected Apps available to you', result: 'The list varies by app, device, and country' },
     { where: 'Work or school account', action: 'Ask your admin if search or apps are off', result: 'Workspace settings can limit availability' },
   ]} />

3. **Tune it for your group**

   Ask for changes in small steps instead of starting over.

   <PromptBox label="Adjust prompt">
   Revise the itinerary: reduce each day to at most [2] main stops, swap [place] for something quieter, and add a long lunch break on days [2 and 4]. Keep the [VERIFY] tags. Then tell me the 3 days that look the most rushed.
   </PromptBox>

4. **Build a budget table**

   <PromptBox label="Budget prompt">
   Make a budget table for this trip with these rows: lodging, local transport, food, activities, tickets, shopping, and a 10% buffer. I will give you the numbers I already know: [lodging per night, any fixed tickets]. For everything else, give a low and high estimate and mark it "estimate, verify." Show the totals for both, and tell me which two lines are most likely to go over.
   </PromptBox>

   The AI's numbers for cost are guesses unless it cites a page. Replace them with real prices from the official sites or your booking pages. This is planning help, not financial advice.

5. **Make a packing list**

   <PromptBox label="Packing list prompt">
   Make a packing list for [number] days in [destination] in [month], for [travelers]. Group it into documents, clothing, health, electronics, and extras. Mark anything that depends on rules (for example, medicines, power adapters, batteries) with [VERIFY], and don't state those rules as facts.
   </PromptBox>

   Check weather from a weather service close to your departure date.

6. **Turn the plan into a verification list**

   This is the most important step.

   <PromptBox label="Verification list prompt">
   From the itinerary and budget above, list every detail I must confirm before booking or leaving: opening hours and closed days, ticket prices and whether tickets must be reserved, transport schedules and passes, entry or visa requirements for my nationality, vaccination or health rules, and local rules for kids or medicines. For each, name the type of official source to check (for example, the attraction's site, the rail operator, the embassy or immigration office).
   </PromptBox>

   Work through the list on official sites. Update the plan with what you find, and note the date you checked.

</Steps>

## Quick reference

| What you want | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| Better suggestions | Constraints prompt: dates, group, budget, pace, needs |
| A realistic schedule | Itinerary prompt: nearby places grouped, rainy-day alternative |
| A budget you can trust | Give your known numbers, mark the rest as estimates |
| Fewer surprises | Ask for [VERIFY] tags, then build a verification list |
| Current information | Ask the AI to search the web and cite sources, then open the links |
| Opening hours and prices | Re-check on the official site of the place |
| Visa and entry rules | Re-check with the government of the destination, not the AI |
| Worried about limits | Claude web search counts toward your usage limits. See [Claude plans and limits](/claude/c02-plans-limits/) |

## Common sticking points

### The itinerary looks great but is impossible to follow

- **Cause**: The prompt didn't include your pace, transport, or how far the places are from each other.
- **Fix**: Add "group nearby places, show travel time between stops, and keep to [2] main stops per day." Compare the route on a map yourself.

### The AI gave an opening time or price that turned out to be wrong

- **Cause**: The AI may rely on training data or an old page, and hours and prices change.
- **Fix**: Ask for [VERIFY] tags and confirm on the official site. Treat any figure without a citation as a guess, and note that cited pages can also be out of date.

### The visa or entry advice sounds sure but I can't confirm it

- **Cause**: Rules depend on your nationality, purpose, and the date, and they can change.
- **Fix**: Use the AI only to list the questions to ask. Confirm the rule with the destination government's official site or embassy. We did not verify entry rules for any country.

### The AI keeps suggesting only famous places

- **Cause**: Without a preference, the AI leans on the most common suggestions.
- **Fix**: Say what you want to avoid ("no big tour groups") and what you enjoy ("local markets, walking"), and ask for two lesser-known options per day.

### I'm not sure whether a web search was used

- **Cause**: Some apps decide on their own when to search, and the toggle may not exist.
- **Fix**: Include "Search the web and cite your sources" in the prompt, and look for citations in the reply. If there are none, treat the answer as unverified.

## Practical tips

- **Use the AI for the draft and official sites for the facts.** Re-check hours, prices, transport, and entry rules before you pay.
- **Write down the date you checked.** Details change, so a note like "checked 2026-11-02" helps if you revisit the plan later.
- **Keep a buffer.** Leave free time and a budget cushion in the plan.
- **Save the booking confirmations yourself.** Keep them in your own email or files, not in the chat.
- **Be careful with maps and live location.** Sharing where you are in real time is optional. Share only what you are comfortable with.
- **Ask a person if the trip is high-stakes.** For health needs, a long journey with children, or a complex visa, a travel agent or the official office can confirm what the AI cannot.
