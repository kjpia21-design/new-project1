<Summary>
- The efficient order for a long video is to **summarize it first to see where the key parts are**, then cut just those sections into Shorts.
- Google's official help says the Gemini app can find YouTube videos and answer questions about their content. You can upload your own video files and ask about them, but for accounts without Google AI Pro or Ultra, the total video length is limited to 5 minutes (as of 2026-10-15).
- Vrew is a Korean service that makes automatic captions with speech recognition and lets you cut video the way you'd edit text. We couldn't confirm its pricing or free allowance on the official pages.
- **Be careful about re-editing other people's videos.** Using them without permission is a copyright problem, and under YouTube's monetization policy it can also count as "reused content."
- Summaries can be wrong. Check numbers, names, and quotes against the video yourself.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Use AI to pull the key points and a timestamped outline from a long video (a lecture, meeting, interview, or your own YouTube video).
- Make a Shorts (short vertical video) outline from the sections you chose in the summary.
- Finish one Short in Vrew with automatic captions, silence removal, and cut editing.
- Check copyright, AI disclosure, and reused content issues before you upload.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Video summary** | AI reads a video's speech and visuals and organizes the key points as text. |
| **Shorts** | YouTube's short vertical videos. YouTube's help says you can make them up to 3 minutes long with the Shorts creation tools. |
| **Automatic captions** | A feature that converts speech to text through speech recognition and uses it as captions. |
| **Cut editing** | Editing that trims out unneeded parts and joins the parts you need. |
| **Reused content** | YouTube's term (from its monetization policy) for videos that take other people's content without meaningful commentary or modification. |

<Steps>

1. **Check the rights to the video first**

   First decide whether the video you want to edit is **a video you made** or **a video you have permission to use**. For any other video (someone else's YouTube video, broadcasts, movies, news footage, etc.), only summarize it for your own understanding, and don't cut it up and post it to your channel. For copyright basics, see [Can You Use AI Output?](/basics/09-ai-copyright-ethics/).

2. **Summarize the video with the Gemini app**

   There are two ways.

   - **A video on YouTube**: On a computer, go to gemini.google.com, sign in, and with Keep Activity turned on, ask it to find the video and then ask for a summary. The official help's example is also two steps: "Find the video. Summarize the first video."
   - **Your own video file**: Upload it in the chat with Add files. Per the official help, one video can be up to 2 GB with a total length of up to 5 minutes. It says that with Google AI Pro or Ultra you can extend the total length to 1 hour (as of 2026-10-15). It also warns that if a file is too long or large, it may miss details.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Asking the Gemini app to find a video and summarize it" officialUrl="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16622858?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop" />

   <PromptBox>
   Summarize the video I just uploaded. Give me (1) a one-paragraph summary, (2) 5 key topics, (3) a timestamped outline (start time and a one-line description), and (4) a list of the places in the video where numbers or proper nouns come up. Don't guess at anything that isn't in the video; if you're not sure, mark it "not sure."
   </PromptBox>

   <Aside type="note" title="How do I fit a long video under the 5-minute limit?">
   Accounts without Google AI Pro or Ultra have a 5-minute total video length limit. You can split a long video into pieces of 5 minutes or less, or convert the video's audio to text (see Tutorial 14) and summarize the text. For summarizing text, see [Tutorial 02](/chat/02-summarize-documents/).
   </Aside>

3. **Check the summary**

   AI summaries can be wrong or leave things out. Open the video at the outline's timestamps and, at the very least, verify the **numbers, people's names, and quotes**. For how to check, see [Can You Trust AI Answers?](/basics/04-fact-checking/).

4. **Make a Shorts outline**

   Pick a section from the summary where you can get away with "teaching just one thing." A Short works best with a single message.

   <PromptBox>
   I want to turn the [12:30-14:10] section of the outline above into a vertical Short of 60 seconds or less. Put together (1) a one-sentence hook for the first 3 seconds, (2) which lines to keep and which to cut, (3) up to 5 key sentences to use as captions, and (4) a one-line closing. Don't make up any new lines that aren't in the video.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Make captions and cut in Vrew**

   According to Vrew's official site, it automatically makes captions by recognizing the video's speech, and you can cut the video as if you were editing text. It also introduces a feature that finds and removes silent sections in one go, a Shorts template, and translated captions. The sequence below is the work order this tutorial recommends; check exact menu names inside Vrew.

   <FlowSteps title="Flow for making Shorts with Vrew" officialUrl="https://vrew.ai/ko/" steps={[
     { where: 'Import the video', action: 'Bring in the video and select the speech language (Korean in this example)', result: 'A caption draft appears through speech recognition' },
     { where: 'Edit the caption text', action: 'Fix wrong characters and delete sentences you don\'t need', result: 'When you delete text, the matching video section shrinks too' },
     { where: 'Clean up silence and filler', action: 'Use the silence removal feature to clear empty sections' },
     { where: 'Shape it for Shorts', action: 'Check the aspect ratio and caption position suited for Shorts' },
     { where: 'Export', action: 'Save as a video file', result: 'Go on to the pre-upload checks' },
   ]} />

   <Aside type="caution" title="Always read the automatic captions">
   Speech recognition often gets proper nouns, technical terms, and numbers wrong. If wrong captions go up as they are, they become wrong information. Play the video and read the captions from start to finish.
   </Aside>

6. **Check before uploading**

   - **AI disclosure**: YouTube's help says realistic-looking AI-generated or altered content must be disclosed when you upload. For example, it applies if you make a real person appear to say something they didn't, alter footage of a real event, or create a realistic scene that didn't happen. On the other hand, it says minor edits such as generating captions, color correction, quality enhancement, or help with scripts and titles don't need disclosure. The setting is called 'AI use' in YouTube Studio.
   - **Copyright claims**: If the video contains someone else's music or footage, a claim may be applied automatically. Per the help, a Short of 1-3 minutes that gets a claim is blocked regardless of policy.
   - **Reused content**: YouTube says that if you upload other people's videos without meaningful commentary or modification, it may limit monetization for your whole channel. That's true even if you have permission.
   - **Likeness rights and personal information**: Check that you have consent from the people who appear in the video and that no personal information is visible on screen. For detailed criteria, see [Using AI While Protecting Personal and Company Information](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### Gemini can't find the video or can't summarize it

- **Cause**: Keep Activity may be off, you may have asked in a Live conversation, or the feature may be restricted on your (work or school) account.
- **Fix**: Turn on Keep Activity in a regular conversation and ask again. If that doesn't work, upload the video file directly, or convert the video's speech to text and summarize the text.

### The file is too long to upload

- **Cause**: You hit the limit on total video length (5 minutes without Pro or Ultra) or file size (2 GB for video).
- **Fix**: Split it into sections and upload them. To raise the length limit, check the plan requirements on the official page.

### The summary has things that weren't in the video

- **Cause**: AI can make up plausible-sounding content. The official help also warns that the longer the video, the easier it is to miss details.
- **Fix**: Add "don't include anything that isn't there" to your prompt, have it write timestamps alongside, and check them yourself.

### The automatic captions have lots of wrong characters

- **Cause**: Background noise, technical terms, fast speech, and dialects are easy for speech recognition to get wrong.
- **Fix**: Re-record in a quiet environment or fix the captions yourself. Also check that the speech language setting is correct.

### I uploaded and got a copyright claim

- **Cause**: The video or background music matched someone else's content and was caught by automatic detection.
- **Fix**: Check the claim details under the Content menu in YouTube Studio, then remove that part, or if you have legitimate rights, look into the dispute process.

## Practical tips

- **Start with your own videos.** Your own content, such as lectures, presentations, and interviews, has the least rights worry.
- **One message per Short.** Pick just the single most useful section from the summary.
- **Make captions easy to read.** One or two lines at a time, with only the key words shown large.
- **Don't delete the original.** If something goes wrong, you can check from the beginning.
- **Use other people's videos "to understand."** Summarizing to study is different from cutting and posting. To post, get permission and substantially add your own commentary or opinion.
- **Set your AI disclosure standard in advance.** Compare against the examples in YouTube's help of what requires disclosure, and when in doubt, choose to disclose.
