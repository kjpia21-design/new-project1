## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Convert a recording file to text.
- Quickly fix errors in the converted text.
- Use AI to make meeting notes, a list of decisions, and a to-do table by owner.
- Follow the principles for recording consent and for handling personal and confidential information.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Speech recognition (STT)** | Technology that turns spoken words into text. |
| **Speaker identification** | A feature that separates who said what. Availability and supported languages differ by tool. For example, speaker labels in Pixel Recorder are reportedly supported only for US English. |
| **Transcription time (quota)** | The amount of transcription the service allows per month. ClovaNote's help says it renews monthly and doesn't carry over. |
| **Summary** | Condensing the converted text into short meeting notes. Conversational AI like Claude is good at this stage. |

## Step by step

The example is "a recording of a 30-minute weekly team meeting."

<Steps>

1. **Get consent before recording**

   At the start of the meeting, say "we're recording this to write the meeting notes," and check that no one objects. If outside people are attending, it's safer to get consent in advance in writing or by email. Legal requirements for recording differ by country and situation, and the tool docs used for this guide can't confirm them. We recommend checking your company's policies and with your legal team.

2. **Record and check the file**

   Use your smartphone's recorder or the tool's recording feature. Check the file format (m4a, mp3, wav, etc.) and length. If the length exceeds the tool's limit, split it up before uploading. Keep the microphone close to whoever's speaking, and recognition improves if people talk over each other less.

3. **Convert it to text**

   In ClovaNote, you can record or upload an existing recording file when creating a new note. Uploading and converting require a network connection. According to NAVER WORKS Help, in the mobile app you tap "Upload file" in ClovaNote, and on PC web you click the new note button at the top left to upload a file; you choose the recognition language (Korean, Korean + English, and so on), then select the audio type and number of participants. Once uploaded, it converts automatically and you get a notification. The recognition language of an already uploaded note can't be changed, so check the language before uploading. The help also says that files from the iPhone's default recorder must first be moved to the Files app. Follow your screen for how the buttons look.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Uploading a recording file and choosing the language" officialUrl="https://help.worksmobile.com/en/use-guides/clovanote/create/note/" />

   If you run out of transcription time, the recording may be saved but not converted. The help says the quota is deducted when conversion starts.

4. **Fix the converted text**

   - Skim for and fix people's names, product names, and English abbreviations first.
   - Always replay the original recording to verify numbers (amounts, dates).
   - Fix any wrong speaker identification.

5. **Make the meeting notes with AI**

   Paste the corrected text into whichever of Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini you use (if it's company confidential, check step 6 first). Specifying a format makes the results more stable. The prompting principles are the same as in [C4. How to write prompts](/claude/c04-prompting/), and for summarizing long text, see also [Summarizing Meeting Notes and Long Documents, and Pulling Out Action Items](/chat/02-summarize-documents/).

   <PromptBox>
   Below is the text I got by converting a recording of our weekly team meeting. There may be speech recognition errors. Organize it in this format.
   1. One-line summary
   2. Decisions (numbered list)
   3. To-do table (owner / due date / task)
   4. Open items
   5. Things to double-check (places where numbers or names look suspicious)
   If an owner or due date isn't in the text, don't make one up; write "TBD."

   (Paste the transcribed text here)
   </PromptBox>

   If you want to use this format every time in Claude, you can save it as a skill in [C8. Skills](/claude/c08-skills/).

6. **Check for sensitive information, then share**

   - Are there names, contact details, national ID numbers, or customer information? If needed, mask them and share only the summary.
   - If it's company confidential, process it only in tools your internal security policy allows.
   - Decide how long to keep the original recording and the converted text. ClovaNote says administrators can restrict downloads.

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### The upload won't go through

- **Cause**: You may have exceeded a format, size, or length limit.
- **Fix**: Convert the format to m4a or mp3, and if the length exceeds your plan's limit (180 minutes for Lite/Team, 360 for Business and above), split it up and upload in parts (files over the limit can't be uploaded). Supported formats can differ between mobile and PC.

### The recognition is a mess

- **Cause**: Noise, people talking over each other, a distant microphone, or a mismatched language setting.
- **Fix**: Check the language setting (Korean / Korean + English) and record up close. Tell the AI your list of technical terms at the organizing stage.

### It didn't convert and only the recording was saved

- **Cause**: You may have used up your monthly transcription time.
- **Fix**: Wait for the next month's renewal or check your plan. It's described as not carrying over.

### The AI makes up things that aren't there

- **Cause**: In a summarization request, the model may fill in blanks.
- **Fix**: Add "mark missing information as TBD" to your prompt, and check important numbers and decisions against the original.

### Audio won't upload to the Gemini app

- **Cause**: Google's help gives the total audio length for one upload as 10 minutes (3 hours for Google AI Pro and Ultra). You can upload up to 10 files at a time, with non-video files at 100 MB or less (you can't put video or audio inside a zip).
- **Fix**: Split the audio or check your plan, and if that doesn't work, do the transcription with another tool first.

## Practical tips

- **State the agenda item at the start of the meeting**, and it'll be easier to organize afterward.
- **Have people say their names when they speak**, and speaker identification gets easier.
- **Check AI summaries against the original before distributing them.** Especially numbers, due dates, and owners.
- **For meetings you can't upload to outside tools**, don't upload them; use an internally approved tool.
- **For recurring meetings, fix your prompt format** so you get the same layout every time.
