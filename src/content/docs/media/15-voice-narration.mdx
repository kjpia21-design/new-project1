<Summary>
- AI voice narration works like this: **write a script → have a voice model read it → fix the pronunciation**.
- This tutorial centers on ElevenLabs and introduces Canva's text-to-speech as a simple alternative. Both services reportedly list Korean among their supported languages.
- **Never clone someone else's voice without their consent.** The rule is to clone **your own voice, with your own consent**.
- ElevenLabs' terms of service say free users can use it for non-commercial purposes only, while paid subscribers can also use it commercially (as of 2026-10-10; prices are in the table below).
- Korean pronunciation and intonation quality isn't guaranteed by the official docs. **Always listen to it yourself.**
</Summary>


## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Write a narration script for a promo video and turn it into an audio file.
- Know the tricks for fixing the pronunciation of numbers, English words, and proper nouns.
- Understand the consent rules and prohibitions for voice cloning.
- Check the terms items to confirm before commercial use.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **TTS (text to speech)** | Technology that turns text into a voice that reads it aloud. |
| **Premade voice** | A default voice the service provides. Unlike cloning, it raises fewer consent issues. |
| **Voice cloning** | A feature that creates a similar voice from a sample of a specific person's voice. **That person's consent is required.** |
| **Instant Voice Cloning (IVC)** | A cloning method that quickly builds a voice from a short sample. The official docs recommend at least 1 minute, with about 1-2 minutes of clean audio, and say recording more than 3 minutes brings little improvement. |
| **Professional Voice Cloning (PVC)** | A high-fidelity clone that requires verifying the voice is yours. Only your own voice is allowed. |

<Aside type="caution" title="The principle of voice cloning: the speaker's own consent">
- **Don't clone the voice of an employee, family member, voice actor, or celebrity without that person's consent.** ElevenLabs' Prohibited Use Policy bans "cloning, synthesizing, or impersonating the voice, identity, or likeness of a living person without that person's express, informed consent (or other valid authorization)," and for the deceased, it also bans uses that could cause harm or infringe posthumous rights. Impersonating organizations, deceptive advertising, and fraud are also prohibited.
- When you create an IVC, there's a step where you name the voice and **confirm you have the right and consent to clone it** before saving (per the official docs). Even if you're cloning your own voice, you need to confirm.
- For PVC, **you can't clone another person's voice even with their consent**; only your own voice is allowed, and the official docs say every PVC goes through verification that the voice is yours. If someone else wants to share their voice, they create and verify a PVC in their own account and send it via a private sharing link.
- Impersonating political candidates or elected officials without approval, and political campaigns (without prior written consent), are also prohibited. The description of safeguards that block cloning of high-risk voices such as celebrities (No-Go Voices) is something we couldn't confirm because we couldn't open the original help page.
- The policy says violations or attempts to circumvent it can result in content removal, usage restrictions, suspension, or termination, and that information may be shared with law enforcement or regulators where legally required.
- If you believe your voice was used without permission, the policy says you can use ElevenLabs' "Unauthorized Use of Voice" report.
- OpenAI's API docs follow the same principle. To create a custom voice from a recording, the voice's owner must supply a recording of themselves reading a specified consent statement (including a Korean sentence; as of 2026-10-26, the docs also describe a prompt-to-voice method that creates a voice from a text description without a recording), and the speech synthesis guide says that under the usage policies you must clearly tell end users the voice is AI-generated (developers.openai.com, checked 2026-10-10). We couldn't open the original usage policy (openai.com).
</Aside>

## Step by step

The example is narration for the 15-second video from [Tutorial 13](/media/13-promo-video/).

<Steps>

1. **Write the script**

   For a 15-second video, about 50-60 characters in Korean is about right (it varies with speaking speed). Use easy sentences, one breath per sentence. You can also have an AI like Claude make a first draft.

   <PromptBox>
   Write a narration script for a 15-second video announcing our new café menu item, the "Yuja Cream Latte." Three sentences, about 35 words total. Use a warm, calm tone. Spell out numbers and English words the way they're pronounced (for example, $1.00 → one dollar).
   </PromptBox>

2. **Choose a voice**

   In ElevenLabs, pick a premade voice or a library voice and preview it with your Korean script. The official docs say that for voices in the Voice Library, the person sharing them can set conditions, and some can be used only by paid subscribers. We couldn't confirm the commercial-use terms for library voices from the original this time, so check each voice's detail screen and the terms. Follow the on-screen guidance for menu names.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Choosing a voice and previewing it with a Korean script" officialUrl="https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech" />

3. **Generate it and listen**

   Paste the text and generate. There's a limit on how many characters you can enter at once (per the official model docs, Multilingual v2 and Eleven v4 allow 10,000 characters per request, about 10 minutes; Eleven v3 allows 5,000; Flash v2.5 allows 40,000). **Listen to the generated file all the way through.**

4. **Fix the pronunciation**

   - Spell numbers out in words. (For example, 1,000 won → "one thousand won," written out in Korean as 천 원)
   - For English product names, write them as they sound in Korean or try altering the spelling.
   - For awkward spots, split the sentence or add a comma and generate again.
   - Credits are used every time you generate, so fix and regenerate one sentence at a time.

5. **Check commercial use and labeling**

   - Does your account's plan allow **commercial use**? (The free plan is non-commercial only)
   - Did you follow the free plan's non-commercial conditions and the usage terms shown on screen?
   - If you used a cloned voice, do you have a record of the owner's consent?
   - Have you decided whether to disclose in ads or announcements that the voice is AI? ElevenLabs' policy says to make clear that someone is interacting with AI when the law requires it or when not disclosing would mislead them, and prohibits removing or hiding provenance signals such as ElevenLabs watermarks and metadata to evade detection.

6. **Add it to your video**

   Download the audio file, lay it over the video in an editing tool such as Canva from [Tutorial 12](/media/12-canva-sns/), and match the length. For a simple video, you can also use Canva's text to speech directly.

</Steps>

## Quick reference

### Cloning your own voice (the consent principle)

| Step | Details |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Record a clean sample in **your own** voice (a quiet room, a steady distance) |
| 2 | Upload the sample on the cloning screen (click the + icon next to Voices on the left of the dashboard and choose Instant Voice Clone; IVC needs at least 1 minute, about 1-2 minutes recommended) |
| 3 | Name it, **confirm you have the right and consent to clone it**, and save |
| 4 | Use it only for the purposes you've confirmed. When sharing with others, use only the methods the service allows |
| Prohibited | Other people's voices, voices of public figures or politicians, deceptive uses |

### Commercial-use checklist

| Item | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Plan | Free is non-commercial only; commercial use requires a paid subscription (terms). Check plan details on the official pricing page |
| Voice source | Record whether it's a premade, library, or cloned voice |
| Consent | For cloning, the voice owner's written consent, specifying the scope and duration of use |
| Terms | Terms may differ by region, such as non-EEA/EEA. Check the terms that apply to you |
| Labeling | Decide whether to label the AI voice according to company policy and relevant laws |

## Common sticking points

### Numbers or English words are read strangely

- **Cause**: Korean models don't always read mixed numbers and English naturally.
- **Fix**: Spell them out in words or split the sentence and generate again.

### Credits run out quickly

- **Cause**: Credits are used per character (pricing page FAQ: for Multilingual v2, 1 character = 1 credit).
- **Fix**: Finalize the script before generating, and regenerate only the sentence you're fixing.

### My voice clone won't work

- **Cause**: Sample quality, a missing consent confirmation, or plan requirements.
- **Fix**: Re-record in a quiet environment and complete the consent confirmation step. Check on the pricing page which plans allow cloning.

### I was asked to clone an employee's voice

- **Cause**: It's a request made for convenience, but without consent it violates the policy.
- **Fix**: Explain the purpose and scope to the person and get their written consent. PVC can't be used to make someone else's voice; the only option is for the person to create and verify it themselves and then share it. If consent is hard to get, use a premade voice.

## Practical tips

- **A premade voice is simpler and safer than cloning.**
- **Even when you clone your own voice, write down the scope of use.**
- **Read your script aloud and polish it.** Sentences that are hard to read also sound awkward from an AI.
- **Have legal and PR review official company videos.**
- **Keep the generated audio files and usage records (voice name, date, plan).**
