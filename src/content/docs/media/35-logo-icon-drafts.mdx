<Summary>
- AI is good at producing **several rough directions quickly**. Treat the output as a **draft to react to**, not a finished brand mark.
- Ask for **one idea per request**: the name, the feeling, a shape, two or three colors, and what to leave out (text, real brands, existing characters).
- Refine **one change at a time** and say what must stay the same. The official image guides recommend this for edits.
- Before real use, check three things: **does it resemble an existing mark, can you actually use and edit the file, and has a professional looked at it** (as of 2026-11-02).
- This is general information, not legal advice. The official pages we opened do not say that AI can produce print-ready or vector logo files, so we do not claim it.
</Summary>

<Aside type="caution" title="A logo is more than a picture">
A logo identifies a business, so it is easier to get wrong than an ordinary illustration. The US trademark office explains that a trademark identifies the source of goods or services, and that creative, unique marks are easier to protect than ones that merely describe the goods. For anything you will put on a storefront, packaging or ads, plan a **professional review** (a designer, and for trademarks an attorney or the trademark office in your country) before you commit. This tutorial helps you reach a good brief and a first draft, not a final brand.
</Aside>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Write a short logo or icon brief that an AI tool can act on.
- Generate 3 or 4 different directions with Claude Design or Gemini and compare them.
- Refine one chosen direction without losing what you liked.
- Run a simple pre-use check and know when to hand the draft to a professional.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Logo** | The mark that identifies a business or project. Often a symbol, a wordmark (the name in a styled font), or both. |
| **Icon** | A small simple symbol used in an app, a button or a social media profile. It must stay readable when tiny. |
| **Draft vs. final** | A draft is for choosing a direction. A final file has been checked for resemblance, cleaned up and handed over in the formats you need. |
| **Trademark** | The USPTO describes it as any word, phrase, symbol, design, or combination that identifies the source of your goods or services. |
| **Reference image** | An image you upload to guide the result. Use only images you have the rights to. |

## Step by step

The example is "a logo and an app icon for a neighborhood book club called Page Turners."

<Steps>

1. **Write a brief before you open any tool**

   Both the Claude Design help and the ChatGPT image guide stress being specific: Claude Design's help names goal, layout, content and audience, and the ChatGPT guide says to explain the purpose or audience, the main subject, the setting and style, and constraints including what must not appear. Adapt that to a logo.

   | Part | Example |
   | --- | --- |
   | Name and purpose | "Page Turners," a neighborhood book club, for a social media profile and flyers |
   | Audience and feeling | Adults, friendly, warm, a little playful |
   | Shape ideas | An open book, a page corner folding, a simple speech bubble |
   | Colors | Two or three: deep green and cream |
   | Avoid | Real brands, existing characters, photo-realism, tiny details |

2. **Generate several directions, not one**

   The Claude Design help suggests asking for 2 or 3 options when you are unsure of the direction, because comparing is faster than guessing. The same idea works in Gemini and ChatGPT: ask for separate, clearly different concepts and pick one.

   <PromptBox>
   I need logo ideas for a neighborhood book club called "Page Turners." The audience is adults, and the feeling should be friendly and warm. Show 3 different directions: (1) a simple open-book symbol, (2) a wordmark only, with a folded page corner, (3) a round badge. Use deep green and cream, flat shapes, and thick simple lines so it stays readable when small. Do not copy any existing logo, brand or character, and do not use photos.
   </PromptBox>

   In the Gemini app, go to gemini.google.com, click Open Sidebar at the top left, then Images, and enter your prompt. Gemini's help also suggests starting with words like draw, generate or create, and describing the style, such as cartoon illustration, plus the subject and setting.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Starting an image request from the Images page in the Gemini app" officialUrl="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14286560" />

   In Claude Design, you can also ask in any chat for a "brand sheet" or one-pager that lays several concepts side by side so you can compare them. That is this site's suggestion; the help page describes designs and one-pagers in general terms.

3. **Choose one direction and refine one thing at a time**

   The ChatGPT guide says to make small, targeted revisions, adjusting one element at a time so the rest does not drift, and to say exactly what should change and what must stay the same. Gemini's help describes "local edits" as a Nano Banana 2 feature for quick, specific changes to parts of an image.

   <PromptBox>
   Edit the attached logo. Change only the color of the book from cream to a lighter mint green. Keep the shape, line thickness, layout, and background exactly the same. Do not add text or other elements.
   </PromptBox>

   <FlowSteps title="Refine a Gemini image" officialUrl="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14286560" steps={[
     { where: 'gemini.google.com, top left', action: 'Click Open Sidebar, then Library', result: 'Your generated images' },
     { where: 'The image you chose', action: 'Click it, then click Chat under the image', result: 'An input box to continue on that image' },
     { where: 'Text box', action: 'Write one change and what to keep, then click Submit', result: 'The edited image' },
   ]} />

   If you are using Claude Design, use chat for big changes (like a different shape), inline comments for one spot, and direct editing for quick visual tweaks. The help also notes that inline comments occasionally do not appear, in which case you paste the same note into chat.

4. **Be careful with text inside the image**

   The ChatGPT guide says to keep in-image text short, put the exact wording in quotation marks, and describe font style, size, color and placement. Gemini's help says Nano Banana 2 has better text rendering for all supported languages, but the ChatGPT guide adds that for production-critical typography you should review every word and finish the asset in a design tool. For a wordmark, proofread the spelling letter by letter. If the name looks wrong, generate the symbol without text and add the name yourself in a design tool such as Canva (see [the Canva tutorial](/media/12-canva-sns/)).

   <PromptBox>
   Add only the words "Page Turners" in a rounded sans-serif font, deep green, on one line below the symbol. Do not add any other text, and do not change the symbol.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Test the draft the way a logo is really used**

   Make the draft small (about the size of a profile picture), then look at it in black and white and on both light and dark backgrounds. If you cannot recognize it, ask for a simpler version.

   <PromptBox>
   Simplify the attached symbol so it is still readable at 48 pixels wide. Remove fine details and keep no more than two colors. Keep the open-book idea and the overall shape. Do not add text.
   </PromptBox>

   For an icon, ask for one clear shape on a plain background. Whether you can get a transparent background or a vector file is not stated on the official pages we read, so check the file you actually get rather than assuming.

6. **Check before real use**

   <Aside type="caution" title="Resemblance, rights and professional review">
   Work through these before the draft appears on a sign, product, website or ad.
   </Aside>

   - **Resemblance.** Search for similar marks in your field. The USPTO explains that trademark rights are tied to how a mark is used with specific goods or services, and that a mark identifies the source of those goods or services. In practice that means a mark close to someone else's in your field can cause trouble. Use your country's official trademark database; we could not open the Korean trademark search site (KIPRIS) from this environment, so we do not describe it here.
   - **Do not ask for other people's marks.** The ChatGPT guide says to request a generic or original design rather than imitating a specific brand, product, artist or artwork, and to use images in line with your organization's guidelines and OpenAI's usage policies.
   - **Terms.** Gemini's help says that by generating images you agreed to Google's Terms of Service and Prohibited Use Policy, not to violate others' copyright or privacy rights, and to use your discretion before you rely on, publish or use the content. Users with work or school accounts may be subject to different terms. We did not find a sentence on those pages that states who owns a generated logo or whether it can be registered as a trademark, so we make no claim. For copyright and ethics in general, see [Can You Use AI Output?](/basics/09-ai-copyright-ethics/).
   - **Your own contribution.** The Korean copyright guidance summarized in that tutorial says output with no creative contribution from a person is hard to register. If the logo matters to your business, keep your sketches, briefs and edit history, and ask a professional how that applies to you.
   - **Professional review.** Have a designer clean up the final shapes and provide the file formats you need, and consult a trademark attorney or your trademark office before you register or rely on the mark.

</Steps>

## Quick reference

### Prompt building blocks for logos and icons

| Need | Wording to try |
| --- | --- |
| Simple and flat | "Flat shapes, thick simple lines, no gradients, no shadows" |
| Readable when small | "Must stay readable at 48 pixels wide, at most two colors" |
| Different directions | "Show 3 different concepts: symbol only, wordmark only, badge" |
| Keep the rest | "Change only ○○. Keep the shape, colors, layout and background exactly the same" |
| Originality | "An original design. Do not imitate any existing brand, logo, character or artist" |
| Icon | "One clear central shape on a plain background, no text" |

### Which tool for which step

| Step | Option |
| --- | --- |
| Compare several directions side by side | Claude Design (ask for a one-pager) or an image request in Gemini or ChatGPT |
| Fix one detail of a chosen image | Gemini Library, then Chat, or ChatGPT area edit |
| Add the name in a proper font | A design tool such as Canva |
| Final cleanup and file formats | A professional designer |

## Common sticking points

### Every direction looks the same

- **Cause**: The brief is too general, or it names no concrete shapes.
- **Fix**: List 3 specific shape ideas and ask for one concept per idea.

### The letters in the name are wrong

- **Cause**: Text rendering is not always accurate.
- **Fix**: Put the exact text in quotation marks, keep it short, and proofread it. If it still fails, generate the symbol without text and add the name in a design tool.

### Edits keep changing the whole picture

- **Cause**: Several changes were requested at once, or nothing was said about what to keep.
- **Fix**: Change one element at a time and list what must stay the same.

### It looks like a famous brand

- **Cause**: The prompt used a brand, artist or character name, or the result happens to resemble one.
- **Fix**: Remove those names, ask for an original design, and search for similar marks before using it.

### I cannot get a transparent or vector file

- **Cause**: The official pages we read do not describe these outputs.
- **Fix**: Do not assume. Check the downloaded file, and if you need a clean vector logo, ask a designer to redraw it from your chosen draft.

## Practical tips

- **Save your brief.** Keep the brief and the prompt for each good draft, so you can repeat or explain how it was made.
- **Compare at small size first.** Many designs that look good large fall apart as a 48-pixel icon.
- **Keep personal information out.** Do not upload photos of people or private documents as references.
- **Be honest about how it was made.** The ChatGPT guide says credit to OpenAI is optional, though you can explain how an asset was made when that is useful. Check your organization's policy on labeling AI-made work.
- **Treat the draft as a design brief.** The most useful output is often a clear direction you can hand to a designer.
