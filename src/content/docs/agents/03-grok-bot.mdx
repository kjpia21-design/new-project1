- **Grok Bot** is an **always-on AI agent** that xAI (shown as SpaceXAI on the site) released in beta on 2026-08-11. You hand work to a named Bot like sending a text, and the Bot logs into your apps on **its own cloud computer** and keeps working even after you close the app.

## Concepts to know first

### What is it, and who makes it?

Grok Bot is an app made by xAI. The announcement describes it as an "AI teammate you can hand your work to," and says it started as an internal prototype before being released publicly. The site footer lists the company name as SpaceXAI LLC. In the app stores, the provider appears as X Corp.

One thing to know: **actual login, usage metering, and privacy settings go through your Cursor account.** You sign in to the app with "Sign In with Cursor," and your usage is counted on your Cursor account.

### How is it different from a chat AI and @grok?

| | Regular chat AI (Grok chatbot, etc.) | Grok Bot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it works | Inside the chat window | The Bot's dedicated **cloud computer** (browser, files, terminal) |
| Output | Answers, drafts | Work **actually finished** in your apps (per official description) |
| If I close the app | It stops | **Keeps running** |
| How it proceeds | One conversation at a time | Multiple Bots in parallel, Bots handing work to each other |
| Memory | Centered on the conversation | A named Bot keeps your preferences and a summary of past work |

Asking by adding `@grok` to a post on X is a separate service. To hand work to Grok Bot from X, tag `@bot` ([see below](#handing-off-tasks-to-the-bot-from-x)).

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Bot** | An AI teammate with a name, a job, and a way of working. You can create several. |
| **Agent Computer** | The cloud computer the Bots use. **All your Bots share one.** |
| **Connector (plugin)** | A connection you install from the Marketplace. When one exists, it's more reliable than clicking through a site. |
| **Computer use** | The Bot operating a site directly when there's no connector. |
| **Skill** | A reusable instruction that saves "this is how to do this task." |
| **Routine** | A recurring task that sets which Bot does what, and when. |
| **Auto Review** | A feature where a model checks tool calls and computer actions before they run. |
| **Takeover** | You handle logins, two-factor authentication, CAPTCHAs, and payment confirmation yourself on the computer. |

### Who can use it, and how much does it cost?

There's no separate "Grok Bot plan." Access comes from one of the following (per Cursor Help).

| What I have | Grok Bot |
| --- | --- |
| Premium, Super, Ultra of The X Subscription (individual and team seats) | Included. You sign in with the same account, and it draws from the **weekly usage shared** with Grok and Cursor. Super and Ultra are larger. Plus and Free aren't included (as of 2026-10-19) |
| (Legacy) Cursor Pro, Pro+, Ultra (individual paid) | Included. Weekly usage gets larger the higher you go |
| Cursor Teams (self-serve) | Included for all members |
| SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, SuperGrok Heavy | **Link** to your Cursor account to use |
| X Premium+ | **Link** to your Cursor account to use |
| Self-serve Grok Business seat | Sign in with "Sign in with Grok" |
| SuperGrok Lite | **Not included** |
| Cursor Enterprise, Grok Enterprise | Contact the sales rep (waitlist / sales inquiry) |

As of 2026-10-19, Cursor Help calls the Cursor plans "legacy" and says new customers start with The X Subscription. In the rules below, the points about Cursor and SuperGrok linking are for the legacy plans. On The X Subscription, once you use up your weekly usage, extra usage is deducted from prepaid credits.

Rules to know:

- Weekly usage resets every week. Cursor Help explains that **The X Subscription shares the same weekly usage as the Grok and Cursor apps** and has no separate Grok Bot meter (as of 2026-10-26). The August xAI announcement said "its own usage, separate from Grok and Cursor plans," but for what applies now, follow Cursor Help. On legacy Cursor plans, higher plans get more weekly usage.
- If you use up your weekly usage, **pay-as-you-go (on-demand)** usage may be billed through Cursor. Check your limit in advance.
- A Cursor plan and a SuperGrok link **don't add together.** If you have both, whichever gives more usage applies.
- Once you link SuperGrok or X Premium+, **you can't unlink it or move it to a different Cursor account.** Make sure you're signed in to the right Cursor account before linking.
- The free trial is **credit**-based, not based on number of days (a 7-day window also applies). A large task can use it all at once.
- Per Cursor's official docs (Models & Pricing), Pro is $20/month, Pro Plus is $60/month, Ultra is $200/month, and Teams is $40/user/month for Standard and $120/user/month for Premium (US dollars, as of 2026-10-13). They say only that Grok Bot weekly usage increases in the order Pro < Pro+ < Ultra, and don't officially disclose specific figures. We haven't listed SuperGrok prices because the x.ai pricing page blocks automated access and we couldn't check.

<Steps>

1. **Check your account and data settings**

   See whether your plan is included in the table above. Grok Bot needs cloud data storage, so it won't start if your Cursor account is in **Legacy Privacy Mode**. In that case, change it to a supported data setting in Cursor's privacy settings.

2. **Install the app**

   - **Desktop**: Download the file for your operating system from the official download page (x.ai/bot). Choose Apple silicon/Intel for macOS, x64/Arm64 for Windows, or deb, rpm, or AppImage for Linux.
   - **iPhone/iPad**: Install "Grok Bot" (provider X Corp.) from the App Store. iOS 18 or later is required.
   - **Android**: Install "Grok Bot" from Google Play. Per the official docs, Android 9 or later.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="The Grok Bot listing on the Korean App Store (provider X Corp., free with in-app purchases)" officialUrl="https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/grok-bot/id6794501026" officialLabel="View on the App Store" />

   <Aside type="note" title="The 2026-08-26 announcement and the current docs differ">
   The download button in the August announcement shows only macOS. The official docs updated 2026-10-07 say Windows, Linux, and Android are supported too. This guide follows the more recent docs, but if you don't see a file for your device on the install screen, recheck the official page.
   </Aside>

3. **Sign in and connect access**

   Sign in to the app with your Cursor account (or with your organization's login if it uses SSO). If you're using it through a SuperGrok subscription, link your Grok account when the app prompts you. As noted earlier, linking can't be undone, so first confirm it's **the right Cursor account**.

   On first launch, a short intro screen appears while setup runs in the background. According to the official getting-started docs (as of 2026-10-26), the app creates your first Bot named "Grok Bot" and opens a conversation with it. If the intro screen asks which tools you use, your answer is only used to decide recommendations and doesn't connect any tools. Preparing the computer can take a few minutes.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="The screen after first launch, with the conversation with the first Bot open" officialUrl="https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started" officialLabel="View in the official Grok Bot docs" />

4. **Create your first Bot**

   Use the first Bot, or pick a suggested teammate as the app guides you, or create your own. Just write a name, **one main job**, and how it works. Rather than piling several roles onto one Bot, splitting them by role builds context better (official recommendation).

   <PromptBox>
   Name: Moa
   Job: Weekly work roundup
   Description: Read the documents and notes I upload and pull out dates, decisions, and open questions separately. Don't change the original files, and note the source (page number or item) alongside each one. Always ask me first before doing anything that affects the outside world, like sending email or posting publicly.
   </PromptBox>

5. **Hand off a safe first task**

   A good request includes the deliverable, where to look, what not to do, the form to return it in, and where to stop. Start with summarizing a document, which needs no login or connector.

   <PromptBox>
   Please summarize the attached document in five lines. Collect dates, decisions, and open questions separately. Note the source page or section for each item, and don't touch the original file.
   </PromptBox>

   You can also hand off tasks by keyboard dictation (`Cmd/Ctrl+D`) or voice chat. There's no official confirmation of how well Korean speech recognition works, so test it yourself.

6. **Log in to the apps you need yourself**

   When the Bot reaches somewhere that needs a login, it asks you to take over the computer. Open the **Agent Computer** in the conversation, press the takeover button, enter passwords, passkeys, two-factor codes, and CAPTCHAs **yourself**, and then hand control back. Don't send passwords or one-time codes in the regular chat.

   The logged-in browser session stays on the shared computer, so other Bots can use it too. For supported services, you can also install a plugin from the sidebar Marketplace and authenticate in the browser. In chat, call a connector with `@` and a skill with `/`.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="The takeover screen where you handle the login yourself in the Agent Computer view" officialUrl="https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started" officialLabel="View in the official Grok Bot docs" />

   <FlowSteps title="Taking over yourself when a login is needed" officialUrl="https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started" steps={[
     { where: 'Conversation screen', action: 'Open the Agent Computer' },
     { where: 'Agent Computer', action: 'Press the take control button', result: 'You operate the computer directly' },
     { where: 'Login screen', action: 'Enter the password, passkey, two-factor code, or CAPTCHA yourself' },
     { where: 'Conversation screen', action: 'Hand control back and tell the Bot to continue' },
   ]} />

7. **Check the result and refine it**

   If the result falls short, ask for fixes. For preferences you want applied from now on, say clearly "from now on, use this format" so it remembers. For important decisions, don't rely on the Bot's memory; tell it to **recheck the current original** (official recommendation).

8. **Save it as a skill and a routine**

   Once a task is stable, save it as a skill, then automate it as a routine. The official recommendation is to do it once, save it, and only then automate, rather than automating from the start.

   <PromptBox>
   Please save the weekly roundup method you just used as a "Weekly work roundup" skill. Include the input materials, output format, source citation rules, and the rule that anything sent outside always needs my approval.
   </PromptBox>

   Then create the routine.

   <PromptBox>
   Run the "Weekly work roundup" skill at 9:07 AM on weekdays and post the result to this conversation. Don't send it outside, and if the source material is missing, don't use old material; tell me it failed.
   </PromptBox>

### Handing off tasks to the Bot from X

If you tag `@bot` in an X post or reply, your main Bot takes that post as a task. The task and result stay in the conversation in the Grok Bot app, and @bot only leaves a short acknowledgment reply on X.

- Your Grok account needs to be linked to your X account, and you need at least one Bot.
- It doesn't respond to posts with video or GIFs, or posts that also tag @grok.
- A task started by a tag **also uses Grok Bot usage.**

### Options on the approval card

| Choice | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow once** | Allow just this time |
| **Always allow** | Saves a rule that keeps allowing similar actions |
| **Deny** | Block it |

There are two kinds of rules. **Ask first** always stops, and **Allow automatically** lets it through only when the automatic check finds nothing else wrong. When they overlap, **Ask first wins.**

### Local computer execution setting

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Ask every time** | Default. Asks every time |
| **Always allow** | Always allow (not recommended) |
| **Never allow** | No command execution on my computer |

This setting is separate from using the cloud computer. Unless you have a reason to need your own files, we recommend **Never allow**.

## What to watch for with payments, permissions, and privacy

Grok Bot logs in to your apps and does real work. The more convenient it is, the higher the cost of mistakes. We cover general principles further in [Using Personal AI Agents Safely](/agents/05-agent-safety/).

<Aside type="caution" title="All Bots share one computer">
Files, browser logins, and command-line credentials are shared with all Bots. The official docs also say "don't use a Bot as a security boundary." Even if you decide "this Bot is for email only," assume other Bots can reach other services logged in on the same computer. Even if you delete a Bot, the files and login sessions on the shared computer remain.
</Aside>

### Payments and purchases

- The official announcement includes a case of building a website and **buying a domain and deploying it**. That means purchases are possible.
- On the other hand, in the docs we checked this time, **we couldn't find a Bot-specific payment method or a feature for setting a spending cap.** Don't assume one exists.
- Payment confirmation is designed so you handle it yourself at the **takeover** step, where the Bot hands over control of the computer. Don't send card numbers in chat.
- For purchases, financial transfers, deletions, permission changes, production environment changes, and agreeing to terms, state **"ask first"** explicitly in your request. This is the official recommendation.
- Approval only controls actions that are about to happen and **can't undo what's already done.** Read the target, scope, and amount on the confirmation screen, and don't approve if you don't understand it.
- For money-related work like customer refunds, spell out the policy scope in a sentence and have it only draft at first.

### Permissions

- Start with read-only and drafting. Let it send, post, buy, and delete only after approval.
- **Auto Review** is a model-based check, so it's not perfect. The docs also say it doesn't replace least privilege and explicit approval. Avoid broad rules like "allow everything in the browser" and keep them narrow (for example, "always ask first before sending outside email").
- Approvals started by routines or other Bots expire after about 10 minutes and won't run. If you missed one, have the Bot try again.
- Log out of services you don't use, and revoke permissions for connected apps at the source service.
- If you can create an account with limited scope on the service side (a service account), use that. A Bot works with only the permissions of the person logged in.

### Privacy and data

- Grok Bot **requires cloud data storage**. Opting out of training follows your Cursor account and privacy settings.
- The Bot's computer currently runs in the **US**. That means data from users in Korea is processed on overseas servers. Check your organization's policy before handling company materials.
- A Bot **share link** exposes only the Bot's settings and doesn't share your computer or logins, but anything secret or any internal address you put in the settings will be exposed. Don't put customer information in either.
- Enterprise-only controls (network policies, audit logs, etc.) aren't in self-serve plans.
- Per the official security docs (Security), Anysphere holds ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001 certifications, and Grok Bot is currently within scope (as of 2026-10-12). The Cursor security page also lists company-wide SOC 2 Type II attestation and AIUC-1 certification, but doesn't say separately whether Grok Bot falls within that scope. If you're adopting it for work, review the reports (trust.cursor.com) and contract terms yourself.

### Usage and cost

- Running lots of routines or parallel Bots drains weekly usage fast, and if pay-as-you-go is on, extra charges accrue.
- The free trial is credit-based, so one big task can use it up. Start small and scale up while watching the usage screen.
- If you're away for a long time, the app may ask whether to keep running routines, and if there's no answer, routines may stop.

## Common sticking points

### It won't start and I get a Legacy Privacy Mode error

- **Cause**: Grok Bot needs cloud data storage, and Legacy Privacy Mode doesn't allow it.
- **Fix**: Change your Cursor account's data setting to a supported value. If it's an organization account, contact your admin.

### I signed in but it says I don't have access

- **Cause**: Your plan isn't eligible (such as SuperGrok Lite), you haven't linked SuperGrok, or you signed in with a different Cursor account.
- **Fix**: Check the plan table above. If you need to link SuperGrok, link your Grok account on the app's access screen and refresh the status.

### The computer is stuck on "preparing" for a long time

- **Cause**: First-time setup and image updates can take a few minutes.
- **Fix**: Keep the app open and wait. If it seems stuck, go from least destructive: retry, restart the app, check for updates, then the computer update in settings. **Reset** is the last resort. You can lose work since the last save.

### The Bot seems stuck

- **Cause**: It may be waiting for a login, CAPTCHA, approval, or secret input.
- **Fix**: Look at the questions and approval cards in the conversation, and check the screen in the Agent Computer. If it's going the wrong way, correct it briefly, and to interrupt, send "stop now." Also check whether your usage has run out.

### A site keeps asking me to log in

- **Cause**: The site expires sessions early or blocks automation.
- **Fix**: Take over and log in yourself, confirm the logged-in screen appears, and then hand control back. Don't try to get around authentication. The Bot is designed to hand those steps to a person too.

### A routine didn't run

- **Cause**: It may be turned off, the schedule or time zone may be wrong, the owning Bot may have been deleted, the plugin authentication may have lapsed, or usage may have run out.
- **Fix**: Check the items above and the run history on the routines screen. **A Test run does real work.** Test only with safe inputs.

### @bot on X doesn't reply

- **Cause**: One of: regional restriction, a post with video/GIF, tagging @grok at the same time, failing internal review, Grok Bot not linked, or weekly usage exhausted.
- **Fix**: Check the country registered on your X account, and simplify the post and tag again.

## Practical tips

- **Split Bots by role.** Piling everything on one blurs the context. Just don't forget the computer is shared.
- **Do it once, save it as a skill, then make it a routine.** Don't automate from the start.
- **Write down what not to do.** Putting "draft only, don't send" in your first request cuts accidents significantly.
- **Put a no-data rule in routines.** If the source is missing, have it report failure rather than use old material.
- **Avoid the top of the hour for routine times.** Offset them, like 9:07.
- **Test Korean tasks in small steps.** Korean quality has no official confirmation, so first run a short check with Korean sites and Korean documents.
- **Audit regularly.** Clean up installed connectors, logged-in sites, and routines you've left on.
