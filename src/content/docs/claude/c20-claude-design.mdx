<Summary>
- **Claude Design** is a feature for making visual deliverables like designs, clickable prototypes, and one-pagers by talking with Claude. It's one of the artifact templates, so you can use it **in any conversation**, from the Artifacts tab, and in Claude Code (as of 2026-11-02).
- It's available on the Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise plans and is on by default. On Enterprise, it has been on by default since 2026-10-15.
- The earlier **standalone version (claude.ai/design) shuts down on 2026-12-14**. Projects and design systems made there need to be moved to Claude before that date.
- The first result is a starting point. You refine it in three ways: **chat (big changes), inline comments (specific parts), and direct canvas editing (simple visual tweaks)**.
- There's no separate usage; it uses **the same limits as the rest of your Claude usage**. If you hit the limit you can't use it until it resets, though you can keep going if you've turned on usage credits.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Make a screen mockup or one-pager in one or two exchanges and check it on the canvas.
- Choose the right editing method for the situation: chat, inline comments, or direct editing.
- Export the result to PDF, PPTX, HTML, and more, or share it with your team, and hand it off to Claude Code for the development stage.
- Know the standalone version's shutdown schedule and limits, and prepare to move safely.

## Concepts to know first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Claude Design** | A feature that pairs a conversation with a canvas to make designs. When you describe something in the conversation, a working design appears on the canvas beside it. |
| **Canvas and artboards** | A design is a canvas with several artboards (one screen each) on it. Inside an artboard are layers such as text, shapes, and images. |
| **Design system** | A collection of an organization's colors, fonts, and component rules. When you attach one, Claude builds with those rules and checks and fixes its own output against them. |
| **Inline comment** | A note you leave by clicking a specific spot on the canvas, like "change just this." |
| **/design, /design-sync** | Commands used in Claude Code. `/design` creates, edits, and syncs designs, and `/design-sync` brings in a design system. |

This feature runs on the same framework as [C6. Artifacts](/claude/c06-artifacts/). Sharing methods and general export are already covered in C6, so this article covers only what's needed for design. For making documents and slides as a follow-up, also see the built-in skills in [C8. Skills](/claude/c08-skills/) or [C21](/claude/c21-office-agents/).

<Aside type="caution" title="The standalone version (claude.ai/design) closes on 2026-12-14">
According to the help, the standalone version keeps working until the day it closes, and existing projects stay there until then. The feature for moving to Claude is rolling out in stages starting with paid plans. It's advised that you **move your design systems to Claude before it closes**. This article is based on Design inside Claude (the integrated version) (as of 2026-11-02).

<Steps>

1. **Pick where to start**

   The easiest way is to ask in any conversation, like "Make a one-pager from this proposal" or "Make a mockup of the onboarding flow we just talked about." You can also choose Output > Design in the message input box, or pick the Design template in the Artifacts tab. In Claude Code, you can use `/design` in both the terminal and the desktop app. You can also ask from the iOS and Android apps, and view the result full screen later in the Artifacts tab. However, you can change sharing settings only on the web or desktop.

   <ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Choosing Design from the Output menu in the message input box" officialUrl="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design" />

2. **(Optional) Attach a design system and reference materials**

   It's noted that new projects automatically inherit your organization's design system. To attach one yourself, bring it from a GitHub repository, a design file, an uploaded file, or a local codebase with Claude Code's `/design-sync`. You can also upload screenshots, competitor screens, wireframes, and existing slides or documents as references for "something like this." The help says results get better the more **complete a design system you bring, with styles, fonts, and components all included**, and that imported results follow the original's quality as is.

3. **Make your request by writing the goal, layout, content, and audience**

   The four elements of a good prompt, according to the official help, are **goal, layout, content, and audience**. You don't have to be a designer; the more specific, the better. If you're missing needed information, Claude asks you first.

   <PromptBox>
   Please make a sign-up page for a neighborhood library's book club. The audience is residents in their 40s to 60s, mostly opening it on mobile. At the top, put a one-line club intro and a sign-up button; below that, three schedule cards (date, book title, capacity); and at the bottom, a sign-up form (name, choice of contact method). Make the text large, use calm colors, and design for a mobile screen.
   </PromptBox>

   This prompt is an example. Don't put in personal information like real names or contact details; use fake values.

4. **Refine in three ways**

   The first result is a starting point. The help divides the editing tools like this.

   | Method | When | Example |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **Chat** | Big changes like overall feel, structure, or new sections, and requests that need explanation | "Make the colors darker and simpler," "Show me 2-3 alternative layouts" |
   | **Inline comments** | Changing just one specific part | Click and leave "more spacing" or "use a dropdown instead of radio buttons" |
   | **Direct canvas editing** | Quick visual tweaks like dragging to move, resizing, and aligning | Drag elements to align them |

   If a comment isn't applied, it's advised to paste the same content into chat. That's because there's a known issue where comments occasionally disappear. If you want to try a different direction, say "save the current one and try a completely different approach," and Claude tells you it saved the current work. The help states that there's no version history feature yet.

5. **Export and share**

   When viewing a project, choose a format from the Export button at the top right (as of 2026-11-02).

   | Purpose | Options |
   | --- | --- |
   | Save as a file | .zip download, PDF, PPTX, Google Slides, standalone HTML |
   | Send to other tools | Canva, Gamma, HubSpot, Miro, Netlify, Replit, v0, Vercel, Wix, Lovable, and others in the help list |
   | Hand off to development | Send to a local coding agent, send to Claude Code on the web |

   A design you make is visible only to you at first. In Share, you set who can open it and whether they can view, comment, or edit. Sharing rules for Team and Enterprise follow the same artifact rules as [C6](/claude/c06-artifacts/).

6. **Hand off to development (Claude Code)**

   If the design is at the stage of being built as software, you're advised to hand it off to Claude Code. Claude Code picks up the existing work instead of starting over from a screenshot. Using `/design` in Claude Code, you can bring a design into the codebase, export code as a working prototype, or have it build from start to finish. For Claude Code itself, see [C12](/claude/c12-claude-code-overview/).

</Steps>

## Common sticking points

### Inline comments aren't applied

- **Cause**: There's a known issue where comments sometimes disappear before Claude reads them. Even if you don't see them on the page, you can find them in the comments view.
- **Fix**: Paste the same content directly into chat.

### I get "chat upstream error"

- **Cause**: This is an error the help lists as a known limitation. The cause isn't explained.
- **Fix**: Try opening a new chat tab within the same project.

### It says I can't use Design

- **Cause**: When you hit your usage limit, you can't use Design until the limit resets. The Design-specific limit is gone.
- **Fix**: Wait until the limit resets, or keep going if you've turned on usage credits. For how limits are structured, see [C2](/claude/c02-plans-limits/).

### Connecting a large code repository is slow

- **Cause**: Connecting a very large repository on the web can cause delays or browser problems.
- **Fix**: It's advised to consider connecting with `/design-sync` in Claude Code.

### The wrong thing gets deleted from the properties panel's layer list

- **Cause**: In the layer list, Delete, Backspace, and cut apply to what's selected on the canvas, not to the row in the list.
- **Fix**: Before deleting, select the row by pressing Return on it.

### Things get weird when more than one person edits at once

- **Cause**: It's noted that simultaneous editing by multiple people is still basic and may not be stable.
- **Fix**: Have one person edit at a time, and let the others take part by viewing and commenting.

## Practical tips

- **Start simple and add more.** It's advised to build the core layout and content first, and ask for behavior, edge cases, and polish later.
- **Be specific with feedback.** "Reduce the spacing between input fields to 8px" works far better than "it looks off."
- **Ask for 2-3 alternatives.** When you're unsure of the direction, comparing is faster than guessing.
- **Mention responsive early.** Write at the start whether it will be used on mobile, tablet, or desktop.
- **Use Claude as a reviewer.** You can ask Claude to check accessibility, contrast, information hierarchy, and usability. A person should make the final call.
- **Keep brand materials and personal information separate.** Don't put real customer names, contact details, or internal materials in a mockup; build it with fake values. Check your organization's policy first on how far uploaded materials and code repositories are used.
- **Navigation for keyboard and screen reader users** is in a table in the official help. Placing new text, artboards, and shapes on the canvas needs a mouse or trackpad, and if you only use the keyboard, you're advised to ask Claude to add them.
