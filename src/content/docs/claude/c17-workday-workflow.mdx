<Summary>
- This isn't an article introducing a new feature. It's **a case study of chaining together, within a single day, the features you learned in C5-C10**.
- The flow is **morning (project + connectors) → mid-morning (lock in format with skills) → around lunch (Research) → afternoon (deliverables as artifacts) → end of day (memory and wrap-up)**.
- Each tool has a different role. Projects handle **materials and rules**, connectors handle **access to your data**, skills handle **how the work is done**, Research handles **investigation**, artifacts handle **deliverables**, and memory handles **continuity**.
- Don't use everything from the start. Starting with **one project** and adding one piece at a time where it's inconvenient lasts longer.
- The case is one day in the life of a fictional office worker. The screens may differ depending on your account, app version, and plan.
</Summary>

## What you'll be able to do after this tutorial

- Split your work into **projects**, and sketch which feature to pull out when during the day.
- Tell apart **what goes in project instructions, what goes in a skill, and what goes in each request** when you repeat the same task.
- Carry research → organizing → a shareable deliverable through **a single flow**.
- Know at which points in the day to check privacy and permission issues.

## Concepts to know first

I'll explain through the day of a fictional person, **Minji** (a planner on a small team). Each week Minji handles one client proposal and writes a weekly progress report.

| Feature | Its role during the day | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Projects** | Keep proposal materials and tone/format rules in one place, and separate conversations by task | [C5](/claude/c05-projects/) |
| **Connectors** | Let Claude read your own Gmail, Google Calendar, and Drive | [C7](/claude/c07-connectors/) |
| **Skills** | Lock in how work is done as a folder, like "reports in this format" | [C8](/claude/c08-skills/) |
| **Research** | Investigate multiple sources step by step and write a report with citations | [C10](/claude/c10-research/) |
| **Artifacts** | Build deliverables (documents, slides, small tools) beside the conversation and share them | [C6](/claude/c06-artifacts/) |
| **Memory** | Carry preferences and context from a conversation into the next one | [C9](/claude/c09-memory-settings/) |

## Step by step

<Steps>

1. **Morning: open the project and organize your day**

   If you split projects by task, materials and rules don't get mixed together ([C5](/claude/c05-projects/)). Minji put the proposal template and past proposals in the **knowledge** of her "Client proposals" project, and rules like "formal tone, conclusion first, tables of 3 columns or fewer" in its **instructions**. Materials that don't change belong in knowledge, and content that changes every time is more accurate when you tell it in the conversation.

   If you've connected the Gmail and Google Calendar connectors ([C7](/claude/c07-connectors/)), you can have today's schedule and the emails you need to read organized in one go. Connectors read only within your own permissions, and by default they ask for approval before write actions like sending email.

   <PromptBox>
   Please pull together today's events on my calendar and, from the emails I've received since yesterday, pick the ones that look like they need a reply. Show events as a table in time order, and emails with sender, gist, and suggested reply timing. Don't send any emails; just mark the ones that need a draft.
   </PromptBox>

   <Aside type="caution" title="Turn on only the connectors you need">
   You can turn connectors on and off per conversation. Keeping connectors you don't need today turned off lowers the risk of reading the wrong material. For emails that contain customer names or contact details, double-check before pasting the summary anywhere outside.
   </Aside>

2. **Mid-morning: lock in the format of recurring documents**

   A document with the same structure every week, like a weekly progress report, is a good candidate for a **skill** ([C8](/claude/c08-skills/)). A skill is a folder of instructions that Claude loads when needed. To use skills, code execution must be turned on, and you switch them on and off with a toggle under Customize > Skills.

   If building a skill feels like too much, starting by writing the format in your project instructions is plenty. This split makes it easy to decide where things go.

   | Content | Where it goes |
   | --- | --- |
   | Tone and rules to always follow in this project | Project **instructions** |
   | Materials to keep referencing in this project | Project **knowledge** |
   | Work procedures used across projects (such as report format) | **Skill** |
   | Requests that apply only today | Directly in the conversation |

   <PromptBox>
   Please write a draft of this week's progress report. Based on the notes below, split it into four parts: what I did last week / what I'll do this week / blockers / requests. Don't make up numbers or dates that aren't in the notes; mark them [needs confirmation].

   [Paste this week's notes here]
   </PromptBox>

3. **Around lunch: use Research when you need to investigate**

   For research where you need to gather and compare multiple sources, like industry trends for a client, **Research** fits better than one or two searches ([C10](/claude/c10-research/)). The official help says to use Research for comprehensive investigations that take five or more tool calls and 1-3 minutes. It uses the same limits as regular chat and can use them up faster. So it's better to write a specific question before lunch and run it once.

   <PromptBox>
   Please research the last year of trends in [industry/field]. Pull out the 3-5 major changes our client ([one line on the client's size and situation]) should address in the proposal, and attach a supporting source to each. Mark separately anything where the sources are weak or conflict.
   </PromptBox>

   <Aside type="caution" title="Open the citation links yourself">
   Even a report with citations can be wrong. Before using a number or claim in a proposal, click the citation link and confirm it in the original source.
   </Aside>

4. **Afternoon: make the deliverable as an artifact**

   Once the research and drafts are in, turn them into something you can show. An artifact is a document, slide deck, or small tool that opens beside the conversation ([C6](/claude/c06-artifacts/)). A new artifact starts private, and you set the scope from the Share menu. Sharing works differently depending on your plan, so check the table in C6 first.

   <PromptBox>
   Based on the research above and the proposal template (project materials), please make a client proposal summary document. Keep it to one page, in this order: title / three key proposals / supporting evidence / next steps. Cite sources for the evidence, and don't include any unconfirmed numbers.
   </PromptBox>

   <Aside type="note" title="Check before sharing">
   Before you share, read it through once to make sure it doesn't contain customer names, contact details, or internal unit prices. For how much someone with the link can see, check the permissions explanation in C6.
   </Aside>

5. **End of day: tidy up for tomorrow**

   If there's an instruction you kept correcting in today's conversation, it's a candidate to add to your project instructions. For example, if you said "cut tables down to 3 columns or fewer" three times, add a line to the instructions. Memory is saved automatically by topic during conversations, and under Settings > Your Claude (Memory on older screens) you can see what's been saved and edit or delete it ([C9](/claude/c09-memory-settings/)). Each project has its own memory space, so it doesn't get mixed with other work.

   <PromptBox>
   Please sort out any formatting or tone rules that I corrected or asked for repeatedly in today's conversation. Suggest them as short sentences that I can add to my project instructions, one line each.
   </PromptBox>

</Steps>

## Quick reference

### Choosing a feature by scene

| If the job is... | Feature to use first | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I paste the same material every time | Project knowledge | [C5](/claude/c05-projects/) |
| I need to review and organize my email, calendar, and documents | Connectors | [C7](/claude/c07-connectors/) |
| I write the same format of document every week | Project instructions or a skill | [C8](/claude/c08-skills/) |
| It's research comparing multiple sources | Research | [C10](/claude/c10-research/) |
| I need a document, slides, or a tool to show others | Artifacts | [C6](/claude/c06-artifacts/) |
| I need my preferences remembered in the next conversation | Memory | [C9](/claude/c09-memory-settings/) |
| The same job needs to run automatically at set times | Scheduled tasks and routines | [C18](/claude/c18-automate-recurring/) |

### Constraints to remember when combining

- Context that isn't in knowledge is **not shared between conversations.** A project doesn't automatically know what was said in another conversation ([C5](/claude/c05-projects/)).
- Connectors **inherit your permissions as they are.** In a team project, there are cases where connector data can't be shared with teammates ([C7](/claude/c07-connectors/)).
- Research, skills, and artifacts may not appear depending on your plan and organization settings. If a menu is missing, Team/Enterprise users should check with their Owner settings.
- Prices and limits change. Check your own usage under Settings > Usage.

## Common sticking points

### I used every feature, but the results got more scattered

- **Cause**: You may have turned on all connectors, put too many rules in the instructions, or mixed unrelated materials into the knowledge.
- **Fix**: Turn on only the connectors today's work needs, shorten the instructions, and split materials into projects by task.

### The project doesn't know what we talked about yesterday

- **Cause**: Between conversations, only materials in the knowledge base are shared. Memory is saved by topic, but it doesn't capture everything.
- **Fix**: Write decisions you'll keep needing directly into the project knowledge or instructions.

### I'm not sure whether to trust the numbers in a Research result

- **Cause**: Even with citations, the source may be weak or the interpretation may be off.
- **Fix**: Open the citation links yourself to check the original, and write in your prompt that unconfirmed figures should be left as "needs confirmation."

### The person I shared an artifact with can't open it

- **Cause**: Sharing methods and permissions differ by plan.
- **Fix**: Check the sharing methods table in [C6](/claude/c06-artifacts/) and the "I tried to share but the other person can't open it" item.

## Practical tips

- **Start with one project.** Make a project for just the one task you do most often, use it for a week, then add connectors and skills.
- **Grow your instructions through conversation.** Each time an off-target answer comes out, add one line.
- **Batch your research questions and run them once.** Research can use more of your limit.
- **Have a person confirm write actions.** For sending email or sharing, follow the order of draft → I check → run.
- **Think twice before putting sensitive material into prompts and knowledge.** Handle personal information and client information according to [Privacy and security](/basics/05-privacy-safety/).
- **Consider automation for things you do at the same time every day.** Just remember that automatic runs happen while no one is watching. C18 covers safe design.
